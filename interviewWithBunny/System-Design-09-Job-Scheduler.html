01Requirements

Functional Requirements

System ko kya-kya karna chahiye — ye 7 features hain jo ek distributed job scheduler me hone hi chahiye. Interview me pehle ye list bol do, phir design shuru karo.

7 Features

  • Feature 1 — Job schedule karna: Job ko specified time par schedule karne ki ability — immediate (abhi chalao), future (kisi date-time par), ya cron expression se (jaise roz raat 12 baje). Teenon tareeke support karne hain.
  • Feature 2 — Real-time monitoring: Job ki status real-time me dekhni chahiye — pending, running, success, failed, cancelled. User ko pata chalna chahiye ki uska job abhi kis halat me hai.
  • Feature 3 — Update / Cancel: Scheduled job ko execute hone se pehle update ya cancel kar sakte hain. Schedule badlo, payload badlo, ya job hi hata do — execution se pehle tak ye sab allowed hai.
  • Feature 4 — Job dependencies (DAG): Job ek doosre par depend kar sakte hain — Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) execution order. Matlab Job B tabhi chalega jab Job A poora ho jaye.
  • Feature 5 — Retry mechanism: Fail hone par job dobara chale — configurable retry count aur backoff strategy ke saath. Kitni baar retry karna hai, aur har retry ke beech kitna gap rakhna hai, ye job define karte waqt set hota hai.
  • Feature 6 — Dead letter queue: Jo job saare retries ke baad bhi fail ho jaye, wo dead letter queue me jaye — taaki wo khoye nahi, aur baad me inspect/fix karke dobara chalaya ja sake.
  • Feature 7 — Prioritization aur resource allocation: Job prioritization aur per-job resource limits (CPU, memory) — important job pehle chale, aur ek job poora system ka resource na kha jaye.
Interview tip: Functional requirements bolte waqt har feature ke saath ek chhota example do — jaise "roz subah 6 baje ETL pipeline chalani hai" (cron), ya "payment job fail ho to 3 baar retry" — interviewer ko lagta hai tumne asal system socha hai, sirf list ratta nahi maari.

02Requirements

Non-Functional Requirements

System kaisa behave kare — scale, reliability aur scheduling ki shartein. Ye numbers interview me yaad rakhne wale hain.

Scale & Performance

  • Job Volume: Rozana millions of jobs, peak par thousands of jobs per second.
  • Executors: Parallel execution ke liye 100s of executor instances.
  • Latency: Job apne scheduled time ke ±2 seconds ke andar execute hona chahiye — 2s ki tolerance allowed hai.

Reliability & Consistency

  • CAP Theorem: Availability >> Consistency — job kam se kam ek baar to run hona hi chahiye, eventual consistency acceptable hai. Matlab thodi der ke liye status thoda peeche dikh sakta hai, lekin job chhootna nahi chahiye.
  • Execution Guarantee: At-least-once execution — fail hone par job retry hoga, isliye idempotency zaroori hai (job dobara chale to bhi result galat na ho).
  • Durability: Job state persisted rahe — system fail ho jaye tab bhi koi job lost nahi honi chahiye.

Scheduling Requirements

  • Cron Support: Standard cron expressions — jaise 0 0 * * * matlab roz raat 12 baje (midnight).
  • Time Zones: Alag-alag time zones me scheduling support karni hai — user IST me schedule kare, system UTC me store kare.
  • Backfill: Missed jobs chalane ki ability — agar scheduler down tha aur jobs miss ho gayi, to unhe baad me run karna hai.
Interview FavoriteInterviewers ALWAYS ask: "How to prevent duplicate scheduling?". Answer: (1) Redis distributed lock (SETNX) before publishing to Kafka, (2) Database last_polled_time update with WHERE clause, (3) Kafka exactly-once semantics with transactional.id. Show understanding of multiple layers.
WarningNEVER assume jobs are idempotent. Always design for at-least-once execution. Executors may crash mid-job, jobs may be rescheduled, retries happen. Jobs MUST handle duplicate execution safely (check state, upsert not insert).

03Requirements

Core Entities

System ke 5 core entities — inke bina design adhura hai. Har entity ka matlab aur uske main fields yaad rakho.

  • Entity 1 — Job: Task definition — job_id, name, schedule (cron/timestamp), payload, dependencies. Ye wo "kya karna hai, kab karna hai" wali definition hai, jo PostgreSQL me store hoti hai.
  • Entity 2 — Scheduler: Wo component jo time/cron expression ke hisaab se jobs ko schedule karta hai. Is design me iska naam Watcher hai — har 20 second me poll karta hai.
  • Entity 3 — Executor: Worker jo job execute karta hai — queue se job pull karta hai aur job logic chalata hai. 100s of executor instances parallel me chalte hain.
  • Entity 4 — JobRun: Execution instance — run_id, job_id, status, start_time, end_time, logs. Ek hi Job ke kai JobRuns ho sakte hain (har run, har retry ek alag JobRun hai).
  • Entity 5 — DAG (Directed Acyclic Graph): Workflow jisme multiple dependent jobs hote hain — A pehle, phir B, phir C aur D parallel me. "Acyclic" matlab cycle allowed nahi — A→B→C→A mana hai.
Job vs JobRun — farak samjho: Job ek definition hai ("roz raat 12 baje ETL chalao"), JobRun us definition ka ek execution hai ("aaj raat 12 baje wali run, run_id X, status success"). Monitoring aur history JobRuns par hoti hai, scheduling Jobs par.

05Design

High Level Design

Poora system ek line me: Clients → LB + API Gateway → Job Service → Job DB; Watcher → Redis + Kafka → Consumer → Executor Pool. Neeche har hop ka kaam hai, aur design diagram bhi hai.

  • Clients/Users → LB + API Gateway: Authentication, authorization, rate limiting, routing — saare requests pehle yahin se guzarte hain.
  • Job Service → Job DB (PostgreSQL): Job definitions store karta hai — schedule, payload, dependencies, retry config.
  • Job Executor → Job DB: Execute karne layak jobs pull karta hai, status update karta hai.
  • Scheduler (Watcher) → Redis + Kafka: Execution ke liye scheduled jobs ko poll karta hai, Kafka topics par publish karta hai.
  • Kafka Topics: run (immediate execution), retry (retry wale failed jobs), dead (permanently failed jobs).
  • Job Consumer Service → Executor Pool: Kafka se consume karta hai, available executors ko dispatch karta hai.
  • Executor Services (100s instances): Job logic execute karte hain, DB me status update karte hain, results Kafka par publish karte hain.
  • Redis: Scheduler ke liye last-polled-time store karta hai, executors ke liye distributed locks rakhta hai.

Design Diagram — poora flow ek nazar me

Diagram me dikhne wali extra details (jo text me chhoti likhi hain)

  • Watcher polling: Watcher har 10/20 second me poll karta hai aur [now, now+5min] window me scheduled jobs pull karta hai, unhe "queued" mark karta hai.
  • Stuck jobs ki pehchan: Watcher wo jobs bhi fetch karta hai jinki status 'running' hai aur last modified time 15 second se zyada purana hai — ye potentially stuck/crashed jobs hote hain.
  • Job DB schema (diagram se): Jobs table me id (index), name, schedule_type, status (paused/scheduled/cancel), schedule_time (partition), cron_expression, payload, retries (3), meta. Job_runs table me id, job_id (index), status (queued/running/success/failed), start_time, end_time, modified_time (index), executor_id, attempt_number, error_msg.
  • Cancel flow (diagram se): Cancel request Redis se hokar jaati hai — job: 1232, request: cancel, TTL — aur executor har 10 second me job ki status update karta hai.
  • Alternatives (diagram ke neeche): Redis Sorted Set aur Amazon SQS (exact time timestamp par scheduling support karta hai) — dono ko alternative scheduling mechanism ke roop me likha gaya hai.
Imaandari se note: Diagram hand-drawn whiteboard hai — kuch chhote labels (jaise "101.(1-100)") sirf image me hain aur unka detail text me nahi hai. Upar wahi likha hai jo diagram me saaf padha ja sakta hai; jo padha nahi ja saka, wo yahan invent nahi kiya gaya.

06Design

Deep Dive Design (Low Level)

Ab andar ki kahani — job create hone se lekar execute, retry, DAG, cancel aur monitoring tak, 10 steps me. Ye section interview ka sabse important hissa hai.

Step 1: Job Creation & Scheduling

  • User bhejta hai: POST /v1/api/jobs with {name: 'ETL Pipeline', schedule_type: 'cron', schedule_value: '0 0 * * *', payload: {db_connection}, retry_count: 3, timeout: 3600}.
  • Job Service validate karta hai: cron expression valid hai, one-time schedule ho to schedule_time future me hai, dependencies se cycle to nahi ban raha (DAG validation).
  • Service create karta hai: Job record PostgreSQL me — {job_id: UUID, name, schedule_type: 'cron', schedule_value, status: 'scheduled', next_run_time: '2025-01-21T00:00:00Z', created_at}.
  • Service calculate karta hai: next_run_time cron parser library (Python me croniter) se, indexed column me store karta hai taaki polling efficient rahe.
  • Service return karta hai: {job_id, status: 'scheduled', next_run_time}.

Step 2: Scheduler (Watcher) Polling

  • Watcher service chalti hai: infinite loop, 20-second interval (configurable).
  • Service query karti hai: SELECT * FROM jobs WHERE status IN ('scheduled', 'running') AND next_run_time <= NOW() AND last_polled_time < (NOW - 30s) LIMIT 1000.
  • Service fetch karti hai: execution ke liye ready 1000 jobs, last_polled_time = NOW() update karti hai taaki doosre watchers duplicate process na karein.
  • Har job ke liye: schedule_type ke hisaab se check karta hai ki run karna hai ya nahi — cron ho to current time expression se match karta hai, one-time ho to next_run_time beet gaya hai ya nahi.
  • Service publish karti hai: Job ko Kafka topic 'run' par — {job_id, run_id: UUID, payload, timestamp, attempt: 1}.
TipKey optimization: Batch watcher queries. Fetching 1 job per query = 1000 queries/min overhead. Fetching 1000 jobs per query = 1 query/min, 1000x reduction in DB load. Pagination + batch Kafka publishing critical for scale.

Step 3: Job Consumption & Execution

  • Job Consumer Service: Kafka topics 'run' aur 'retry' ko consume karta hai, consumer group 'job-consumers', job_id se partitioned (ordering ke liye).
  • Consumer receive karta hai: Job message {job_id, run_id, payload, attempt: 1}.
  • Consumer create karta hai: JobRun record DB me — {run_id, job_id, status: 'pending', scheduled_at, created_at}.
  • Consumer check karta hai: Redis executors:available se available executor capacity (har executor par active jobs track hote hain), load balancing implement karta hai.
  • Consumer dispatch karta hai: Job ko Executor Service ko internal API POST /executor/run se — {run_id, job_id, payload}, status 'running' update karta hai.
  • Executor update karta hai: DB me status='running', start_time=NOW(), aur har 10 second me heartbeat publish karta hai Redis par — executor:{executor_id}:heartbeat.

Step 4: Job Execution by Executor

  • Executor receive karta hai: Job details {run_id, job_id, payload}.
  • Executor fetch karta hai: Full job definition Job DB se (ya Redis cache se, TTL=5 min) — retry_count, timeout, dependencies lene ke liye.
  • Executor run karta hai: Job logic isolated process/container me (Docker container ya alag thread, resource limits ke saath).
  • Job execution: User-defined code chalta hai (Python script, shell command, external service ko HTTP request), logs centralized logging me stream hote hain (CloudWatch, ELK).
  • Timeout handling: Execution timeout (3600s) se zyada ho to process kill karo, status 'timeout' mark karo.
  • Executor update karta hai: DB me status='success' ya 'failed', end_time, error_msg (agar failed ho), execution_time_ms.
WarningNEVER store job logs in database. 100K jobs/day × 10KB logs = 1GB/day, 365GB/year in DB (expensive, slow queries). Stream logs to S3/CloudWatch, store only URL in DB. DB for metadata, object storage for logs.

Step 5: Retry Mechanism

  • Job fail hone par: Executor check karta hai job.retry_count > current_attempt (jaise retry_count=3, attempt=1).
  • Executor publish karta hai: Kafka 'retry' topic par — {job_id, run_id, payload, attempt: 2, retry_delay: 60s (exponential backoff)}.
  • Delay calculation: retry_delay = base_delay * (2 ^ attempt) + jitter — jaise attempts 1, 2, 3 ke liye 60s, 120s, 240s.
  • Retry Consumer: 'retry' topic consume karta hai, retry_delay duration tak sota hai, phir dobara 'run' topic par republish karta hai.
  • Final failure: Agar attempt > retry_count (jaise attempt=4, retry_count=3), to 'dead' topic par publish karo, job status='failed_permanently' update karo, alert bhejo.
Interview FavoriteMust mention: Exponential backoff with jitter. Without jitter, 100 jobs failing at same time all retry at exact same moment → thundering herd → system overload. Jitter (random 0-30s) spreads retries over time.

Step 6: DAG Dependency Resolution

  • Job definition: Job B, Job A ke completion par depend karta hai — jobs.dependencies = [job_a_id] me store hota hai.
  • Scheduler check karta hai: Job B schedule karne se pehle, JobRuns table query karo — WHERE job_id IN (dependencies) AND status='success' AND run_date = TODAY.
  • Conditional execution: Saari dependencies poori hain to Job B ko Kafka 'run' par publish karo, warna skip karo aur agle watcher cycle ka wait karo.
  • Parallel execution: Jobs C aur D dono B par depend karte hain — B succeed hote hi C aur D dono simultaneously publish hote hain, parallel execution ke liye.
  • Cycle detection: Job create karte waqt dependency graph par DFS (Depth-First Search) chalao, cycle mile to reject karo (jaise A→B→C→A).
TipDAG cycle detection is critical. Allow A→B→C→A dependency creates infinite loop, system hangs. Run DFS on dependency graph during job creation, reject if cycle found. Also limit dependency depth (e.g., max 10 levels).

Step 7: Job Cancellation

  • User bhejta hai: POST /v1/api/jobs/{job_id}/cancel.
  • Job Service check karta hai: current status — agar 'scheduled' hai (running nahi), to DB me status='cancelled' update karo, agle scheduling cycle se hata do.
  • Agar status='running' hai: Kafka 'cancel' topic par cancellation event publish karo — {job_id, run_id}.
  • Executor receive karta hai: 'cancel' topic ko subscribe karke cancellation event, job process ko SIGTERM bhejta hai.
  • Graceful shutdown: Process ke paas 30 second hain cleanup ke liye (DB connections band karo, state save karo), phir exit na ho to SIGKILL.
  • Executor update karta hai: DB me status='cancelled', end_time=NOW(), completion event publish karta hai.

Step 8: Monitoring & Health Checks

  • Watcher service monitor karti hai: Executor heartbeats Redis me — agar executor:{id}:heartbeat 60 second se update nahi hua, to executor 'unhealthy' mark karo.
  • Service identify karti hai: Unhealthy executor par chal rahe jobs — query JobRuns WHERE executor_id={id} AND status='running'.
  • Service reschedule karti hai: Un jobs ko Kafka 'run' topic par publish karke, note 'rescheduled_from_dead_executor' ke saath, purani run ki status='executor_died' update karti hai.
  • Dashboard metrics: Total jobs scheduled, running, success, failed (last 24h), avg execution time, success rate %, executors online/offline.
  • Alerts trigger hote hain: job failure rate >10%, executor capacity >80%, dead letter queue size >100 jobs.
Interview FavoriteInterviewers love asking: "What if executor crashes during job execution?". Answer: (1) Heartbeat monitoring detects dead executor, (2) Orphaned jobs rescheduled as retries, (3) Jobs must be idempotent. Show understanding of failure recovery.

Step 9: Distributed Locking (Prevent Duplicate Execution)

  • Problem: Multiple watcher instances jobs poll karte hain, ek hi job do baar schedule ho sakta hai.
  • Solution: Redis distributed lock, SETNX se — job schedule karne se pehle lock acquire karo: SET lock:job:{job_id}:schedule {watcher_id} NX EX 60.
  • Lock mil gaya: Watcher job schedule karta hai (Kafka par publish), lock release karta hai — DELETE lock:job:{job_id}:schedule.
  • Lock nahi mila: Doosra watcher us job ko already process kar raha hai, agle job par jao.
  • Auto-expiry: Watcher crash ho jaye to lock 60 second me expire ho jata hai, deadlock nahi hota, agla watcher cycle job ko phir pick kar leta hai.

Step 10: Handling Missed Jobs (Backfill)

  • Scenario: Scheduler 2 ghante down raha, 100 hourly jobs miss ho gayi.
  • Backfill detection: Watcher restart par query karo — jobs WHERE next_run_time < (NOW - 2 * schedule_interval) AND status='scheduled'.
  • Backfill strategy: Har missed job ke liye JobRun banao — scheduled_at = missed_time, status='backfill', Kafka par publish karo.
  • Throttling: Backfill jobs controlled rate par publish karo (100 jobs/sec) taaki executors overwhelm na hon.
  • User notification: Alert bhejo — 'Scheduler was down, running 100 missed jobs' — transparency ke liye.
WarningNEVER use polling interval <10 seconds for watcher. 5s interval = 12 polls/min = 12 DB queries/min + 12 lock acquisitions. Minimal latency improvement but 2x overhead. 20s interval is sweet spot for most use cases.

Alternative Scheduling Solutions

Custom scheduler banana hi ek raasta nahi hai — 3 alternative approaches hain. Interview me trade-offs ke saath bolo, to design ki gehrai dikhti hai.

Approach 1: Event-Driven (Amazon EventBridge)

  • Concept: Jobs time-based polling se nahi, events se trigger hote hain.
  • Implementation: EventBridge rules cron expressions se match karte hain, Lambda functions ya SQS queues trigger karte hain.
  • Pros: Serverless, auto-scaling, no polling overhead, pay-per-execution.
  • Cons: Vendor lock-in (AWS), ek rule par max 300 targets, cold start latency.
  • Use case: Simple scheduled tasks, cloud-native architectures.

Approach 2: Managed Service (AWS Step Functions, Temporal)

  • Concept: Workflow orchestration as a service — built-in retry, state management ke saath.
  • Implementation: Workflows define karo JSON (Step Functions) ya code (Temporal) se, service execution sambhalta hai.
  • Pros: No infrastructure management, built-in monitoring, durable execution (crash ke baad bhi survive karta hai).
  • Cons: Cost (per state transition), learning curve, execution par kam control.
  • Use case: Complex workflows with multiple steps, enterprise applications.

Approach 3: Custom with Delay Queue (RabbitMQ, SQS)

  • Concept: Message queue ke delay feature se jobs schedule karo.
  • Implementation: Message publish karo delay = (scheduled_time - now) ke saath, delay expire hote hi consumer pick kar leta hai.
  • Pros: Simple, existing message queue ka use, priorities support karta hai.
  • Cons: Max delay ki limit (SQS: 15 min, phir rescheduling chahiye), no cron support, recurring jobs ke liye poor.
  • Use case: One-time delayed jobs, reminder systems.
TipPriority queue via separate Kafka topics is simpler than single-topic priority. 3 topics (high/med/low) with different consumer counts gives natural prioritization. Single topic requires custom consumer logic to peek at priority header.
Kab kaunsa approach? Simple one-time reminders ho to delay queue kaafi hai. Complex multi-step workflows ho to Temporal/Step Functions. Aur jab full control, custom DAGs aur 10K jobs/sec scale chahiye, to custom scheduler (is lesson ka design) sahi hai.

Millions of jobs per day aur 10K jobs/sec — ye 12 techniques system ko scale par chalati hain. Har technique ek line me, interview me 3–4 bolna kaafi hai.

  • Technique 1 — Horizontal Executor Scaling: Zyada executor instances add karo, Kafka partitioning parallel processing ensure karta hai.
  • Technique 2 — Database Indexing: Index on (next_run_time, status, last_polled_time) — Watcher queries tez ho jati hain.
  • Technique 3 — Job Caching: Job definitions Redis me cache karo (TTL: 5 min) — DB reads 90% kam ho jate hain.
  • Technique 4 — Kafka Partitioning: 'run' topic ko job_id se partition karo — ordering milti hai, race conditions nahi hote.
  • Technique 5 — Read Replicas: Job history queries PostgreSQL read replicas par bhejo, writes sirf primary par.
  • Technique 6 — Watcher Sharding: Multiple watcher instances, alag polling intervals — watcher-1 har 20s, watcher-2 har 60s low-priority jobs ke liye.
  • Technique 7 — Priority Queues: High/low priority jobs ke liye alag Kafka topics, high-priority consumers ke zyada instances.
  • Technique 8 — Batch Processing: Watcher ek query me 1000 jobs fetch karta hai (1 ki jagah), Kafka par batch me publish karta hai.

Common Interview Questions

Lesson ke 10 sabse important interview Q&A — har answer step-by-step hai, example ke saath. Yehi sawaal interview me sabse zyada poochhe jate hain.

Q1: How do you prevent the same job from being scheduled twice by multiple watcher instances?

  • Lock acquisition: Job schedule karne se pehle watcher try karta hai — SET lock:job:{job_id}:schedule {watcher_id} NX EX 60 (set if not exists, 60s expiry).
  • Lock success: Agar 1 return ho, to watcher job ko Kafka par publish karta hai, DB me last_polled_time update karta hai, lock release karta hai.
  • Lock failure: Agar 0 return ho, to doosra watcher us job ko already process kar raha hai — skip karke agle job par jao.
  • Database-level protection: UPDATE jobs SET last_polled_time = NOW() WHERE job_id = {id} AND last_polled_time < (NOW - 30s) — update tabhi hota hai jab recently poll nahi hua.
  • Auto-expiry: Watcher crash ho jaye to lock 60s me expire ho jata hai, deadlock nahi hota.
  • Alternative: Kafka ko single-source scheduling banao — sirf 1 watcher Kafka par publish kare (leader election via Zookeeper), baaki consumers sambhalte hain.
  • Trade-off: Redis lock tez aur simple hai, Kafka leader election multi-datacenter ke liye zyada robust hai.
  • Example: Job J1, next_run_time=10:00 AM → Watcher-1 aur Watcher-2 ek saath poll karte hain → Watcher-1 lock leta hai → J1 ko Kafka par publish karta hai → lock release karta hai → Watcher-2 ka lock fail hota hai → J1 skip karta hai.

Q2: How do you handle jobs that are scheduled while the scheduler is down?

  • Detection: Watcher restart par query karo — jobs WHERE next_run_time < NOW() AND status IN ('scheduled', 'running') — missed jobs pehchano.
  • Categorization: Pichle 2 ghante ke missed jobs = recent misses (high priority), 2 ghante se zyada purane = stale (policy ke hisaab se skip ya backfill).
  • Backfill execution: Har missed job ke liye JobRun banao — scheduled_at = original_next_run_time, status='backfill', Kafka 'run' topic par publish karo.
  • Rate limiting: Backfill jobs 100 jobs/sec ki rate par publish karo taaki executors overwhelm na hon (1000 missed jobs ho to queue karne me 10s lagenge).
  • SLA check: Job ki SLA ho (schedule ke 1 ghante ke andar run hona chahiye) aur breach ho gaya ho, to skip karo, 'missed_sla' mark karo, user ko alert karo.
  • Recurring jobs: Cron jobs ke liye saare missed runs calculate karo — daily job 3 din down raha to Day 1, 2, 3 ke liye 3 JobRuns banao, ya sirf most recent par skip karo (configurable).
  • Example: Scheduler 10:00–12:00 down raha, Job A hourly schedule (10:00, 11:00, 12:00) → 12:05 restart par 2 missed runs detect hui → backfill ne 10:00 aur 11:00 ke runs banaye → dono Kafka par publish → executors ne process kiya → next_run_time 13:00 update hua.

Q3: How do you implement retry mechanism with exponential backoff?

  • Initial failure: Executor exception pakadta hai, job.retry_count (jaise 3) vs current attempt (1) check karta hai — retries bache hain to aage badho.
  • Delay calculation: retry_delay = base_delay * (2 ^ (attempt - 1)) + random(0, base_delay/2) — attempt 1 → 60s, attempt 2 → 120s + jitter (0-30s), attempt 3 → 240s + jitter.
  • Publish to retry topic: Executor Kafka 'retry' par publish karta hai — {job_id, run_id, payload, attempt: 2, scheduled_retry_at: NOW() + retry_delay}.
  • Retry consumer: Dedicated consumer group 'retry' topic padhta hai, har message ke liye wait_time = scheduled_retry_at - NOW() calculate karta hai, wait_time > 0 ho to sleep karta hai, phir 'run' topic par republish karta hai.
  • Final failure: Attempt > retry_count ho to 'dead' topic par publish karo, DB status='failed_permanently' update karo, SNS/email se alert bhejo.
  • Jitter rationale: Thundering herd rokta hai — 100 jobs ek saath fail hon to bina jitter ke sab ek hi moment par retry karte.
  • Alternative: Kafka delayed message feature (Kafka 3.0+) ya SQS delay queue retry scheduling ke liye.
  • Example: Job 10:00 fail hua → retry_count=3 → attempt 1 fail → retry 10:01 (60s) → attempt 2 fail → retry 10:03 (120s) → attempt 3 fail → retry 10:07 (240s) → attempt 4 (retry_count exceed) → dead letter queue.

Q4: How do you handle job dependencies in a DAG (Directed Acyclic Graph)?

  • Storage: Job table me dependencies column hai — job_b.dependencies = [job_a_id, job_c_id] matlab B, A aur C par depend karta hai.
  • Cycle detection: Job create/update karte waqt naye job se DFS (Depth-First Search) chalao — ek hi node do baar visit ho to cycle hai, reject karo: 'Circular dependency detected: A→B→C→A'.
  • Scheduling check: Watcher Job B schedule karne se pehle query karta hai — SELECT COUNT(*) FROM job_runs WHERE job_id IN (job_a_id, job_c_id) AND status='success' AND DATE(scheduled_at) = TODAY — count 2 na ho (dependencies missing) to Job B is cycle me skip karo.
  • Trigger on completion: Job A successfully complete hote hi 'job.completed' event Kafka par publish hota hai — {job_id: A, run_date}.
  • Dependency watcher: Alag service 'job.completed' consume karti hai, jobs query karti hai WHERE dependencies CONTAINS job_a_id, check karti hai saari dependencies poori hui ya nahi, poori hui to dependent jobs 'run' topic par publish karo.
  • Parallel execution: Ek hi level ke jobs (C aur D dono sirf B par depend karte hain) simultaneously publish hote hain, parallel execution ke liye.
  • Alternative: Airflow ka approach — deployment par DAG precompute karo, graph memory me store karo, har run par traverse karo.
  • Example: DAG: A → B → C, D (B, A par depend karta hai; C aur D, B par) → A 10:00 run hota hai, succeed hota hai → B 10:05 schedule hota hai, succeed hota hai → C aur D dono 10:10 parallel schedule hote hain.

Q5: What happens if an executor crashes while running a job?

  • Heartbeat monitoring: Executors har 10 second me Redis par heartbeat publish karte hain — executor:{id}:heartbeat, timestamp ke saath.
  • Watcher health check: Alag health check service har 30 second me Redis poll karti hai, kisi executor ka heartbeat 60 second se zyada purana ho (3 missed heartbeats) to 'unhealthy' mark karti hai.
  • Job identification: Query karo — JobRuns WHERE executor_id = {unhealthy_id} AND status='running' — orphaned jobs mil jate hain.
  • Rescheduling: Har orphaned job ke liye status='executor_died' update karo, naya JobRun banao attempt = old_attempt + 1 ke saath (retry count hota hai), Kafka 'run' topic par publish karo.
  • Cleanup: executor:{id} ko Redis executors:available pool se hatao, ops team ko alert karo.
  • Idempotency requirement: Jobs idempotent hone chahiye (multiple baar run karna safe ho) kyunki crashed job crash se pehle partially complete ho chuka ho sakta hai.
  • Example: Executor E1 Job J1 chala raha hai (database me likh raha hai) → E1 50% completion par crash → heartbeat ruk gaya → health check 60s baad detect karta hai → Job J1 Executor E2 par reschedule hota hai → E2 full job dobara chalata hai → job code ko 'partial completion' scenario handle karna chahiye (check karo data already likha hai ya nahi, skip karo ya upsert karo).
  • Prevention: Critical sections ke liye distributed locks use karo (job modify karne se pehle specific resource lock kare).

Q6: How do you implement priority-based job scheduling?

  • Priority definition: Jobs me priority field hota hai (1-10, 10=highest), Job table me store hota hai.
  • Topic separation: Kafka topics — high_priority_run (priority 8-10), medium_priority_run (5-7), low_priority_run (1-4).
  • Watcher routing: Job schedule karte waqt job.priority ke hisaab se sahi topic par publish karo.
  • Consumer allocation: High priority topic par 50 consumer instances, medium par 30, low par 20 (high priority ko 2.5x zyada resources).
  • Within-topic ordering: Ek hi priority topic ke jobs FIFO (First In First Out) process hote hain, Kafka partitioning se.
  • Starvation prevention: Low priority jobs 1 ghante se zyada wait kar rahe hon to temporarily medium priority par boost karo (aging algorithm).
  • Alternative: Single topic with priority header — consumers high-priority partitions ko zyada frequently poll karte hain.
  • Example: Job A (priority 9) aur Job B (priority 3) ek saath schedule hue → A high_priority_run par gaya, B low_priority_run par → high topic par 50 consumers, low par 20 → A ko executor <1s me mila, B ko 5s wait karna pada.
  • Trade-off: Zyada topics = zyada complexity lekin better isolation; single topic = simpler lekin consumers ko priority-aware logic chahiye.

Q7: How do you handle time zone conversions for scheduled jobs?

  • Storage: DB me saare timestamps UTC me store hote hain — jobs.next_run_time = '2025-01-21T00:00:00Z'.
  • User input: Job creation schedule ke saath time zone accept karta hai — {schedule: '0 0 * * *', timezone: 'America/New_York'}.
  • Conversion on save: Backend timezone library (Python me pytz) se UTC me convert karta hai — cron '0 0 * * *' EST (UTC-5) me → UTC '0 5 * * *', UTC version store hota hai.
  • Display: User job dekhe to uske timezone me convert karke dikhao — '2025-01-21T00:00:00Z' UTC → '2025-01-20T19:00:00' EST.
  • DST handling: Daylight saving time change hote waqt (March/November) next_run_time dobara calculate karo — jaise '9 AM EST' wala job UTC me 1 ghanta shift ho jata hai.
  • Watcher logic: Hamesha UTC me kaam karta hai, scheduling ke liye timezone awareness nahi chahiye.
  • Complexity: DST boundary ke aas-paas recurring jobs — '2 AM EST daily' wala job DST start wale din (2 AM exist hi nahi karta) 3 AM par skip karo ya 1 AM par chalao (configurable).
  • Example: India ka user (IST, UTC+5:30) daily midnight local time par job schedule karta hai → backend UTC 18:30 previous day store karta hai → watcher 18:30 UTC par job trigger karta hai → user UI me '00:00 IST' dekhta hai.
  • Alternative: Har job ke saath timezone store karo, watcher on-the-fly convert kare (zyada flexible lekin complex).

Q8: How do you implement job cancellation for already running jobs?

  • User request: POST /jobs/{job_id}/cancel — service status check karta hai, 'running' ho to Kafka 'cancel' topic par publish karta hai — {job_id, run_id}.
  • Executor subscription: Saare executors 'cancel' topic ko subscribe karte hain, har executor ka alag consumer group (taaki sabko message mile).
  • Executor matching: Executor check karta hai run_id currently running job se match karta hai ya nahi — match ho to cancellation shuru karo.
  • Signal sending: Executor job process ko SIGTERM (signal 15) bhejta hai — graceful shutdown ki allow karta hai (connections band karo, state save karo).
  • Timeout: Process 30 second me exit na ho to SIGKILL (signal 9) bhejkar force terminate karo.
  • Status update: Executor DB me status='cancelled', end_time=NOW() update karta hai, 'job.cancelled' event publish karta hai.
  • Cleanup: Job ke paas jo distributed locks hain unhe release karo, transactions rollback karo agar applicable ho.
  • Edge case: Cancellation receive hone se pehle job already complete ho gaya ho → executor cancel message ignore karta hai, DB status 'success' rehta hai.
  • Alternative: Shared memory flag use karo (Redis cancel:{run_id} = true), job periodically flag check kare aur set ho to exit kare (job code ka cooperation chahiye).
  • Example: 2 ghante ka long-running ETL job → user 30 min baad cancel karta hai → cancel event publish hota hai → executor SIGTERM bhejta hai → job ka cleanup handler chalta hai (partial data commit karta hai) → gracefully exit karta hai → status='cancelled', user retry kar sakta hai ya partial results analyze kar sakta hai.

Q9: How do you prevent job duplication on executor restart?

  • Job uniqueness: Har JobRun ka unique run_id hota hai, executor start par currently_running_job = run_id Redis me store karta hai.
  • Restart detection: Executor restart par Redis me currently_running_job check karta hai — agar hai, to wo mid-execution me kill hua tha.
  • Status reconciliation: DB me run_id ki status query karo — agar abhi bhi 'running' hai, to 'executor_died' mark karo (executor crash hua tha), dobara execute mat karo (duplication rokta hai).
  • Kafka offset management: Executor Kafka offset tabhi commit karta hai jab job complete ho — restart par uncommitted messages dobara deliver hote hain (at-least-once).
  • Idempotency enforcement: job_id + scheduled_at combination uniqueness ensure karta hai — executor same (job_id, scheduled_at) wala job start karne ki koshish kare to DB unique constraint fail ho jata hai, execution skip hoti hai.
  • Exactly-once attempt: Kafka transactions (Kafka 0.11+) use karo, har executor ka transactional.id — message process aur offset commit atomically hote hain.
  • Trade-off: At-least-once (simple, idempotent jobs chahiye) vs exactly-once (complex, duplication ki guarantee lekin slow).
  • Example: Executor E1 Job J1 chala raha hai (run_id=R1) → E1 crash → E1 restart → Redis check karta hai, currently_running_job=R1 milta hai → DB query karta hai, R1 status='running' → R1 ko 'executor_died' update karta hai, dobara run nahi karta → health check R1 ko naye run_id=R2 ke saath reschedule karta hai → retry count hota hai.

Q10: What's your strategy for monitoring and alerting on job failures?

  • Metrics collection: Executors metrics publish karte hain Prometheus/CloudWatch par — job_success_count, job_failure_count, job_duration_ms, jobs_in_queue (priority ke hisaab se).
  • Failure thresholds: Alert rules — single job failure rate >50% (pichle 10 runs me 6 fail) = critical alert; overall system failure rate >10% in 1 hour = warning.
  • SLA monitoring: Job ki SLA ho (schedule ke 2 ghante ke andar complete hona chahiye) to alert karo agar end_time - scheduled_at > 2 hours.
  • Dead letter queue size: Alert karo agar dead topic me 100 se zyada messages hain (systemic issue ka sign hai, isolated failures ka nahi).
  • Executor health: Alert karo agar 30% se kam executors healthy hain (capacity issue).
  • Dashboard: Grafana dashboard dikhata hai — jobs by status (pie chart), execution time trends (line graph), failure reasons (top 10 errors).
  • Alert channels: PagerDuty critical ke liye (job X 5 baar fail hua), Slack warnings ke liye (10% system failure rate), Email daily digest saari failures ka.
  • Example: ETL job lagatar 3 baar fail hota hai → alert 'Job ETL_Pipeline failing (3/3 attempts), error: DB connection timeout' Slack par jata hai → on-call engineer investigate karta hai, DB overloaded milta hai → DB scale karta hai → retry 4 par job succeed hota hai.

Interview FAQs

Scheduler design ke 36 sabse zyada poochhe jaane wale sawaal — lesson ke Q&A aur related concepts, current-era interviews ke hisaab se. Har sawaal par click karo, jawab khulega.

Q01Job scheduler kya hota hai, aur ye normal cron se kaise alag hai?

Job scheduler ek aisa system hai jo jobs ko specified time par automatically schedule aur execute karta hai — cron, retries, failure handling aur real-time execution ke saath. Normal cron sirf ek machine par time-based command chalata hai; distributed job scheduler (jaise Airflow, Temporal, Celery) hazaron jobs per second, multiple executors, DAG dependencies, retry aur monitoring — ye sab deta hai. Cron single-node hai, scheduler distributed hai.

Q02Airflow, Temporal aur Celery me kya farak hai?

Airflow DAG-based workflow orchestration hai — Python se DAG define karo, scheduler chalata hai, UI me monitoring milti hai. Temporal durable execution deta hai — workflow code crash ke baad bhi wahin se resume hota hai, state service sambhalta hai. Celery distributed task queue hai — simple, Redis/RabbitMQ ke saath tasks distribute karta hai, lekin DAG aur durable workflows ke liye kam features hain. Interview me bolo: simple tasks ke liye Celery, complex DAGs ke liye Airflow, durable long-running workflows ke liye Temporal.

Q03DAG kya hai, aur isme cycle kyun mana hai?

DAG matlab Directed Acyclic Graph — jobs nodes hote hain, dependencies directed edges hote hain, aur cycle nahi hota. Cycle mana hai kyunki A→B→C→A me koi job kabhi start hi nahi ho sakta — har job doosre ka wait karta rahega, system hang ho jayega. Isliye job create karte waqt dependency graph par DFS chalakar cycle detect karte hain, aur cycle mile to job reject kar dete hain.

Q04Cron expression kaise padhte hain? 0 0 * * * ka matlab kya hai?

Cron expression ke 5 fields hote hain: minute, hour, day-of-month, month, day-of-week. 0 0 * * * matlab minute=0, hour=0, baaki sab * (har) — roz raat 12 baje (midnight). 30 2 * * 1 matlab har Monday raat 2:30 baje. 0 9-17 * * 1-5 matlab weekday par har ghante subah 9 se shaam 5 ke beech. Cron ko samajhna scheduler design ki pehli shart hai.

Q05At-least-once vs exactly-once execution — kya farak hai, aur kaunsa choose karein?

At-least-once me job kam se kam ek baar zaroor chalega, lekin crash ya retry ki wajah se do baar bhi chal sakta hai — isliye jobs idempotent hone chahiye. Exactly-once me job sirf ek hi baar chalega, lekin iske liye Kafka transactions jaise complex mechanisms chahiye, performance hit hota hai. Job scheduling ke liye standard hai at-least-once + idempotency — simple hai, aur zyada tar jobs ko idempotent banana mushkil nahi hota (upsert use karo, insert nahi).

Q06Multiple watcher instances ek hi job ko do baar schedule na karein — kaise rokte hain?

Teen layers hain. Pehla: Redis distributed lock — schedule karne se pehle SET lock:job:{job_id}:schedule {watcher_id} NX EX 60 karo; lock mile to hi Kafka par publish karo. Doosra: DB update with WHERE clause — UPDATE jobs SET last_polled_time = NOW() WHERE last_polled_time < (NOW - 30s), taaki doosra watcher same job ko dobara na uthaye. Teesra: Kafka exactly-once semantics with transactional.id. Interview me teeno layers bolo — ek layer bolna kaafi nahi hota.

Q07Watcher kya karta hai, aur polling interval kitna rakhna chahiye?

Watcher (Scheduler) ek infinite loop me har 20 second me PostgreSQL poll karta hai — jobs jinki next_run_time beet gayi ho aur last_polled_time 30 second se purana ho, unhe 1000 ki batch me fetch karta hai, Redis lock lekar Kafka 'run' topic par publish karta hai. 20s interval sweet spot hai — 5s interval par latency sirf thodi kam hoti hai lekin DB queries aur lock acquisitions 2x ho jate hain. 10 second se kam interval kabhi mat rakho.

Q08Retry me exponential backoff kya hai, aur jitter kyun zaroori hai?

Exponential backoff me har retry ke beech ka gap double hota jata hai — 60s, phir 120s, phir 240s (base_delay * 2^attempt). Isse failing system ko saans lene ka time milta hai. Jitter matlab har delay me random 0-30s add karna — bina jitter ke 100 jobs ek saath fail hon to sab ek hi moment par retry karte hain, thundering herd ban jata hai aur system phir overload ho jata hai. Jitter retries ko time par phaila deta hai.

Q09Dead letter queue kya hai, aur kab use hoti hai?

Dead letter queue (is design me Kafka 'dead' topic) wo jagah hai jahan permanently failed jobs jate hain — jab attempt retry_count se zyada ho jaye (jaise 3 retries ke baad 4th attempt). Job wahan kho nahi jata — ops team use inspect kar sakti hai, fix karke dobara chala sakti hai. Dead topic me 100 se zyada messages hon to alert trigger hota hai, kyunki ye systemic issue ka sign hota hai, isolated failures ka nahi.

Q10Backfill kya hai? Scheduler down ho to missed jobs ka kya hota hai?

Backfill matlab missed jobs ko baad me chalana. Scheduler down raha (jaise 2 ghante), to restart par watcher query karta hai jobs WHERE next_run_time < (NOW - 2 * schedule_interval) AND status='scheduled' — missed jobs mil jati hain. Har missed job ke liye JobRun banta hai (scheduled_at = missed_time, status='backfill') aur Kafka par publish hota hai — 100 jobs/sec ki throttled rate par, taaki executors overwhelm na hon. User ko alert bhi bheja jata hai: 'Scheduler was down, running 100 missed jobs'.

Q11Executor crash ho jaye to running job ka kya hota hai?

Executors har 10 second me Redis par heartbeat publish karte hain. Health check service har 30 second me check karti hai — 60 second se heartbeat na aaye (3 missed heartbeats) to executor 'unhealthy' mark hota hai. Us executor par 'running' jobs query karke orphaned jobs pehchane jate hain, unki status 'executor_died' hoti hai, aur naye JobRun (attempt + 1) ke saath Kafka 'run' topic par reschedule hote hain. Isiliye jobs idempotent hone chahiye — crashed job aadha complete ho chuka ho sakta hai.

Q12Priority-based scheduling kaise implement karte hain?

Jobs me priority field hota hai (1-10, 10 sabse zyada). Watcher job ko priority ke hisaab se alag Kafka topic par publish karta hai — high_priority_run (8-10), medium_priority_run (5-7), low_priority_run (1-4). High priority topic par 50 consumer instances, medium par 30, low par 20 hote hain — high priority ko 2.5x zyada resources milte hain. Starvation rokne ke liye aging algorithm hai — low priority job 1 ghante se zyada wait kare to temporarily medium par boost ho jata hai.

Q13Time zone conversions kaise handle karte hain? DST ka kya karein?

DB me saare timestamps UTC me store hote hain. User job create karte waqt timezone deta hai (jaise America/New_York), backend pytz se UTC me convert karke store karta hai — cron '0 0 * * *' EST me UTC '0 5 * * *' ban jata hai. Display karte waqt wapas user ke timezone me convert karte hain. DST change (March/November) par next_run_time dobara calculate karna padta hai, kyunki '9 AM EST' UTC me 1 ghanta shift ho jata hai. Watcher hamesha UTC me kaam karta hai, use timezone ki parwah nahi hoti.

Q14Running job ko cancel kaise karte hain?

User POST /jobs/{job_id}/cancel bhejta hai. Job 'scheduled' ho to DB me status='cancelled' kar do, kaam khatam. Job 'running' ho to Kafka 'cancel' topic par event publish hota hai {job_id, run_id}. Executor cancel event sunta hai, match karne wale process ko SIGTERM bhejta hai — 30 second graceful shutdown ke liye (connections band karo, state save karo). 30s me exit na ho to SIGKILL se force terminate karte hain. Phir DB status='cancelled' aur 'job.cancelled' event publish hota hai.

Q15Idempotency kyun zaroori hai? Job idempotent kaise banate hain?

At-least-once execution me job do baar chal sakta hai — executor crash, retry, ya reschedule ki wajah se. Agar job idempotent nahi hai, to dobara chalne par galat result hoga (jaise payment do baar deduct). Idempotent banane ke tareeke: state check karo (pehle se process hua hai to skip karo), insert ki jagah upsert use karo, aur job_id + scheduled_at ki unique constraint lagao taaki duplicate run DB par hi fail ho jaye.

Q16Kafka topics kaun se hain, aur har ek ka kya kaam hai?

Paanch topics hain. run (10 partitions, job_id se) — immediate execution ke liye ready jobs. retry (5 partitions) — retry bache hue failed jobs. dead (1 partition, low volume) — permanently failed jobs. cancel (3 partitions) — running jobs ki cancellation requests. completed (10 partitions) — downstream systems ke liye job completion events. Partitioning job_id se hoti hai taaki ek job ke events ka order bana rahe.

Q17Redis is design me kis kaam aata hai?

Redis paanch cheezein sambhalta hai: distributed locks (lock:job:{job_id}:schedule, NX EX 60), executor heartbeats (executor:{id}:heartbeat, har 10s), available executors ka pool (executors:available hash — load balancing ke liye), job definitions ka cache (job:{job_id}:cache, TTL 5 min — DB reads 90% kam), aur last_poll_time (watcher ka last successful poll). Redis yahan fast coordination layer hai, source of truth nahi — source of truth PostgreSQL hai.

Q18PostgreSQL me Jobs aur JobRuns tables me kya farak hai?

Jobs table master job definitions rakhti hai — job_id, name, schedule_type, schedule_value, next_run_time (indexed), last_polled_time, status, payload (jsonb), dependencies (uuid array), retry_count, timeout, priority. JobRuns table execution history rakhti hai — run_id, job_id (indexed FK), status (pending, running, success, failed, timeout, cancelled, executor_died), scheduled_at, start_time, end_time, execution_time_ms, executor_id, attempt, error_msg, logs_url. Ek Job ke kai JobRuns hote hain — har run aur har retry ek alag JobRun hai.

Q19Watcher query tez kaise rehti hai? Kaun se indexes lagte hain?

Watcher har 20s me query karta hai: SELECT * FROM jobs WHERE status IN ('scheduled', 'running') AND next_run_time <= NOW() AND last_polled_time < (NOW - 30s) LIMIT 1000. Ye tez isliye hai kyunki (next_run_time, status, last_polled_time) par composite index hai, aur next_run_time alag se indexed hai. Bina index ke millions of rows ki full table scan hoti, har poll par. LIMIT 1000 batch processing hai — ek poll me 1000 jobs, 1 query/min, 1000x kam DB load.

Q2010K jobs per second kaise handle karte hain?

Chaaro taraf parallelism hai. Watcher 1000 jobs per poll batch me fetch karta hai. Kafka 'run' topic 10 partitions me job_id se partition hota hai — parallel consumption. 100-1000 executor instances parallel me jobs chalate hain, har executor me 10 threads — 100 executors × 10 threads = 1000 concurrent jobs. Job definitions Redis me cached rehti hain (TTL 5 min) — DB reads 90% kam. History queries read replicas par jati hain, writes sirf primary par. Ye sab milkar 10K messages/sec Kafka throughput deta hai.

Q21Scheduling latency ±2 seconds kaise achieve hoti hai?

Latency ke teen hisse hain: watcher polling (20s interval — ye sabse bada hissa hai, lekin cron jobs ke liye acceptable hai), lock acquisition (~10ms per job, Redis SETNX), Kafka publish (~50ms batch of 1000 jobs) aur consumer processing (~100ms Kafka se executor assignment tak). Total ~20.16s from schedule time — polling interval hi latency decide karta hai. Isiliye lesson kehta hai jobs scheduled time ke ±2s ke andar execute hone chahiye — ye tolerance watcher interval ke andar ka execution window hai, aur immediate jobs ke liye runnow API hai.

Q22Circuit breaker kya hai is context me?

Agar job kisi external dependency par depend karta hai (jaise ek API) aur wo API lagatar 5 se zyada baar fail ho jaye, to circuit breaker job ko 10 minute ke liye pause kar deta hai. Isse failing API par spam requests nahi jati, aur system retry storm se bachta hai. 10 minute baad circuit phir try karta hai — API theek ho to job resume, nahi to phir pause. Ye retry mechanism ka complement hai — retry job ko dobara chalata hai, circuit breaker baar-baar fail hone par rukne ko kehta hai.

Q23Job logs database me kyun nahi store karte?

Simple maths hai: 100K jobs/day × 10KB logs = 1GB/day, saal bhar me 365GB — DB me itna data expensive hai aur queries slow ho jati hain. Isliye logs S3 ya CloudWatch par async stream karte hain, aur DB me sirf logs_url store karte hain. DB metadata ke liye hai, object storage logs ke liye. Ye separation scaling ke liye zaroori hai.

Q24EventBridge se scheduling karna custom scheduler se kab better hai?

EventBridge better hai jab jobs simple hon aur tum cloud-native stack me ho — serverless hai, auto-scaling hai, polling overhead nahi hai, pay-per-execution hai. Lekin limits hain: AWS vendor lock-in, ek rule par max 300 targets, cold start latency. Custom scheduler better hai jab complex DAGs hon, 10K jobs/sec scale chahiye, multi-cloud ya on-prem chahiye, ya execution par full control chahiye. Interview me dono ke trade-offs bolo, ek ko 'best' mat kaho.

Q25Step Functions ya Temporal kab use karna chahiye?

Jab workflows complex hon aur multiple steps hon — jaise ek workflow me 10 steps, har step par retry, state management aur monitoring chahiye. Step Functions (JSON se define) aur Temporal (code se define) durable execution dete hain — crash ke baad bhi workflow wahin se resume hota hai, infrastructure manage nahi karna padta. Cost per state transition hoti hai aur execution par control kam hota hai, lekin enterprise applications me ye trade-off worth hota hai.

Q26Delay queue (SQS/RabbitMQ) se scheduling ki kya limits hain?

Delay queue simple hai — message publish karo delay = (scheduled_time - now) ke saath, delay expire hote hi consumer pick kar leta hai. Lekin limits hain: max delay (SQS: 15 min — uske baad rescheduling chahiye), cron support nahi hai (recurring jobs mushkil hain), aur delay queues one-time delayed jobs aur reminder systems ke liye best hain. Recurring cron jobs ya DAGs ke liye ye approach poor hai — wahan custom scheduler ya managed service chahiye.

Q27JobRun ki saari statuses kaun si hain?

Saat statuses hain: pending (queue me wait kar raha hai), running (execute ho raha hai), success (complete ho gaya), failed (fail hua, retry bacha hai), timeout (3600s exceed ho gaya), cancelled (user ne cancel kiya), executor_died (executor crash ho gaya, job orphaned tha). Job ki status alag hai — scheduled, running, paused, cancelled. JobRun ki status ek execution ki kahani batati hai, Job ki status definition ki current state batati hai.

Q28last_polled_time ka kya kaam hai?

last_polled_time har job ka ek timestamp hai jo batata hai Watcher ne use kab poll kiya tha. Watcher query me condition hoti hai last_polled_time < (NOW - 30s) — matlab 30 second ke andar poll hua job dobara nahi uthaya jata. Jab watcher job fetch karta hai, to last_polled_time = NOW() update kar deta hai. Ye duplicate scheduling rokne ki database-level protection hai — Redis lock ke saath doosri layer. Agar ek watcher crash ho jaye to 30s ke baad doosra watcher job ko pick kar sakta hai.

Q29Watcher sharding kya hai?

Ek hi watcher saare jobs poll kare to wo bottleneck ban jata hai. Watcher sharding me multiple watcher instances hote hain, alag polling intervals ke saath — jaise watcher-1 har 20s high-priority jobs poll karta hai, watcher-2 har 60s low-priority jobs. Har watcher apni shard ke jobs dekhta hai, Redis locks ensure karte hain ki ek job do watchers schedule na karein. Isse polling load distribute hota hai aur system scale karta hai.

Q30Starvation prevention (aging) kya hai?

Priority queues me ek problem hoti hai: high priority jobs hamesha aate rahein to low priority jobs kabhi execute hi nahi hote — ye starvation hai. Aging algorithm iska solution hai — agar low priority job 1 ghante se zyada wait kar raha hai, to uski priority temporarily boost kar di jati hai (low se medium). Isse har job ko eventually execute hone ka chance milta hai, aur high priority jobs ka fayda bhi bana rehta hai.

Q31Monitoring aur alerting ke thresholds kya hain?

Chaar thresholds yaad rakho. Single job failure rate >50% (pichle 10 runs me 6 fail) = critical alert. Overall system failure rate >10% in 1 hour = warning. Dead letter queue me 100 se zyada messages = systemic issue ka alert. 30% se kam healthy executors = capacity issue ka alert. Metrics Prometheus/CloudWatch par jate hain, dashboard Grafana par hota hai — jobs by status, execution time trends, top 10 failure reasons. Critical alerts PagerDuty par, warnings Slack par, daily digest email par.

Q32Job timeout 3600s exceed ho jaye to kya hota hai?

Har job ka timeout hota hai — default 3600s (1 hour), per job configurable. Execution timeout se zyada ho jaye to executor process kill kar deta hai, JobRun ki status 'timeout' mark hoti hai, end_time aur execution_time_ms update hote hain. Timeout retry_count ke hisaab se retry ho sakta hai — timeout bhi ek failure hai. Long-running jobs ke liye timeout badha kar set karna chahiye, warna har ghante job kill hogi.

Q33One-time, cron aur interval schedule me kya farak hai?

Teen schedule_type hain. one_time — job ek hi baar, kisi specific timestamp par chalega (jaise kal subah 10 baje report bhejo). cron — recurring, cron expression se (jaise roz raat 12 baje). interval — har X time baad repeat (jaise har 30 minute me). DB me schedule_type enum (cron, one_time, interval) hai, schedule_value me cron expression ya ISO timestamp store hota hai. Watcher har type ko alag logic se check karta hai — cron ke liye expression match, one-time ke liye next_run_time beet gaya ya nahi.

Q34DAG Visualizer kyun chahiye? Sirf list kaafi nahi hai?

DAG Visualizer graph view dikhata hai — nodes jobs hote hain, edges dependencies hote hain. List se ye pata nahi chalta ki kaun kis par depend karta hai, kahan bottleneck hai, ya kaunsa job fail hone par poora workflow ruk jayega. Visualizer me ek nazar me dikhta hai: A ke baad B, B ke baad C aur D parallel. Debugging aur onboarding dono me kaam aata hai — naya engineer DAG dekh kar 2 minute me workflow samajh jata hai.

Q3520s polling interval sweet spot kyun hai? 5s kyun nahi rakhte?

5s interval par latency sirf 15 second kam hoti hai, lekin DB queries 4x ho jati hain (12 polls/min vs 3 polls/min), lock acquisitions bhi 4x, aur Redis/DB load badhta hai — minimal latency improvement, 2x-4x overhead. 20s interval par watcher 3 polls/min karta hai, 1000 jobs per poll fetch karta hai — scale aur latency ka balance hai. Lesson saaf kehta hai: polling interval 10 second se kam kabhi mat rakho. Latency kam karni hai to polling interval ghatao mat — immediate jobs ke liye runnow API do.

Q36Interview me is design ko 2 minute me kaise samjhaye?

One-liner se shuru karo: DAG-based scheduling → Redis/DB for job queue → Multiple executors → Kafka for retry/dead-letter → Watcher for monitoring. Phir flow bolo: user Job Service se job create karta hai (PostgreSQL), Watcher har 20s poll karke Kafka 'run' topic par publish karta hai, Consumer executors ko dispatch karta hai, fail hone par retry topic aur phir dead topic. Phir do hard problems bolo: duplicate scheduling (Redis SETNX lock) aur executor crash (heartbeat + reschedule). Aakhir me numbers: 10K jobs/sec, 1000 jobs per poll, 60s/120s/240s backoff. Bas — 2 minute me poora design.
