01Requirements, Entities & APIs

Functional Requirements

Functional requirements batate hain ki system kya-kya karega — user ke liye kaunsi features hongi. Is lesson me 8 features hain, registration aur KYC se lekar batch operations tak.

8 Features — ek-ek karke

  • Feature 1 — Registration, login aur KYC: user register kare, login kare, aur KYC verify karwaye (Aadhaar, PAN card). Bina KYC ke trading nahi kar sakta — ye regulatory requirement hai.
  • Feature 2 — Real-time prices, history aur charts: user ko real-time stock prices, historical data aur charts (candlestick, line graphs) dekhne milne chahiye.
  • Feature 3 — Bank account link: user apna bank account verify/link kare, taaki fund deposit aur withdrawal ho sake.
  • Feature 4 — Watchlist: user apni watchlist banaye — tracked stocks ki real-time price updates WebSocket push se milti rahein.
  • Feature 5 — Portfolio dashboard: current holdings, P&L (Profit & Loss) aur performance metrics ka dashboard.
  • Feature 6 — Order types: user 3 tarah ke orders de sakta hai — Market order (turant current price par execute), Limit order (sirf specified price ya usse behtar par execute), Stop-loss order (price threshold par pahunchte hi trigger).
  • Feature 7 — Order placement flow: order place karo → available funds ke hisaab se orders limit ho → order status updates milte rahein: PENDING → VERIFIED → PLACED → EXECUTED/REJECTED.
  • Feature 8 — Batch processing: multiple stocks ke liye bulk operations — jaise portfolio rebalancing — ek saath karne ki support.

Diagram me functional requirements ke saath do extra points bhi dikh rahe hain: platform ko commission-free bataya gaya hai, aur orders ko place, modify aur cancel karne ki baat hai — modify/cancel ka detail Section 04 (DELETE order API) aur Step 5 me hai.

02Requirements, Entities & APIs

Non-Functional Requirements

Non-functional requirements batate hain ki system kitne scale par aur kitni tezi se chalega — aur galti ki keemat kya hai. Trading system me galti ka matlab seedha paisa doobna hai, isliye yahan consistency availability se upar hai.

Scale & Performance

  • Scale: high-frequency trades, millions of users, 8,000–10,000 stocks (NSE/BSE combined).
  • Trade volume: peak hours me 100,000+ orders/minute (market volatility ke time), normal time par 10,000 orders/min.
  • Latency: order placement 100ms se kam, market data (updated stock value) ka real-time push 50ms se kam.

Reliability & Consistency

  • Uptime: market hours (subah 9:15 AM – 3:30 PM IST) me 99.99% availability. Lesson me ek khaas baat kahi gayi hai — system downtime, uptime se zyada important hai, matlab market hours me system ka band hona sabse badi failure hai.
  • Wrong balance: duplicate trade ya stale price se huge financial losses ho sakte hain — isliye consistency critical hai.
  • Buy-Sell balance: buy ya sell orders highly available hone chahiye — koi order lost nahi hona chahiye.
Interview-favorite

Diagram me NFR ke paas ek CAP Theorem note hai jo text me nahi hai: Consistency >> Availability — correctness, uptime se zyada important hai. Galat balance, duplicate trade ya stale price se seedha financial loss hota hai, isliye is system me consistency ko availability se upar rakha jata hai. Interview me ye point zaroor bolna — ye batata hai ki tumne trade-off socha hai, sirf numbers ratte nahi hain.

03Requirements, Entities & APIs

Core Entity

Core entities wo 7 tables/objects hain jin par poora system khada hai. Har entity ke fields yaad rakhna — interview me schema design yahin se shuru hota hai.

7 Entities

  • Entity 1 — User: user_id, email, name, phone, kyc_status (PENDING / VERIFIED / REJECTED), pan_card, aadhaar_number, linked_bank_account, created_at.
  • Entity 2 — Stock: stock_symbol (RELIANCE, TCS, INFY), company_name, exchange (NSE/BSE), current_price, day_high, day_low, volume, market_cap, last_updated_at.
  • Entity 3 — Order: order_id, user_id, stock_symbol, order_type (MARKET / LIMIT / STOP_LOSS), trade_type (BUY / SELL), quantity, price (LIMIT ke liye limit price, MARKET ke liye null), status (PENDING / VERIFIED / PLACED / EXECUTED / REJECTED / CANCELLED), placed_at, executed_at, exchange_order_id.
  • Entity 4 — Trade: trade_id, order_id, stock_symbol, quantity, executed_price, executed_at, exchange (NSE/BSE), commission_fee.
  • Entity 5 — Portfolio: user_id, stock_symbol, quantity (owned shares), avg_buy_price, current_value, total_investment, profit_loss (calculated), last_updated_at.
  • Entity 6 — Watchlist: user_id, stock_symbols[] (array — jaise ['RELIANCE', 'TCS', 'INFY']), created_at, updated_at.
  • Entity 7 — Funds: user_id, available_balance (trading ke liye available cash), locked_balance (pending orders me locked funds), total_balance, bank_account_id, last_deposit_at.

Yaad rakhne wali baat: Order aur Trade alag entities hain — Order user ki ichha hai (place hua order), Trade exchange par hui asal execution hai. Ek order se ek trade banta hai (is lesson me partial fill cover nahi hai). Funds me available aur locked balance ka alag-alag hona hi overdraft rokne ki jad hai — detail Step 2 aur Lab 4 me hai.

04Requirements, Entities & APIs

API Designing

API design 5 groups me banta hai — auth, market data, trading, portfolio & funds, watchlist & batch. Har endpoint ke saath uska kaam aur important rules diye gaye hain.

Users & Authentication

  • POST /auth/signup — email, phone, password se user registration.
  • POST /auth/login — credentials se login, JWT token return karta hai.
  • POST /auth/verify-kyc — KYC documents (PAN, Aadhaar) submit karo, verification workflow trigger hota hai.

Market Data

  • GET /api/v1/stocks — saare stocks ki list (paginated), current prices, volume, change% ke saath.
  • GET /api/v1/stocks/{symbol} — ek stock ki detail (OHLC, volume, market cap, historical data).
  • GET /api/v1/stocks/{symbol}/history — charts ke liye historical price data (daily / weekly / monthly candles).

Trading

  • POST /api/v1/orders — buy/sell order place karo: {stockSymbol, orderType, tradeType, quantity, price (LIMIT ke liye)}.
  • GET /api/v1/orders — user ke orders ki list, status ke saath; date / status / stock se filter.
  • DELETE /api/v1/orders/{orderId} — pending order cancel karo — sirf tab jab status PENDING ya PLACED ho, EXECUTED order cancel nahi hota.

Portfolio & Funds

  • GET /api/v1/portfolio — holdings, current value, P&L, performance.
  • GET /api/v1/portfolio/performance — performance metrics (daily/monthly returns, total P&L).
  • POST /api/v1/funds/deposit — linked bank account se fund deposit shuru karo.
  • POST /api/v1/funds/withdraw — bank account me withdrawal — sirf available_balance se, locked balance se nahi.
  • GET /api/v1/funds/history — fund transactions ki history (deposits, withdrawals, order locks).

Watchlist & Batch

  • GET /api/v1/watchlist — watchlist, tracked stocks ki real-time prices ke saath.
  • POST /api/v1/watchlist — watchlist me stock jodo {stockSymbol: 'RELIANCE'}.
  • DELETE /api/v1/watchlist/{stockSymbol} — watchlist se stock hatao.
  • POST /api/v1/batch/validate/{stockId}/symbols — multiple stock symbols ko ek saath validate karo (bulk operations).

Diagram se ek note: diagram ke API hisse me kuch endpoints thode alag roop me likhe hain — jaise WS /api/v1/stocks/{symbol} (stock details ke liye WebSocket), GET /api/v1/portfolio/holdings aur GET /api/v1/portfolio/positions, aur watchlist ke liye /api/v1/watchlists (plural) aur POST /api/v1/watchlists/{watchlistId}/symbols. Is SPA ke upar wale endpoints lesson text wale hain; diagram wale variants yahan isliye note kiye gaye hain taaki diagram dekh kar confusion na ho — matlab ek hi hai, naam thoda alag hai.

05Requirements, Entities & APIs

High Level Design

High Level Design me poora architecture ek line me: users/client → LB/API Gateway (Authentication, Authorization, Routing, Rate limiting, Round-robin) → Services → Databases. Har service ka ek database aur ek clear kaam hai.

Services aur unke stores

  • User Svc → UserDB: registration, login, KYC verification (Aadhaar/PAN validation), session management.
  • Price Tracker Svc → InfluxDB (time-series DB): real-time stock prices aur historical OHLC data store karta hai; Exchange data feed ko subscribe karta hai.
  • WatchList Svc → Watch DB: user ke tracked stocks; price badalte hi WebSocket update trigger karta hai.
  • Order Svc → Order DB + Kafka: orders place karta hai, Kafka 'new_orders' topic par publish karta hai, user input validate karta hai, order status track karta hai.
  • Portfolio Svc → Trade DB: holdings, P&L aur average buy price calculate karta hai; trade execution par update karta hai; PnL calculation logic yahin hai.
  • Payment Svc → Payment DB: Payment Gateway (Razorpay, Stripe) se fund deposits/withdrawals, bank account linking.
  • Validator: orders ko Exchange bhejne se pehle validate karta hai — sufficient funds, risk checks, duplicate order prevention.
  • Exchange Gateway: NSE/BSE se FIX protocol par baat karta hai — orders bhejta hai, execution confirmations leta hai; ek connection par 400–500 symbols/legs handle karta hai.
  • Exchange (NSE/BSE): external matching engine — trades execute karta hai, order_status updates Kafka se wapas bhejta hai.

Kafka — event backbone

  • new_orders — order placement
  • verified_orders — validation ke baad ke orders
  • order_status — execution updates
  • stock_price — real-time price feed

Real-time path

  • WebSocket Gateway: clients ko real-time price updates push karta hai (watchlist, portfolio); Redis PubSub ke stock_price channel ko subscribe karta hai.
  • InfluxDB: time-series DB stock prices ke liye — high write throughput, charts ke liye efficient range queries.
  • Redis PubSub: real-time price updates — publish: stock_price updates, subscribe: WebSocket servers, low latency 10ms se kam.

Diagram ke LLD hisse me do cheezein aur dikh rahi hain jo yahan bhi yaad rakhni chahiye: Price Ingester Svc (Exchange se live updates lekar InfluxDB aur Kafka me daalta hai) aur WebSocket Gateway ki stickiness (user hamesha same server se juda rehta hai, taaki uska connection state bana rahe). In dono ka detail Step 1 aur Section 08 me hai.

06.1Deep Dive (LLD)

Step 1: Real-Time Price Updates

Real-time price ka raasta: Exchange → Price Tracker Svc → InfluxDB (persist) + Redis PubSub (fan-out) → WebSocket servers (subscribe) → Clients (selective push). Total latency ~20ms — 50ms target se bahut neeche.

Step-by-step

  • Exchange data feed: Price Tracker Svc NSE/BSE ke real-time price stream ko subscribe karta hai (WebSocket/FIX protocol). Tick-by-tick updates milti hain 8,000–10,000 stocks ke liye. Example update: {symbol: 'RELIANCE', price: 2458.75, volume: 125000, timestamp: '2026-01-26T10:30:45.123Z'}.
  • InfluxDB me write (time-series): INSERT stock_prices measurement me: {symbol: 'RELIANCE', price: 2458.75, volume: 125000, day_high: 2465.00, day_low: 2450.00, timestamp}. InfluxDB time-series ke liye optimized hai — high write throughput (100,000+ writes/sec), charts ke liye efficient range queries (1D, 1W, 1M, 1Y historical data), automatic downsampling (1-min candles hamesha ke liye, 5-min / 1-hour / daily me aggregate).
  • Redis PubSub me publish: PUBLISH stock_price '{"symbol": "RELIANCE", "price": 2458.75, "change": +0.45%}'. Channel: stock_price (saare stock updates ke liye ek global channel). Low latency: Exchange → Redis PubSub → WebSocket clients, 10ms se kam.
  • WebSocket Gateway subscribe karta hai: 10+ WebSocket server instances Redis PubSub ko SUBSCRIBE stock_price karte hain. Message milte hi parse karo aur us stock ko dekhne wale connected clients ko broadcast karo — RELIANCE dekhne wale clients ko {symbol: 'RELIANCE', price: 2458.75, change: +0.45%} turant milta hai.
  • Client connection: user app kholta hai → WebSocket connection banta hai: ws://api.trading.com/prices. Client apni watchlist bhejta hai: {action: 'subscribe', symbols: ['RELIANCE', 'TCS', 'INFY']}. Server sirf yehi stocks ke price changes bhejta hai — saare 8,000–10,000 stocks ke nahi. Har client ko sirf watched stocks milte hain, isse bandwidth 1000× kam ho jati hai.
Interview-favorite

Latency breakdown yaad rakho — Exchange → Price Tracker: 5ms (network), Price Tracker → Redis PubSub: 2ms (PUBLISH command), Redis PubSub → WebSocket server: 3ms (in-memory fan-out), WebSocket server → Client: 10ms (internet latency). Total: ~20ms — 50ms target ke andar. Interview me ye breakdown bolna sabse strong point hai.

Tip

InfluxDB time-series ke liye hai: 100,000+ writes/sec, automatic downsampling (1-min candles → 5-min → hourly → daily, continuous queries se), charts ke liye 1 saal ka data 100ms se kam me. Time-series data ke liye SQL DB use mat karo — ye interview me aksar poocha jata hai.

Scaling aur fallback: horizontal scaling — aur WebSocket servers jodo (har ek 10,000 connections handle karta hai); load balancer sticky sessions se user ko hamesha same server par bhejta hai (user_id hash → same server), taaki WebSocket state bana rahe; Redis PubSub single channel hai, saare servers subscribe karte hain (fan-out), koi coordination nahi chahiye. Agar WebSocket disconnect ho jaye to client exponential backoff se auto-reconnect karta hai (1s, 2s, 4s, max 30s); reconnect par watchlist dobara subscribe karo aur chhoote hue updates REST API se lo (GET /api/v1/stocks/{symbol}).

06.2Deep Dive (LLD)

Step 2: Order Placement Flow

Market order ka matlab: turant, best available price par execute karo. Is step me Order Svc validation, fund check, fund locking, order insert aur Kafka publish — sab kuch hai. Worked example: 10 RELIANCE shares.

1. User MARKET BUY order deta hai

POST /api/v1/orders with {stockSymbol: 'RELIANCE', orderType: 'MARKET', tradeType: 'BUY', quantity: 10}. MARKET order current market price (best available price) par turant execute hota hai.

2. Order Svc validation — 5 checks

  • JWT token valid hai ya nahi (user authenticated hai ya nahi).
  • Stock symbol valid hai ya nahi — stock_symbols table query karo; nahi mila to 400 'Invalid stock symbol'.
  • Market hours check — agar current time subah 9:15 se pehle ya dopahar 3:30 ke baad hai to 400 'Market closed'.
  • Quantity positive integer honi chahiye.
  • order_id generate karo (UUID).

3. Available funds check — worked example

  • Funds table query: SELECT available_balance FROM funds WHERE user_id={user_id}.
  • Current price Redis cache se: GET stock_price:RELIANCE → 2458.75.
  • Estimated cost: quantity × price = 10 × 2458.75 = ₹24,587.50.
  • Commission: ₹20 (flat fee). Total required: ₹24,607.50.
  • Agar available_balance ₹24,607.50 se kam hai to 400 'Insufficient funds'.

4. Funds lock karo — ek transaction me

BEGIN TRANSACTION; UPDATE funds SET available_balance = available_balance - 24607.50, locked_balance = locked_balance + 24607.50 WHERE user_id={user_id}; COMMIT; — isse locked funds kisi doosre order me use nahi ho sakte (overdraft prevent hota hai).

5. Order insert + Kafka publish

INSERT INTO orders (order_id, user_id, stock_symbol: 'RELIANCE', order_type: 'MARKET', trade_type: 'BUY', quantity: 10, price: null, status: 'PENDING', placed_at: now()). Phir Kafka.send('new_orders', {orderId, userId, stockSymbol: 'RELIANCE', orderType: 'MARKET', tradeType: 'BUY', quantity: 10}). User ko response: 200 OK {orderId: 'ORD123', status: 'PENDING', message: 'Order placed successfully'}.

Critical

Fund locking ke liye SELECT FOR UPDATE mandatory hai — overdraft rokne ke liye. Balance check aur update ko kabhi alag-alag queries me mat karo (race condition ban jayegi). Ek hi transaction me pessimistic lock lo. Fund updates ke liye isolation level SERIALIZABLE rakho. Duplicate trade ya stale price = huge financial losses.

06.3Deep Dive (LLD)

Step 3: Validation & Exchange Submission

Order Kafka me jaane ke baad Validator use check karta hai, phir Exchange Gateway FIX protocol se NSE/BSE ko bhejta hai. Validation pipeline 4 stage ki hai aur total 50ms se kam me poori hoti hai.

1. Validator 'new_orders' consume karta hai

Validator service (5 instances, 50 partitions se consume karti hai). Order milta hai: {orderId: 'ORD123', ...}.

2. Validation checks — 4 stage

  • Duplicate check: SELECT COUNT(*) FROM orders WHERE user_id={user_id} AND stock_symbol='RELIANCE' AND status IN ('PENDING', 'PLACED') AND placed_at > NOW() - INTERVAL '5 seconds' — agar count > 1 hai to duplicate samjho (user ne double-click karke submit kar diya) aur reject karo. (Production note: duplicate check ke liye Redis SETNX bhi use hota hai — detail Section 08, Technique 8 me hai.)
  • Risk management: user ki total exposure check karo: SELECT SUM(quantity × price) FROM orders WHERE user_id={user_id} AND status IN ('PENDING', 'PLACED') — agar exposure 10× available_balance se zyada hai to reject karo (excessive risk rokne ke liye).
  • Circuit breaker: agar stock price pichhle 5 minute me 10% se zyada move kar gaya hai to reject karo (market volatility protection).
  • Compliance: check karo user is stock me trade karne ke liye blocked/restricted to nahi hai.

3. Validation pass hone par

UPDATE orders SET status='VERIFIED' WHERE order_id='ORD123', phir Kafka 'verified_orders' par publish karo: {orderId: 'ORD123', ...}.

4. Exchange Gateway 'verified_orders' consume karta hai

Exchange Gateway Kafka se verified order leta hai aur use FIX protocol (Financial Information eXchange) me format karta hai: FIX message — 35=D (NewOrderSingle), 55=RELIANCE (Symbol), 54=1 (Buy), 38=10 (Quantity), 40=1 (Market order). Ye message TCP se NSE/BSE matching engine ko bheja jata hai.

5. Order PLACED, matching engine kaam karta hai

UPDATE orders SET status='PLACED', exchange_order_id={exchange_assigned_id} WHERE order_id='ORD123'. NSE/BSE matching engine BUY order ko available SELL orders se match karta hai — execution best available price par hoti hai (jaise ₹2459.00, cached price ₹2458.75 se thoda zyada), aur execution confirmation wapas Exchange Gateway ko bhejta hai.

Interview-favorite

Order validation pipeline: (1) Duplicate check (Redis SETNX), (2) Fund check (SELECT FOR UPDATE), (3) Risk check (total exposure limit), (4) Circuit breaker (price volatility >10% in 5min). Multi-stage pipeline galat orders ko Exchange tak pahunchne se pehle rok deti hai. Total validation 50ms se kam me hoti hai.

Tip

State transitions yaad rakho: PENDING (order bana, funds locked) → VERIFIED (validation pass) → PLACED (Exchange ko bhej diya) → EXECUTED (trade confirm). Status orders table + Kafka 'order_status' topic dono me track hota hai — isse user visibility, retry logic aur audit trail teeno milte hain.

Interview-favorite

Exchange communication: FIX protocol (Financial Information eXchange) via TCP. 400–500 persistent connections maintain karo (connection pool per symbol group). Order format: Tag 35=D (NewOrderSingle), Tag 55=symbol, Tag 54=side (1=Buy, 2=Sell), Tag 38=quantity, Tag 40=type (1=Market, 2=Limit). ExecutionReport milta hai: Tag 35=8.

06.4Deep Dive (LLD)

Step 4: Trade Execution & Status Updates

Execution confirmation aate hi 3 services ek saath kaam karti hain — Order Tracker (order + trade record), Funds (unlock aur actual cost deduct), Portfolio Svc (holdings update). Worked example wahi 10 RELIANCE shares ka hai.

1. Exchange execution confirmation bhejta hai

FIX message: 35=8 (ExecutionReport), 150=2 (Filled). Execution details: {orderId: exchange_order_id, executedPrice: 2459.00, executedQuantity: 10, executedAt: timestamp}.

2. Exchange Gateway Kafka par publish karta hai

Kafka.send('order_status', {orderId: 'ORD123', status: 'EXECUTED', executedPrice: 2459.00, executedQuantity: 10, executedAt: timestamp}). Topic order_status ko Order Tracker Svc, Notification Svc aur Portfolio Svc consume karte hain.

3. Order Tracker Svc order update karta hai

UPDATE orders SET status='EXECUTED', executed_at=now() WHERE order_id='ORD123'. Phir trades table me insert: INSERT INTO trades (trade_id, order_id, stock_symbol: 'RELIANCE', quantity: 10, executed_price: 2459.00, executed_at: now(), exchange: 'NSE', commission_fee: 20).

4. Funds update — unlock aur actual cost deduct

  • Actual cost: 10 × 2459.00 = ₹24,590 (estimated ₹24,587.50 se thoda zyada — price movement ki wajah se).
  • Total debit: ₹24,590 + ₹20 commission = ₹24,610.
  • Transaction: BEGIN TRANSACTION; UPDATE funds SET locked_balance = locked_balance - 24607.50 (estimated unlock), available_balance = available_balance - (24610 - 24607.50) = -2.50 (difference deduct); COMMIT;
  • Final: user ka cash balance ₹24,610 se kam ho gaya.

5. Portfolio Svc holdings update karta hai

Kafka 'order_status' consume karo. Check karo user ke paas stock hai ya nahi: SELECT quantity, avg_buy_price FROM portfolio WHERE user_id={user_id} AND stock_symbol='RELIANCE'. Agar holding pehle se hai to: UPDATE portfolio SET quantity = quantity + 10, avg_buy_price = ((prev_quantity × prev_avg_price) + (10 × 2459.00)) / (prev_quantity + 10). Agar pehli purchase hai to: INSERT INTO portfolio (user_id, stock_symbol: 'RELIANCE', quantity: 10, avg_buy_price: 2459.00, total_investment: 24590, last_updated_at: now()).

  • Current value: 10 × 2460.00 = ₹24,600.
  • Profit/loss: 24600 − 24590 = +₹10 (unrealized gain).
  • UPDATE portfolio SET current_value=24600, profit_loss=10.

Step 5: Limit Order Flow

Limit order ka matlab: sirf tab execute karo jab market price tumhare bataye hue price tak pahunche (SELL ke liye us price ya usse upar, BUY ke liye us price ya usse neeche). Worked example: 5 TCS shares @ ₹3500 SELL.

POST /api/v1/orders with {stockSymbol: 'TCS', orderType: 'LIMIT', tradeType: 'SELL', quantity: 5, price: 3500.00}.

2. Order Svc validation — 3 checks

  • Holdings check: SELECT quantity FROM portfolio WHERE user_id={user_id} AND stock_symbol='TCS' — agar quantity 5 se kam hai to 400 'Insufficient holdings'.
  • Limit price validation: limit price current market price ke ±10% ke andar hona chahiye — taaki galti se SELL @ ₹1 ya ₹100,000 jaise orders na chale jayein.
  • Shares lock karo: UPDATE portfolio SET quantity = quantity - 5, locked_quantity = locked_quantity + 5 — taaki wahi shares do baar sell na ho jayein.

INSERT INTO orders (order_id, user_id, stock_symbol: 'TCS', order_type: 'LIMIT', trade_type: 'SELL', quantity: 5, price: 3500.00, status: 'PENDING', placed_at: now()). 4. Exchange par kya hota hai

  • LIMIT order order book me pada rehta hai jab tak price condition poori na ho.
  • Agar TCS price ₹3500.00 tak pahunch jaye to order us price par BUY order se match ho jata hai.
  • Agar price kabhi ₹3500 tak na pahunche to order khula rehta hai (cancel karne ya market close hone tak).
  • Din ke ant me: execute nahi hua to order cancel ho jata hai (day order) — ya khula rehta hai agar GTC (Good Till Cancelled) specify kiya ho.

5. Execution (jab price ₹3500 hit kare)

  • Exchange 'EXECUTED' status bhejta hai → Kafka 'order_status'.
  • Order Tracker Svc order status ko 'EXECUTED' update karta hai.
  • Portfolio Svc: UPDATE portfolio SET locked_quantity = locked_quantity - 5 (shares unlock — available quantity se pehle hi hata diye gaye the).

Step 7: Portfolio Performance (PnL)

Portfolio dekhte hi P&L real-time calculate hota hai — current price Redis se lekar. P&L ko DB me store karke rakhna galat hai, kyunki price har second badalta hai.

Worked example — ek holding

  • User portfolio dekhta hai: GET /api/v1/portfolio.
  • Har holding ke liye current price Redis se: GET stock_price:RELIANCE → 2460.00.
  • Holding: {stock_symbol: 'RELIANCE', quantity: 10, avg_buy_price: 2459.00, total_investment: 24590}.
  • Current value: 10 × 2460.00 = ₹24,600.
  • Unrealized P&L: 24600 − 24590 = +₹10 (+0.04%).
  • Day change: (current_price − prev_close) / prev_close × 100 = (2460 − 2455) / 2455 × 100 = +0.20%.

Aggregate portfolio — worked example

  • Total investment: SUM(total_investment for all holdings) = ₹2,45,000.
  • Current value: SUM(current_value for all holdings) = ₹2,48,500.
  • Total P&L: 2,48,500 − 2,45,000 = +₹3,500 (+1.43%).
  • Today's change: saare holdings ka day change, current value se weighted.

Response format

{holdings: [{stockSymbol: 'RELIANCE', quantity: 10, avgBuyPrice: 2459.00, currentPrice: 2460.00, currentValue: 24600, profitLoss: +10, profitLossPercent: +0.04%, dayChange: +0.20%}, ...], totalInvestment: 245000, currentValue: 248500, totalProfitLoss: +3500, totalProfitLossPercent: +1.43%}

Critical

P&L ko kabhi calculate karke DB me store mat karo. Current value = quantity × current_price (Redis se). Jab user portfolio dekhe, tabhi on-the-fly calculate karo. Store karne se stale data ka risk hai (price har second badalta hai). Sirf ye store karo: quantity, avg_buy_price, total_investment — ye trade hone tak static rehte hain.

Scaling & Optimization

Scaling ki 12 techniques — har ek ek alag problem solve karti hai: real-time fanout, time-series writes, async order flow, overdraft, sharding, read replicas, connection pooling, validation, auto-scaling, caching, rate limiting aur async notifications.

Technique 1 — Redis PubSub for real-time prices

Price updates stock_price channel par publish hoti hain (10ms se kam latency), WebSocket servers subscribe karke clients ko push karte hain. Polling nahi hoti (100,000+ concurrent connections), horizontally scale hota hai (aur WebSocket servers jodo).

Technique 2 — InfluxDB time-series DB

Stock prices ke liye optimized (100,000+ writes/sec), automatic downsampling (1-min candles → 5-min → hourly → daily), charts ke liye efficient range queries (1 saal ka data 100ms se kam me), retention policies (1-min data forever, aggregates auto-calculated).

Technique 3 — Kafka event-driven architecture

Order flow decoupled hai (Order Svc → Validator → Exchange Gateway → Order Tracker), async processing (user ko turant 200 OK milta hai), horizontal scaling (aur consumers jodo), order processing guaranteed (at-least-once delivery).

Technique 4 — Fund locking with transactions

Overdraft rokne ke liye: order placement par funds lock karo (ek hi transaction me available → locked), execution/cancellation par unlock karo, ACID guarantees (koi race condition nahi), isolation level SERIALIZABLE fund updates ke liye.

Technique 5 — Database sharding

Orders table user_id se sharded hai (user ke saare orders ek shard par, fast queries), Portfolio bhi user_id se sharded hai (user ki holdings ek jagah). 10 shards: har ek 100,000 users (total 1M users). Cross-shard sirf admin queries ke liye (acceptable slow).

Technique 6 — Read replicas for portfolio/watchlist

1 master (writes: order execution, fund updates) + 5 read replicas (reads: portfolio view, watchlist, order history). Read/write split: Portfolio GET → replicas (95% traffic), trade execution → master. Lag 1 sec se kam (portfolio view ke liye acceptable).

Technique 7 — Exchange Gateway connection pooling

NSE/BSE se 400–500 persistent FIX connections maintain karo. Connection per symbol group (RELIANCE, TCS, INFY ek hi connection par). Connections reuse karo (handshake overhead bache), load balancing: orders ko connections par distribute karo.

Technique 8 — Order validation pipeline

Multi-stage: (1) Duplicate check (Redis SET orderId EX 5, SETNX false return kare to duplicate), (2) Fund check (Funds table query), (3) Risk check (total exposure limit), (4) Circuit breaker (price volatility). Jahan possible ho parallel checks — total validation 50ms se kam.

Technique 9 — WebSocket gateway auto-scaling

Connections ke hisaab se auto-scale: agar ek server par connections 10,000 se zyada ho jayein to instance jodo. Sticky sessions: user hamesha same server se judta hai (WebSocket state bana rehta hai). Load balancer user_id hash se route karta hai (consistent routing).

Technique 10 — Price caching strategy

Redis cache: current price (TTL 1 sec, InfluxDB/PubSub se refresh), historical data: popular queries cache karo (RELIANCE 1D chart → 5 min TTL), cache warming: server start par top 100 stocks pre-load karo. Hit rate: current prices ke liye 99% (saare trades cached price use karte hain).

Technique 11 — Rate limiting

Per user: 10 orders/sec (fat-finger errors aur accidental rapid clicks rokne ke liye). Per API endpoint: 100 req/sec per user (portfolio, watchlist). Global: 100,000 orders/min (exceed ho to circuit breaker, system protection).

Technique 12 — Async notification with queues

Notification Svc Kafka 'order_status' ko async consume karta hai. User ko trade confirmation 1 sec ke andar milti hai (push notification). Email/SMS baad ke liye queue me jate hain (non-blocking). Failed notifications exponential backoff se retry hoti hain (3 attempts ke baad DLQ).

Common Interview Questions

Lesson ke 3 sabse bade interview questions — poore detail ke saath. Ye answers ratne ke liye nahi, samajhne ke liye hain: har ek me numbers, worked examples aur trade-offs hain.

Q1. Real-time price updates millions of users tak 50ms se kam latency me kaise pahunchte hain?

Answer: Real-time price distribution — WebSocket + Redis PubSub:

  • (1) Price ingestion: Price Tracker Svc NSE/BSE data feed ko subscribe karta hai (WebSocket/FIX protocol), 8,000–10,000 stocks ke tick-by-tick updates milte hain. Example: {symbol: 'RELIANCE', price: 2458.75, volume: 125000, timestamp}.
  • (2) Dual write: InfluxDB me write (time-series persistence): INSERT stock_prices (symbol='RELIANCE', price=2458.75, volume=125000, timestamp) — async write, high throughput (100,000+ writes/sec), historical charts ke liye use hota hai, real-time push ke liye nahi. Redis PubSub par publish: PUBLISH stock_price '{"symbol": "RELIANCE", "price": 2458.75, "change": +0.45%}' — saare subscribers ko turant fan-out (10ms se kam), koi persistence nahi (in-memory pub/sub).
  • (3) WebSocket servers subscribe karte hain: 10 WebSocket gateway servers (horizontally scaled), har server SUBSCRIBE stock_price karta hai (Redis PubSub channel), real-time me saare price updates milte hain, filter karke connected clients ko push karta hai.
  • (4) Client connections: user app kholta hai → WebSocket connection: ws://api.trading.com/prices. Handshake: client apni watchlist bhejta hai: {action: 'subscribe', symbols: ['RELIANCE', 'TCS', 'INFY']}. Server subscription store karta hai: userConnections[userId] = {socket, symbols: ['RELIANCE', 'TCS', 'INFY']}.
  • (5) Selective push: Redis PubSub message aaya: {symbol: 'RELIANCE', price: 2458.75}. Server connected users ko iterate karta hai — jo user 'RELIANCE' dekh raha hai, use WebSocket se bhejta hai: socket.send({symbol: 'RELIANCE', price: 2458.75}). Sirf RELIANCE dekhne wale users ko update milta hai (saare 8,000–10,000 stocks ke updates nahi) — bandwidth 1000× kam (har user ~10 stocks dekhta hai, saare 8,000–10,000 nahi).
  • (6) Latency breakdown: Exchange → Price Tracker: 5ms (network), Price Tracker → Redis PubSub: 2ms (PUBLISH command), Redis PubSub → WebSocket server: 3ms (in-memory fan-out), WebSocket server → Client: 10ms (internet latency). Total: ~20ms (50ms target se bahut neeche).
  • (7) Scaling: horizontal — aur WebSocket servers jodo (har ek 10,000 connections handle karta hai); load balancer sticky sessions (user_id hash → same server), WebSocket state bana rehta hai; Redis PubSub single channel hai, saare servers subscribe karte hain (fan-out), koi coordination nahi chahiye.
  • (8) Fallback: agar WebSocket disconnect ho jaye to client exponential backoff se auto-reconnect karta hai (1s, 2s, 4s, max 30s); reconnect par watchlist dobara subscribe karo aur chhoote hue updates REST API se lo (GET /api/v1/stocks/{symbol}).

Result: sub-50ms real-time updates, millions of concurrent users tak scale hota hai, efficient bandwidth usage (selective push), resilient (failure par auto-reconnect).

Q2. Multiple orders ek saath place ho to overdraft kaise rokte hain?

Answer: Fund locking — database transactions aur pessimistic locking:

  • (1) Problem: user ke paas ₹50,000 available balance hai. 2 simultaneous MARKET BUY orders deta hai — Order A: 20 RELIANCE @ ₹2459 = ₹49,180; Order B: 5 TCS @ ₹3500 = ₹17,500. Total required: ₹49,180 + ₹17,500 = ₹66,680 > ₹50,000 (overdraft!). Bina locking ke: dono orders balance check karte hain (₹50,000 > ₹49,180 ✓, ₹50,000 > ₹17,500 ✓), dono pass ho jate hain, dono execute ho jate hain → ₹16,680 ka overdraft.
  • (2) Solution — pessimistic locking with SELECT FOR UPDATE: Order A processing: BEGIN TRANSACTION; SELECT available_balance FROM funds WHERE user_id={user_id} FOR UPDATE (row lock ho jati hai, doosri transactions wait karti hain). available_balance = ₹50,000. Check: ₹50,000 >= ₹49,180 ✓ (pass). UPDATE funds SET available_balance = 50000 - 49180 = ₹820, locked_balance = 0 + 49180 = ₹49,180; COMMIT; (lock release).
  • (3) Order B processing (simultaneously): BEGIN TRANSACTION; SELECT available_balance FROM funds WHERE user_id={user_id} FOR UPDATE — Order A ke commit hone tak wait karta hai (row locked hai). Order A commit karte hi padhta hai available_balance = ₹820 (updated value). Check: ₹820 >= ₹17,500 ✗ (fail). ROLLBACK, error return: 400 'Insufficient funds'.
  • (4) Transaction isolation: isolation level SERIALIZABLE (sabse high level). Rokta hai: dirty reads (uncommitted data padhna), non-repeatable reads (transaction ke beech me data badalna), phantom reads (naye rows aa jana). Guarantee: har transaction ko consistent snapshot milta hai.
  • (5) Alternative — optimistic locking with version: funds table me version column hota hai (1 se shuru). Order A: SELECT available_balance, version FROM funds WHERE user_id={user_id} — padha: balance=₹50,000, version=1. UPDATE funds SET available_balance=820, locked_balance=49180, version=2 WHERE user_id={user_id} AND version=1. Agar version abhi bhi 1 hai to update succeed hota hai (1 row affected); agar version badal gaya hai (Order B ne pehle update kar diya) to update fail hota hai (0 rows affected) — naye version se retry karo.
  • (6) Optimistic vs Pessimistic: Pessimistic (SELECT FOR UPDATE) — jab contention high ho (bahut users ek saath trade kar rahe hon), concurrent updates block karta hai (serialize), correctness ki guarantee throughput ki keemat par. Optimistic (version check) — jab contention low ho (ek hi user ke simultaneous orders rare hon), concurrent reads allow karta hai, conflict par retry, zyada throughput lekin retries se latency badhti hai.
  • (7) Production choice: fund updates ke liye pessimistic locking (correctness critical hai, overdraft nahi ho sakta); portfolio updates ke liye optimistic locking (retries tolerate kar sakta hai, kam critical hai).

Result: overdraft possible hi nahi hai, ACID guarantees bane rehte hain, concurrent orders sahi handle hote hain — trade-off: correctness ke liye thodi latency badhti hai (financial transactions ke liye acceptable).

Q3. Complete order flow — placement se execution tak, saari validations aur state transitions ke saath

Answer: Complete order flow (MARKET BUY order):

  • (1) User order deta hai: POST /api/v1/orders {stockSymbol: 'RELIANCE', orderType: 'MARKET', tradeType: 'BUY', quantity: 10}.
  • (2) Order Svc validation: JWT token valid check karo (user authenticated); stock_symbol validate karo: SELECT EXISTS FROM stocks WHERE stock_symbol='RELIANCE' — nahi mila to 400 'Invalid stock'; market hours check karo — current time 9:15 AM–3:30 PM IST ke beech me nahi hai to 400 'Market closed'; quantity positive int honi chahiye, lot size ka multiple (equity ke liye 1); order_id generate karo (UUID).
  • (3) Fund check: current price Redis se: GET stock_price:RELIANCE → ₹2458.75. Estimated cost: 10 × 2458.75 + ₹20 commission = ₹24,607.50. Fund locking: BEGIN TRANSACTION; SELECT available_balance FROM funds WHERE user_id={user_id} FOR UPDATE — agar available_balance 24607.50 se kam hai to ROLLBACK, 400 'Insufficient funds' return karo; UPDATE funds SET available_balance -= 24607.50, locked_balance += 24607.50; COMMIT;
  • (4) Order insert: INSERT INTO orders (order_id, user_id, stock_symbol, order_type: 'MARKET', trade_type: 'BUY', quantity: 10, price: null, status: 'PENDING', placed_at: now()). Status: PENDING (initial state).
  • (5) Kafka publish: Kafka.send('new_orders', {orderId, userId, stockSymbol, orderType, tradeType, quantity}). User ko response: 200 OK {orderId: 'ORD123', status: 'PENDING'}.
  • (6) Validator 'new_orders' consume karta hai: duplicate check — Redis SETNX order_lock:{orderId} 1 EX 30, agar pehle se hai (SETNX returns 0) to duplicate, reject karo. Risk check — total exposure: SELECT SUM(quantity × price) FROM orders WHERE user_id={user_id} AND status IN ('PENDING','PLACED'), agar exposure 10× available_balance se zyada hai to reject karo (excessive risk). Circuit breaker — SELECT price FROM stock_prices WHERE symbol='RELIANCE' AND timestamp > NOW() - 5 MIN, agar 5 min me price volatility 10% se zyada hai to reject karo (market volatility). Sab pass ho to: UPDATE orders SET status='VERIFIED', Kafka 'verified_orders' par publish karo: {orderId}.
  • (7) Exchange Gateway 'verified_orders' consume karta hai: FIX message format karo — Tag 35=D (NewOrderSingle), Tag 55='RELIANCE' (Symbol), Tag 54=1 (Side: Buy), Tag 38=10 (Quantity), Tag 40=1 (OrdType: Market). TCP se NSE matching engine ko bhejo. UPDATE orders SET status='PLACED', exchange_order_id={assigned_by_exchange}.
  • (8) NSE matching engine: BUY order ko best available SELL order(s) se match karta hai. Execution price: ₹2459.00 (execution time ka market price, cached ₹2458.75 se alag ho sakta hai). FIX ExecutionReport bhejta hai: Tag 35=8, Tag 150=2 (ExecType: Filled), details: {executedPrice: 2459.00, executedQuantity: 10}.
  • (9) Exchange Gateway execution leta hai: Kafka 'order_status' par publish karo: {orderId, status: 'EXECUTED', executedPrice: 2459.00, executedQuantity: 10, executedAt: timestamp}.
  • (10) Order Tracker Svc 'order_status' consume karta hai: UPDATE orders SET status='EXECUTED', executed_at=now(). INSERT INTO trades (trade_id, order_id, stock_symbol, quantity: 10, executed_price: 2459.00, executed_at: now(), exchange: 'NSE', commission_fee: 20).
  • (11) Fund update: actual cost: 10 × 2459 + 20 = ₹24,610 (estimated ₹24,607.50 se zyada). BEGIN TRANSACTION; UPDATE funds SET locked_balance -= 24607.50 (estimated unlock), available_balance -= (24610 - 24607.50) = -2.50 (difference deduct); COMMIT;
  • (12) Portfolio Svc 'order_status' consume karta hai: existing holding check karo: SELECT quantity, avg_buy_price FROM portfolio WHERE user_id={user_id} AND stock_symbol='RELIANCE'. Nayi holding ho to: INSERT INTO portfolio (user_id, stock_symbol, quantity: 10, avg_buy_price: 2459.00, total_investment: 24590). Existing ho to: UPDATE portfolio SET quantity += 10, avg_buy_price = (prev_qty × prev_avg + 10 × 2459) / (prev_qty + 10), total_investment = quantity × avg_buy_price.
  • (13) Notification Svc 'order_status' consume karta hai: FCM se push notification: {title: 'Order Executed', body: 'RELIANCE BUY 10 @ ₹2459.00'}. WebSocket push (agar user online hai): {type: 'ORDER_STATUS', orderId, status: 'EXECUTED'}.

State transitions: PENDING (order bana, funds locked) → VERIFIED (validations pass) → PLACED (Exchange ko bhej diya) → EXECUTED (trade confirm) — ya REJECTED (validation fail, funds unlock). Result: end-to-end order flow with validations; fund locking overdraft rokta hai; state tracking se user visibility milti hai; Kafka stages ko decouple karke scalability deta hai.

Interview FAQs

Lesson se aur usse jude topics par 36 interview FAQs — har jawaab me numbers, examples aur trade-offs hain. Lesson ke 3 sabse bade questions poore detail ke saath Section 09 me hain; yahan unse jude aur aage ke sawaal hain.

Q01 Market order, Limit order aur Stop-loss order me kya farak hai?
Market order turant best available price par execute hota hai — price ki guarantee nahi, execution ki guarantee hai. Limit order sirf tumhare bataye hue price ya usse behtar par execute hota hai — price ki guarantee hai, execution ki nahi. Stop-loss order tab trigger hota hai jab price tumhare threshold tak pahunche, phir ye market order ban jata hai — ye loss ko limit karne ke liye use hota hai. Interview me teeno ka ek example dekar samjhana sabse strong rehta hai.
Q02 Order placement latency 100ms se kam kaise achieve hoti hai?
Order placement ka critical path chhota rakha jata hai: Order Svc sirf input validation, fund check (Redis cached price se) aur fund locking karta hai, phir Kafka par publish karke turant 200 OK return kar deta hai. Bhaari kaam — Validator checks, Exchange Gateway, execution — async Kafka consumers karte hain. Isse user ko turant response milta hai aur latency 100ms se kam rehti hai. Lab 1 me cost calculation aur Lab 8 me state machine se ye flow samajh aayega.
Q03 Market data ka real-time push 50ms se kam kaise hota hai?
Dual-write pattern: Price Tracker Svc Exchange se tick lete hi do kaam karta hai — InfluxDB me persist (async, charts ke liye) aur Redis PubSub par publish (real-time fan-out ke liye). WebSocket servers Redis channel ko subscribe karte hain aur sirf watched stocks ke updates selective push karte hain. Latency breakdown: Exchange → Price Tracker 5ms, → Redis PubSub 2ms, → WebSocket server 3ms, → Client 10ms — total ~20ms, jo 50ms target se bahut neeche hai.
Q04 Redis PubSub aur Kafka dono kyu chahiye? Ek se kaam kyu nahi chalta?
Dono ka kaam alag hai. Redis PubSub in-memory fan-out ke liye hai — real-time price updates ko 10+ WebSocket servers tak 10ms se kam me pahunchana, bina persistence ke. Kafka durable event backbone hai — order flow ke events (new_orders, verified_orders, order_status) ko reliably store aur deliver karna, taaki koi order lost na ho aur consumers apni speed se process kar sakein. PubSub 'abhi suno warna miss' hai, Kafka 'event safe hai, jab chaho padho' hai. Isiliye price feed ke liye PubSub aur order flow ke liye Kafka.
Q05 Time-series prices ke liye InfluxDB kyu, PostgreSQL kyu nahi?
Stock prices time-series data hai — har second hazaro writes, aur charts ke liye time-range queries. InfluxDB iske liye bana hai: 100,000+ writes/sec, automatic downsampling (1-min candles → 5-min → hourly → daily), aur 1 saal ka data 100ms se kam me range query. PostgreSQL row-based OLTP hai — itne write volume aur range queries par slow pad jayega aur storage bhi zyada khayega. Rule: time-series data ke liye time-series DB, transactional data ke liye SQL DB.
Q06 Fund locking kya hai aur SELECT FOR UPDATE kyu mandatory hai?
Fund locking ka matlab hai order place hote hi estimated cost ko available balance se locked balance me move kar dena — ek hi transaction me. SELECT FOR UPDATE row par pessimistic lock lagata hai, taaki do simultaneous orders ek hi balance ko ek saath read-update na kar sakein. Bina lock ke dono orders ₹50,000 balance dekh kar pass ho jate aur ₹66,680 ka kharcha karke ₹16,680 ka overdraft kar dete. Lab 4 me locking ON aur OFF dono chala kar farak dekho.
Q07 Optimistic locking aur pessimistic locking me kya farak hai? Funds ke liye kaunsa?
Pessimistic locking (SELECT FOR UPDATE) row ko lock karke concurrent updates ko serialize kar deta hai — correctness ki guarantee, throughput ki keemat par. Optimistic locking version column se kaam karta hai — concurrent reads allow karta hai, conflict par update fail ho jata hai aur retry karna padta hai. Funds ke liye pessimistic locking use hota hai kyunki overdraft kabhi acceptable nahi hai. Portfolio updates ke liye optimistic locking theek hai kyunki wahan retry tolerate kar sakte hain.
Q08 Double-click se duplicate order kaise rokte hain?
Do layers hain. Pehla: Validator me duplicate check — pichhle 5 seconds me same user, same stock ke PENDING ya PLACED orders gin kar, count 1 se zyada ho to reject. Doosra: Redis SETNX se distributed lock — order_lock:{orderId} ko 30 sec TTL ke saath set karo; agar key pehle se hai (SETNX 0 return kare) to duplicate hai. Dono milkar double-click aur retry dono se duplicate orders rokte hain.
Q09 Trading system me circuit breaker kya karta hai?
Circuit breaker market volatility se bachata hai. Validator check karta hai ki stock price pichhle 5 minute me 10% se zyada move to nahi hua — agar hua to order reject kar deta hai. Isse flash crash ya extreme volatility ke time galat price par orders execute nahi hote. Ye validation pipeline ka chauthe stage hai — duplicate check, fund check aur risk check ke baad. Alag se, global rate limit 100,000 orders/min bhi ek circuit breaker ki tarah kaam karta hai.
Q10 Risk check me total exposure limit 10x ka matlab kya hai?
Validator user ke saare open orders (PENDING aur PLACED) ki total value nikalta hai — SUM(quantity × price). Agar ye total available balance ke 10 guna se zyada hai to order reject kar diya jata hai. Isse user apni capacity se bahut zyada risk nahi le sakta. Ye check fund check se alag hai — fund check ek order ke liye balance dekhta hai, risk check user ke poore open exposure ko dekhta hai.
Q11 FIX protocol kya hai? Order bhejne me kaunse tags use hote hain?
FIX (Financial Information eXchange) wo protocol hai jisse Exchange Gateway NSE/BSE se TCP par baat karta hai. NewOrderSingle message me tags hote hain: 35=D (message type), 55=symbol (jaise RELIANCE), 54=side (1=Buy, 2=Sell), 38=quantity, 40=order type (1=Market, 2=Limit), 44=limit price (Limit ke liye). ExecutionReport wapas aata hai: 35=8, 150=2 (Filled). Lab 7 me khud FIX message bana kar dekho.
Q12 Exchange Gateway 400-500 connections kyu maintain karta hai?
Har FIX connection par ek time par limited symbols handle hote hain — ek connection par 400–500 symbols/legs. Connection per symbol group banta hai (jaise RELIANCE, TCS, INFY ek connection par). Persistent connections reuse karne se har order par handshake ka overhead bachta hai, aur orders connections par distribute karke load balance hota hai. Agar har order par naya connection banate to latency aur overhead dono badh jate.
Q13 Kafka topics ke partitions alag-alag kyu hain — 50, 10, 50, 100?
Partition count topic ke kaam aur parallelism par depend karta hai. new_orders: 50 partitions (user_id hash se — Validator ke 5 instances parallel consume karte hain). verified_orders: 10 partitions (volume kam hai, Exchange Gateway consume karta hai). order_status: 50 partitions (order_id hash se — 3 consumers: Order Tracker, Portfolio, Notification). stock_price: 100 partitions (stock_symbol hash se — 8,000–10,000 stocks ka feed sabse zyada volume wala hai). Partition key se ordering bhi milti hai — ek hi user ya stock ke events hamesha same partition me jate hain.
Q14 Order ki state transitions kya hain? Har state ka matlab kya hai?
PENDING — order bana, funds locked. VERIFIED — Validator ke 4 checks pass. PLACED — Exchange Gateway ne FIX se NSE/BSE ko bhej diya, exchange order ID mila. EXECUTED — matching engine ne trade confirm kiya. REJECTED — validation fail, funds unlock. CANCELLED — user ne pending order cancel kiya. Status orders table aur Kafka 'order_status' topic dono me track hota hai — isse user visibility, retry logic aur audit trail milta hai. Lab 8 me poora state machine chala kar dekho.
Q15 P&L ko DB me store kyu nahi karte? On-the-fly calculation kyu?
Kyunki price har second badalta hai. Agar P&L store karke rakho to wo turant stale ho jayega — user ko galat profit/loss dikhega. Isliye sirf static cheezein store hoti hain: quantity, avg_buy_price, total_investment. Jab user portfolio dekhe, tab current price Redis se lekar current_value = quantity × current_price aur profit_loss = current_value − total_investment calculate hota hai. Ye calculation sasti hai (ek Redis GET per holding) aur hamesha fresh hoti hai.
Q16 Average buy price ka formula kya hai?
Weighted average: nayi avg_buy_price = ((purani quantity × purani avg price) + (nayi quantity × naya executed price)) / (purani quantity + nayi quantity). Example: 10 shares @ ₹2459 hain, phir 10 aur @ ₹2469 khareede to avg = (10×2459 + 10×2469) / 20 = ₹2464. Har BUY trade par ye update hota hai; SELL par avg price nahi badalta, sirf quantity kam hoti hai.
Q17 Limit order execute nahi hua to kya hota hai — day order vs GTC?
Limit order order book me pada rehta hai jab tak price condition poori na ho. Agar din ke ant tak price target tak na pahunche to day order (default) cancel ho jata hai aur locked shares ya funds unlock ho jate hain. GTC (Good Till Cancelled) order ho to wo din ke ant me bhi khula rehta hai — jab tak price na pahunche ya user khud cancel na kare. Isiliye order place karte time validity batana zaroori hai.
Q18 Stop-loss order kaise kaam karta hai?
Stop-loss order me tum ek trigger price set karte ho. Jab market price us trigger tak pahunche (ya usse neeche gir jaye, SELL ke case me), order trigger hokar market order ban jata hai aur turant best price par execute hota hai. Iska use loss ko limit karne ke liye hota hai — jaise RELIANCE ₹2459 par khareeda, stop-loss ₹2400 par lagaya to price girne par order apne aap sell ho jayega. Lab 2 me stop-loss ko simulate karke dekho.
Q19 WebSocket me sticky sessions kyu chahiye?
WebSocket connection stateful hota hai — server ko yaad rehta hai ki kaunsa user kaunse stocks dekh raha hai (userConnections map). Agar load balancer user ko har baar alag server par bhej de to subscription state kho jayegi. Isliye user_id hash se consistent routing hoti hai — user hamesha same WebSocket server se judta hai. Server add hone ya fail hone par client reconnect karke watchlist dobara subscribe karta hai.
Q20 Selective push se bandwidth 1000x kaise bachti hai?
Har user average ~10 stocks watch karta hai, jabki exchange par 8,000–10,000 stocks hain. Agar server har user ko saare stocks ke updates bhejta to har user ko 1,000 guna zyada data milta. Selective push me server sirf wahi stocks ke updates bhejta hai jo user ki watchlist me hain — isliye bandwidth ~1000× kam ho jati hai. Lab 5 me users aur watchlist size badha kar ye saving khud calculate karo.
Q21 Orders table ko user_id se shard karne ka fayda kya hai? Cross-shard problem kya hai?
Sharding se user ke saare orders ek hi shard par rehte hain — user ki order history ki query ek shard se poori ho jati hai, fast rehti hai. 10 shards me har shard 100,000 users handle karta hai, total 1M users. Problem cross-shard queries me hai — jaise admin ko saare users ke orders dekhne hon to saare shards query karne padte hain, jo slow hota hai. Isliye cross-shard sirf admin queries ke liye acceptable mana jata hai, user-facing queries hamesha single-shard rehti hain.
Q22 Portfolio ke liye read replicas kyu? Lag acceptable kyu hai?
Portfolio view aur watchlist 95% traffic hai — reads bahut zyada, writes kam. 1 master (writes: order execution, fund updates) + 5 read replicas (reads) se read load distribute hota hai. Replica lag 1 sec se kam hota hai — portfolio view me 1 sec purana price dikhna acceptable hai kyunki price waise bhi har second badalta hai aur P&L on-the-fly calculate hota hai. Trade execution hamesha master par hota hai, taaki correctness me koi compromise na ho.
Q23 Rate limiting — 10 orders/sec per user kyu?
Fat-finger errors aur accidental rapid clicks rokne ke liye — jaise user ne galti se submit button 10 baar dabaa diya. Per user 10 orders/sec, per endpoint 100 req/sec per user (portfolio, watchlist), aur global 100,000 orders/min ka circuit breaker — system ko overload se bachata hai. Rate limit cross hone par request reject hoti hai, system crash nahi hota.
Q24 Notifications async kyu hain? DLQ kya hota hai?
Notifications order flow ke critical path me nahi hain — user ko trade confirmation 1 sec ke andar push se milti hai, lekin email/SMS baad ke liye queue me jate hain (non-blocking). Notification Svc Kafka 'order_status' ko async consume karta hai. Agar notification fail ho jaye to exponential backoff se retry hoti hai; 3 attempts ke baad wo dead-letter queue (DLQ) me chali jati hai, jahan se use baad me manually ya alag process se retry kiya ja sakta hai.
Q25 KYC flow kaise kaam karta hai — PAN aur Aadhaar verification?
User signup ke baad POST /auth/verify-kyc par PAN aur Aadhaar submit karta hai. User Svc KYC verification workflow trigger karta hai — PAN card format aur Aadhaar validate hote hain, aur documents verify hote hain. kyc_status PENDING se VERIFIED ya REJECTED hota hai. Bina VERIFIED KYC ke user trading nahi kar sakta — ye regulatory requirement hai. Aadhaar number encrypted store hota hai, plain text me kabhi nahi.
Q26 Funds deposit aur withdrawal ka flow kya hai?
Deposit: POST /api/v1/funds/deposit — linked bank account se Payment Gateway (Razorpay, Stripe, UPI) ke through paisa aata hai, Payment Transactions table me DEPOSIT entry banti hai, status PENDING se COMPLETED hota hai. Withdrawal: POST /api/v1/funds/withdraw — sirf available_balance se withdraw ho sakta hai, locked balance se nahi. Withdrawal bhi Payment Gateway se bank account me jata hai. Dono ki history GET /api/v1/funds/history se milti hai.
Q27 Market hours check kyu hota hai — 9:15 AM se 3:30 PM IST?
NSE/BSE sirf market hours me trade karte hain — subah 9:15 se dopahar 3:30 IST. Iske bahar order place karo to Order Svc turant 400 'Market closed' return kar deta hai, taaki order Kafka ya Exchange tak jaaye hi nahi. Ye validation Order Svc ke 5 checks me se ek hai — JWT, symbol, market hours, quantity, order_id generation.
Q28 Limit price ko current price ke ±10% ke andar kyu rakhna hota hai?
Galti se galat price daalne se bachane ke liye — jaise SELL order ₹1 par ya ₹100,000 par. ±10% ki band ke bahar ka limit price Order Svc turant reject kar deta hai. Ye ek fat-finger protection hai — user ne ek extra zero laga diya to bhi uska nuksan nahi hoga. Ye check limit order validation ka hissa hai, holdings check aur share locking ke saath.
Q29 Funds me available balance aur locked balance me kya farak hai?
Available balance wo cash hai jo trading ke liye free hai. Locked balance wo cash hai jo pending BUY orders me bandha hua hai — use kisi doosre order me use nahi kar sakte. Total balance = available + locked. Order place hote hi estimated cost available se locked me move hoti hai; execution par locked se actual cost deduct hoti hai; rejection ya cancellation par wapas available me aa jati hai. Ye separation hi overdraft rokta hai.
Q30 Order aur Trade table alag kyu hain?
Order user ki ichha hai — usne kya place kiya, kab, kis price par. Trade asal execution hai — exchange par kya hua, kis price par, kab. Ek order ka ek trade banta hai, lekin dono ka lifecycle alag hai: order PENDING se EXECUTED tak jata hai, trade sirf execution ke baad banta hai aur kabhi change nahi hota (audit trail). Isliye Orders table me status aur exchange_order_id hain, Trades table me executed_price aur commission_fee.
Q31 InfluxDB me downsampling kya hai?
Downsampling ka matlab hai purane granular data ko aggregate karke store karna — 1-min candles se 5-min, phir 1-hour, phir daily candles. InfluxDB continuous queries se ye apne aap karta hai. 1-min candles hamesha ke liye rehte hain, aggregates se charts fast bante hain (1 saal ka data 100ms se kam me). Storage bhi bachta hai aur range queries bhi fast rehti hain.
Q32 Redis keys — stock_price, user_session, order_lock — TTL kyu hai?
Har key ka TTL uske kaam ke hisaab se hai. stock_price:{symbol} ka TTL 1 sec hai — price har second refresh hoti hai, purani price stale nahi rehni chahiye. user_session:{token} ka TTL 24 hours hai — session ek din me expire ho jata hai. order_lock:{orderId} ka TTL 30 sec hai — distributed lock zyada der tak atak na jaye, agar process crash ho jaye to bhi lock apne aap chhoot jaye. TTL ke bina Redis me stale data jama ho jata.
Q33 Watchlist par GIN index kyu lagta hai?
Watchlist me stock_symbols ek text array hai (['RELIANCE', 'TCS', 'INFY']). Ye dhoondhna ki kaunse users ne RELIANCE watch kiya hai, array containment query hai — normal B-tree index is par kaam nahi karta. GIN (Generalized Inverted Index) array ke har element ko index karta hai, isliye 'kaunse users RELIANCE dekh rahe hain' wali query fast ho jati hai. Yehi query WebSocket fanout ke liye chahiye hoti hai.
Q34 Order cancel kab allowed hai?
DELETE /api/v1/orders/{orderId} sirf tab kaam karta hai jab order ka status PENDING ya PLACED ho. EXECUTED order cancel nahi hota — trade ho chuka hai. REJECTED ya CANCELLED order bhi dobara cancel nahi hota. Cancel hone par locked funds ya locked shares unlock ho jate hain — BUY order me locked balance wapas available me, SELL order me locked quantity wapas available quantity me.
Q35 Slippage kya hai? Market order me executed price cached price se alag kyu hota hai?
Slippage ka matlab hai order place karte time ka price aur asal execution price ka farak. Lesson ke example me cached price ₹2458.75 thi, execution ₹2459.00 par hui — ₹0.25 ka slippage. Ye isliye hota hai kyunki market price har millisecond badalta hai — cache se price padhne aur exchange par match hone ke beech me price move kar jata hai. Isiliye fund locking estimated cost par hoti hai aur execution par actual cost ka difference adjust hota hai (₹24,607.50 estimated vs ₹24,610 actual).
Q36 Peak 100,000 orders/minute kaise handle karte hain?
Kai techniques milkar: Kafka event-driven flow (Order Svc turant 200 OK dekar async process karta hai), Validator ke 5 instances 50 partitions se parallel consume karte hain, Exchange Gateway ke 400–500 persistent FIX connections, orders table ki user_id sharding, Redis cached prices (99% hit rate), aur per-user rate limiting (10 orders/sec) jo sudden spikes ko smooth karta hai. Normal 10,000 orders/min se peak 100,000 orders/min tak yehi architecture scale karta hai — consumers aur servers add karke.

Jo is SPA me NAHI hai — imaandari se

  • Stop-loss order ka alag se detail flow — lesson me stop-loss ko order type ke roop me list kiya gaya hai, lekin uska step-by-step flow alag se nahi diya gaya hai. Concept Lab 2 aur FAQ me cover hai; alag flow invent nahi kiya gaya.
  • Partial fills — lesson me ek order se ek trade banta hai; partial execution (order ka hissa-hissa fill hona) cover nahi hai, isliye yahan bhi nahi hai.
  • Diagram ke API naam variants — diagram me kuch endpoints alag naam se hain (jaise WS /api/v1/stocks/{symbol}, /api/v1/watchlists plural). SPA me lesson text wale endpoints hain; diagram wale variants Section 04 me note ke roop me diye gaye hain.
  • Diagram ki numbering — diagram me HLD ko "4" aur LLD ko "5" number diya hai; lesson text me ye 5 aur 6 hain. SPA me lesson text wali numbering use hui hai, farak Home section me note kiya gaya hai.
