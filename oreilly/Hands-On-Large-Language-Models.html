Chapter 1 — Large Language Models ka Introduction

Language model ek aisa system hai jo diye gaye words ke baad agla word (ya token) predict karna seekhta hai. Ye seedha sa prediction task, massive scale par repeat hone se, ek aisa model bana deta hai jo coherent text likh sakta hai, sawaalon ke jawab de sakta hai, summarise kar sakta hai, translate kar sakta hai — aur bhi bahut kuch.

Language model kya hai?

Model 'samajhta' nahi hai insaani sense me — usne bahut bade text corpus se language ke statistical patterns seekhe hain. Agla word predict karte-karte wo grammar, facts, reasoning ke patterns aur style — sab kuch seekh leta hai. Yehi foundation hai jiske upar poori kitab khadi hai.

GPT se ChatGPT tak

GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer) wo model family hai jisne is approach ko famous kiya. ChatGPT ki success ka raaz sirf base model (GPT-3.5) nahi tha — uske saath ek conversational interface aur dialogue ke liye fine-tuning (reinforcement learning from human feedback, jo Chapter 12 me detail me hai) bhi thi. Interface ne capability ko accessible banaya; underlying prediction task wahi raha.

GPT-3 aur GPT-4 — scale ka breakthrough

  • GPT-3: 175 billion parameters, bahut bade corpus par trained. Sirf prompt aur kuch examples se kai tasks kar leta hai — bina task-specific training ke.
  • GPT-4: isse aage ka kadam — multimodal (text ke saath images bhi handle karta hai), reasoning aur instructions follow karne me zyada strong. Phir bhi wahi Transformer aur next-token prediction foundation.

Teen types — generation, representation, multimodal

  • Generative models text produce karte hain — agla token predict karke sequence aage badhate hain.
  • Representation models embeddings produce karte hain — dense numeric vectors jo kisi word, sentence ya document ka matlab capture karte hain. Inka use classification, clustering, search jaise tasks me hota hai, bina text generate kiye.
  • Multimodal models text ke saath doosri modality (khaas taur par images) jodte hain — image ko describe karna, us par sawaal ka jawab dena, ya us par reasoning karna.

Chapter 1 me code kyun kam hai

Chapter 1 jaan-boojh kar code-light hai — hands-on kaam Chapter 2 se shuru hota hai. Is chapter ka kaam hai sahi sawaal dimaag me bitha dena.

Key takeaway

Har task ke liye pehle ye tay karo: generative model chahiye, representation model chahiye, ya multimodal — aur use as-is chalana hai, prompt karna hai, ya fine-tune karna hai? Poori kitab isi sawaal ke aas-paas ghoomti hai.

Chapter 2 — Tokens aur Embeddings

LLM jo kuch bhi karta hai, wo tokens aur embeddings se shuru hota hai. Text pehle numbers me badalta hai — tokens — phir har token ek vector banta hai — embedding — aur us vector ki geometry me hi matlab chhupa hota hai. Ye samajh lo to baaki architecture aasan lagne lagta hai.

Tokens kya hain?

Model ko text characters ya poore words ke roop me nahi milta; wo tokens me tootta hai — text ke chhote chunks, jo model ki basic unit hain. Ek token poora word ho sakta hai, word ka hissa, punctuation, ya space. Tokenisation text ko is tarah todne ka process hai; detokenisation ulta. Tokenizer ka choice har cheez ko affect karta hai: context window me kitna text aayega, prompt ki cost, aur rare words ko model kaise dekhta hai.

Tokenizer ke chaar types

TokenizerKaise kaam karta haiKahan use hota hai

Worked example: ek sentence GPT-2 ke tokenizer se tokenise karke dikhaya gaya hai — kaise words tootte hain, leading space token ka hissa kaise banti hai, aur token IDs wapas text me kaise map hote hain. Wahi idea BERT ke WordPiece se bhi dikhaya gaya hai, jahan subject wala word subwords me toot jata hai.

Embeddings — matlab geometry me

Embedding ek dense vector hai — numbers ki list — jo kisi token, word, sentence ya document ko high-dimensional space me represent karta hai, jahan similar matlab wali cheezein paas-paas baithi hoti hain. Kitab ki central intuition: is space me distance aur direction hi matlab hain — similarity aur word-relations, dono geometry se padhe jate hain.

Word2Vec — classic embedding method

Word2Vec co-occurrence se word embeddings seekhta hai: jo words similar contexts me aate hain, unke vectors similar hote hain. Do architectures hain:

  • CBOW (Continuous Bag of Words): context se word predict karta hai.
  • Skip-gram: word se uska context predict karta hai.
  • Negative sampling: har real (word, context) pair ke saath kuch random negative pairs bhi dikhaye jate hain, taaki model real co-occurrence ko chance se alag karna seekhe.

Figure 2-5 me do-dimensional projection (t-SNE/PCA style) dikhaya gaya hai jisme related words ek saath cluster banate hain — matlab ka visual proof.

Tokens aur embeddings ka unified view

Dono ek hi pipeline ke do hisse hain: tokenisation text ko IDs me badalti hai, embedding layer har ID ko vector me badalti hai, aur Transformer phir un vectors ko context me process karta hai. Isliye ek token ka embedding akele me fixed nahi hota — Transformer me wo positional information aur sequence ke baaki tokens ke saath mil kar banta hai.

Chapter 3 — Large Language Models ke Andar

Transformer 2017 ke paper se aaya architecture hai, aur aaj ke lagbhag saare LLMs usi par khade hain. Ye poori sequence ko ek saath process karta hai — word-by-word nahi, jaise recurrent network karta tha — aur attention se har token ko sequence ke baaki tokens ko dekhne deta hai. Ye parallelism hi LLM-scale training ko possible banata hai.

Attention — har token decide karta hai kisko kitna weight dena hai

Har token ke liye teen vectors bante hain — query, key aur value. Do tokens ke beech attention score ek token ki query aur doosre ki key ki similarity hoti hai, scaled aur softmax se guzri hui. Ek token ka output value vectors ka weighted sum hota hai — matlab har token ki representation un tokens ka blend ban jati hai jo uske liye sabse relevant hain. Kitab ka worked example ek sentence me dikhata hai ki subject wala word kin related words par dhyaan deta hai.

Multi-head attention

Ek attention operation ke bajaye model kai heads parallel chalata hai — har head alag tarah ka rishta seekhta hai. Ek head subject-verb agreement track kar sakta hai, doosra pronoun ka referent, teesra long-range dependency. Saare heads ke outputs concatenate karke wapas model ki hidden size par project kar diye jate hain.

Feed-forward network

Attention ke baad har token ka vector ek feed-forward network se guzarta hai — do linear layers, beech me non-linear activation. Ye har position par independently lagta hai, aur yahin model apni seekhi hui baatein token par apply karta hai.

Layer normalisation aur residual connections

Har sub-layer residual connection se lapeta hota hai (input ko output me jod do) aur layer normalisation se (vector ko stable mean/variance par rescale karo). Ye training ko stable rakhte hain aur bahut gehre networks ko train karna possible banate hain.

Positional encoding

Attention ko akele me order ka koi sense nahi hota, isliye positional information add karni padti hai. Kitab original sinusoidal encoding aur modern variants (learned, aur rotary — RoPE, rotary positional embedding, jo LLaMA discussion me aata hai) cover karti hai. Position token embedding me Transformer blocks se pehle jod di jati hai.

Chapter 4 — Text Classification

Text classification matlab: kisi text ko label dena — positive ya negative sentiment, topic, ya spam/not-spam. Is chapter ka running example movie reviews ka sentiment classification hai, Rotten Tomatoes dataset par. Wahi ek task, chaar alag approaches — aur numbers se pata chalta hai kaun sa tareeka kab jeetta hai.

Task aur dataset

Rotten Tomatoes dataset: 8,530 training examples, 1,066 validation, 1,066 test — har ek positive ya negative label ke saath. Kitab pehli rows, label distribution aur train/validation/test split dikhati hai.

Chaar approaches — ek hi task par

  1. Classic machine learning: TF-IDF features (word counts, weighted by how distinctive a word hai across documents) + logistic-regression classifier. Strong, fast baseline — aur interpretable.
  2. Representation approach: pretrained embedding model (chapter me sentence-transformer) se har review embed karo, phir embeddings par logistic regression train karo. Koi LLM fine-tuning nahi chahiye — embeddings me matlab pehle se hai.
  3. Generative, zero-shot: generative model ko review + dono labels dekar prompt karo, wo ek choose kare. Koi training examples nahi chahiye, par prompt clear hona chahiye aur output parse karna padta hai.
  4. Generative, few-shot / instruction-tuned: Flan-T5 jaisa model, ya GPT-3.5 jaisa bada API model — prompt me label set aur review do.

Comparison point ke liye ek task-specific fine-tuned RoBERTa bhi hai (poora fine-tuning procedure Chapter 11 me; Chapter 4 me sirf result).

  • Sabse zyada accuracy chahiye aur API ki cost chalegi: bada API model mushkil se haraya jata hai.
  • Speed, cost control ya on-premises deployment chahiye: chhota model behtar hai.

Key takeaway

Chapter 5 — Text Clustering aur Topic Modeling

Clustering bina labels ke documents ko similarity se group karta hai. Topic modelling ek kadam aage hai — har group ko ek interpretable label bhi deta hai: topic matlab cluster + use describe karne wale words. Is chapter ka main tool BERTopic hai, jo modern embeddings ko simple, samajhne layak topic representation se jodta hai.

Clustering

Clustering unsupervised hai: groups data se emerge hote hain, analyst baad me unhe interpret karta hai. Chapter me embeddings (sentence-transformer se) liye jate hain, unki dimensionality UMAP se reduce ki jati hai, phir HDBSCAN se cluster kiye jate hain.

Topic modelling — LDA se BERTopic tak

Classical LDA (latent Dirichlet allocation) har document ko topics ka mixture aur har topic ko words ka distribution maanta hai — par uske topics aksar interpret karna mushkil hota hai. BERTopic ka pipeline hai:

  1. Documents embed karo.
  2. UMAP se dimensionality reduce karo.
  3. HDBSCAN se cluster karo.
  4. Class-based TF-IDF (c-TF-IDF) se har topic represent karo — cluster ke saare documents ko ek bada document maan kar wo words dhoondho jo us cluster ke liye distinctive hain.

Result: topics ka set, har ek ke saath representative words aur document count.

Running example — 20 Newsgroups

Dataset 20 Newsgroups hai, kitab me categories ke subset par filter kiya gaya. Mile hue topics me space, religion, computers aur sport jaise topics hain — har ek ke top words aur document counts ke saath. Figure 5-2 me UMAP scatter plot hai (documents 2D me, cluster ke hisaab se coloured), Figure 5-4 me sabse frequent topics ka bar chart, Figure 5-5 me ek topic ka word-score chart, aur Figure 5-6 me hierarchical topic tree / dendrogram.

Code — step by step (notebook)

  1. 20 Newsgroups load karo aur documents ko sentence-transformer se embed karo.
  2. UMAP (n_neighbors, n_components, min_dist settings) aur HDBSCAN chalao, clusters plot karo.
  3. BERTopic model banao, fit karo, topic information table print karo (topic number, count, name).
  4. BERTopic ke built-in plots se topics visualise karo (bar chart, word scores, hierarchy).
  5. BERTopic fine-tune karo: embedding model badlo, UMAP/HDBSCAN parameters badlo, topics ki sankhya badlo, aur representation model (KeyBERT) se topic words improve karo.
pythoncode — description se banaya gaya
from bertopic import BERTopic
from sentence_transformers import SentenceTransformer
from sklearn.datasets import fetch_20newsgroups

docs = fetch_20newsgroups(subset="all")["data"]
emb = SentenceTransformer("all-MiniLM-L6-v2").encode(docs)

topic_model = BERTopic()
topics, _ = topic_model.fit_transform(docs, emb)
print(topic_model.get_topic_info().head())

BERTopic ko fine-tune karna

  • Embedding model badlo — domain ke hisaab se behtar embeddings.
  • UMAP adjust karo — zyada neighbours = zyada global structure; kam neighbours = zyada local structure.
  • HDBSCAN minimum cluster size badlo — bada value = kam, bade topics.
  • Topics merge karo — paas-paas wale topics ek kar do.
  • Custom representation model (jaise KeyBERT) se topic words improve karo.

Chapter 6 — Prompt Engineering

Prompt generative model ko diya gaya input text hai. Prompting sirf sawaal poochhna nahi hai — ye model ki reasoning process aur output format, dono ko design karna hai. Is chapter ka model Phi-3-mini hai (Microsoft, 3.8 billion parameters, kitab ke hisaab se 128k context), jo locally Hugging Face se chalaya jata hai.

Generation parameters

  • Temperature: randomness control karta hai — low = zyada deterministic, high = zyada creative.
  • top_p (nucleus sampling): kitne most-likely tokens consider kiye jayein.
  • top_k: choice ko k sabse likely tokens tak limit karta hai.
  • max_new_tokens: output ki lambai limit karta hai.

Chapter me wahi prompt alag-alag temperatures par chalakar effect dikhaya gaya hai.

Prompting strategies

  • Zero-shot: bina examples ke seedha sawaal. Tab kaam karta hai jab task clear ho aur model ko pehle se aata ho.
  • Few-shot: prompt me kuch input-output examples do. Model pattern imitate karta hai. Chapter me few-shot sentiment aur few-shot translation examples hain.
  • Role prompting / system prompts: model ko role batao (helpful assistant, ya kisi domain ka expert) aur style tay karo.
  • Instruction prompting: clear, specific instruction do — desired output format samet.

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting

CoT me model se final answer se pehle step-by-step reason karne ko kaha jata hai. Kitab ka example ek arithmetic/reasoning problem hai: CoT ke bina model seedha galat jawab deta hai; step-by-step prompt se wo intermediate steps se hokar sahi jawab tak pahunchta hai. Zero-shot CoT bhi dikhaya gaya hai — 'step by step socho' jaisa phrase jodna.

Self-consistency

Self-consistency me kai CoT reasoning paths sample kiye jate hain (jaise 5 ya 10), temperature zero se upar rakhkar, aur sabse common final answer liya jata hai. Ek unlucky reasoning path ka asar kam ho jata hai aur reasoning tasks par accuracy badhti hai — keemat hai zyada generation.

Tree-of-Thought (ToT) prompting

ToT CoT ko tree me generalise karta hai: model kai possible next thoughts generate karta hai, unhe evaluate karta hai, aur sabse promising branches explore karta hai (breadth-first ya depth-first search se). Kitab ka example ek creative-writing / planning task hai, jahan ToT chunne se pehle alag-alag outlines explore karta hai.

Structured output — program me use karne layak

Software me use ke liye output parseable hona chahiye. Do routes dikhaye gaye hain:

  1. Model ko JSON return karne ko prompt karo aur parse karo — schema prompt me hi do.
  2. Generation ko grammar / JSON schema se constrain karo (Outlines library) — model sirf wahi tokens produce kar sakta hai jo output ko valid JSON rakhein. Grammar-constrained example ek JSON object return karta hai jisme named fields hain (jaise kisi vyakti ka naam, umar aur occupation) — aur wo parse hona guaranteed hai.

  4. ToT prompt banao jo branches generate aur evaluate kare.

Chapter 8 — Semantic Search aur Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Semantic search

Tareeka: corpus ke har document (ya chunk) ko embed karo, query ko usi model se embed karo, aur wo documents return karo jinki embeddings query embedding ke sabse kareeb hain (cosine similarity se). Chapter ke example corpus me book ke subject par documents hain, query ek natural-language sawaal hai — top results semantically related hain, bhale hi query se unke common words kam hon.

Vector databases

Scale par karne ke liye embeddings vector database me store ki jati hain. Chapter me Chroma use hua hai (alternatives: Pinecone, Weaviate, FAISS). Vector database embeddings ko index karti hai taaki nearest-neighbour search tez ho, aur har vector ke saath original text aur metadata bhi store karti hai.

Chunking — lambi documents ko todna

Lambi documents ko embed karne se pehle chunks me todna padta hai — kyunki embedding models ki maximum input length hoti hai, aur chhota chunk kisi ek query se relevant hone ki zyada sambhavna rakhta hai. Chunk size aur overlap par discussion hai: zyada bada chunk irrelevant material le aata hai; zyada chhota chunk context kho deta hai.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

  1. Sawaal ke liye sabse relevant chunks retrieve karo.
  2. Un chunks ko prompt me context ke roop me daalo.
  3. Generative model se us context ka use karke jawab banwao.

Fayda: model un information par jawab de sakta hai jin par wo train nahi hua, aur retrieved passages ko cite kar sakta hai. Risk: model phir bhi hallucinate kar sakta hai, ya retrieved context ko ignore kar sakta hai.

Prompt me retrieved passages aur ye instruction hoti hai ki sirf unhi se jawab do. RAG ko evaluate karna

Dono hisson ki evaluation hoti hai: retrieval (kya retrieved chunks relevant hain? precision/recall-style metrics aur human judgement se) aur generation (kya jawab sahi hai, retrieved passages ke prati faithful hai, aur complete hai?). Chapter 9 — Multimodal Large Language Models

Multimodal model ek se zyada modality handle karta hai — is chapter me text aur images. Wo image describe kar sakta hai, us par sawaal ka jawab de sakta hai, ya text description se match karne wali image dhoondh sakta hai. Tareeka: image ka apna encoder, phir use language model se jodne wala bridge.

Vision Transformers (ViT)

Image Transformer ko pixels ke roop me nahi di jati; wo patches me tooti jati hai (jaise 16x16 pixel ke squares), har patch flatten karke linear project kiya jata hai ek vector me, positional embeddings jode jate hain, aur patch vectors ki sequence Transformer encoder se process hoti hai — bilkul waise hi jaise token embeddings ki sequence. [CLS] token ka output image ki representation ke roop me use hota hai.

CLIP — contrastive language-image pre-training

CLIP image-caption pairs par train hota hai. Uske paas ek image encoder aur ek text encoder hai, aur wo contrastively train hota hai: ek batch me sahi pairs ki similarity maximise aur galat pairs ki minimise ki jati hai. Training ke baad, ek image aur ek text jinka matlab same hai, unke embeddings paas-paas hote hain. Kitab ka example: ek puppy ki photo — puppy wale caption ki similarity 0.33 hai, jo car jaise unrelated captions se zyada hai.

BLIP-2 — frozen encoders ke beech bridge

BLIP-2 ek frozen image encoder aur ek frozen language model ko ek chhote trainable bridge — Q-Former (Querying Transformer) — se jodta hai. Q-Former learnable query vectors use karta hai jo image encoder ke output par attend karte hain aur visual tokens ka compact set produce karte hain, jo phir language model ko feed hota hai. Bade encoders frozen rehte hain, isliye BLIP-2 end-to-end train hone wale model se bahut sasta hai.

Multimodal model banana

Example image: sunset ke time road par ek supercar, aur model ka caption usi hisaab se hai — road par sunset me chalti ek orange supercar. Visual question answering bhi dikhaya gaya hai: image ke baare me sawaal poochho, text me jawab pao.

Sentence Transformers library

Sentence Transformers kitab ki main library hai. Sentence Transformer train karna — teen loss functions

STS task par teen training approaches compare ki gayi hain:

  • Softmax loss: model ko train karo ki sentence pair similar hai ya nahi, similarity scores par softmax laga kar.
  • Cosine similarity loss: seedha cosine similarity par train karo, taaki model ki similarity human score se match kare.
  • Multiple Negatives Ranking (MNR) loss: har anchor sentence ke liye positive (similar) sentence batch ke baaki saare sentences se upar rank kare — batch ke baaki examples hi negatives hain. Ye efficient hai kyunki har doosra example negative hai.

Continued pretraining

Fine-tune karne se pehle ek kadam: model ka original pretraining task (masked language modelling, MLM) target domain ke text par continue karo, taaki model us domain ki vocabulary aur style apna le. SetFit — few-shot classification

SetFit sentence transformers ke saath few-shot classification ka method hai. Do stages:

  1. Embedding model ko contrastive learning se fine-tune karo — same class ke sentence pairs ko paas khincho, alag class wale ko door dhakelo.
  2. Resulting embeddings par simple classifier (logistic regression) train karo.

Kitab ke example me sirf 16 examples per class (kul 32 documents) use hote hain, jinse 1,280 sentence pairs banaye jate hain (har example se 20 pairs). Result: F1 0.836 (chapter ke summary figure me lagbhag 0.85), all-mpnet-base-v2 se, 3 epochs me — full BERT fine-tuning ke kareeb, bahut kam labelled data ke saath.

Fine-tuning use task, style ya instructions ke hisaab se dhalta hai. Chapter ka progression — SFT, phir PEFT, phir preference optimisation — aaj ka standard modern recipe hai.

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT)

SFT sabse simple form hai: prompts aur desired completions ke pairs (instruction data) collect karo, aur model ko train karo ki prompt dekh kar completion predict kare — usual next-token loss se. Chapter ka example Databricks Dolly dataset (15,000 instruction-response pairs, kitab ke hisaab se) use karke Phi-3-mini ko fine-tune karta hai. Pehle aur baad ka behaviour dikhaya gaya hai: pehle model prompt ko rambling tareeke se continue karta hai; baad me instruction ka seedha aur concise jawab deta hai.

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT)

Full fine-tuning har parameter update karta hai — bade model ke liye mehenga, aur catastrophic forgetting ka risk (model apni general abilities kho de). PEFT sirf parameters ka chhota subset update karta hai, ya chhoti sankhya me naye parameters add karta hai, baaki frozen rehte hain. Kitab PEFT ko bade generative models ke liye practical default batati hai.

LoRA — Low-Rank Adaptation

LoRA chapter ka main PEFT method hai. Bade weight matrix W ko seedha update karne ke bajaye, LoRA ek low-rank update seekhta hai: do chhoti matrices A aur B, jinka product W me joda jata hai. Rank r update ka size control karta hai (kitab ke example me r=16). Sirf A aur B train hote hain; W frozen rehta hai. Inference ke time update ko W me merge kiya ja sakta hai, isliye koi extra cost nahi. Kitab dikhati hai ki r=16 wala LoRA, kuch epochs me, fully fine-tuned model jitna hi achha instruction-following deta hai — bahut kam trainable parameters ke saath.

QLoRA — LoRA + quantisation

QLoRA LoRA plus quantisation hai: base model 4-bit precision me store hota hai (bitsandbytes library se), isliye bada model bahut kam GPU memory me fit ho jata hai, aur uske upar LoRA adapters train hote hain. QLoRA se aisa model fine-tune karna possible ho jata hai jo warna ek consumer GPU par fit hi nahi hota — full precision LoRA ke muqable sirf chhoti si quality loss ke saath.

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)

RLHF wo method hai jisse model ko human preferences se align kiya jata hai — khaas taur par ChatGPT ke liye. Teen stages:

  1. Supervised fine-tuning instruction data par.
  2. Reward model train karo model outputs ki human rankings par — reward model seekhta hai ki human kis output ko prefer karega.
  3. Reinforcement learning (PPO — Proximal Policy Optimization) se language model update karo taaki wo aise outputs de jinhe reward model high score de — aur supervised model ke kareeb rahe (KL-divergence penalty use zyada door jaane se rokta hai).

Kitab samjhati hai ki RLHF powerful hai, par complex aur mehenga bhi.

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)

DPO RLHF ka simple alternative hai: alag reward model train karne aur reinforcement learning chalane ke bajaye, DPO language model ko seedha output pairs (ek chosen output aur ek rejected output, wahi prompt ke liye) par optimise karta hai — chosen output ki likelihood rejected ke muqable badhata hai. Kitab DPO ko implement karne me aasan aur aksar RLHF jitna effective batati hai.

Interview FAQs — Hands-On LLMs

Current-era interviews me LLMs se poochhe jaane wale sabse kaam ke 36 sawaal — har jawab wahi hai jo is SPA ke book sections me detail me samjhaya gaya hai. Kisi bhi sawaal par click karke jawab kholo.

Q01 Large Language Model (LLM) kya hai?
Ek aisa system jo diye gaye words ke baad agla word (token) predict karna seekhta hai. Ye simple prediction task massive scale par repeat hone se model ko text generate karna, sawaalon ke jawab dena, summarise aur translate karna sikha deta hai. Model 'samajhta' nahi — usne language ke statistical patterns seekhe hain.
Q02 GPT-3 aur GPT-4 me kya farak hai?
GPT-3 (175 billion parameters) scale breakthrough tha — sirf prompt aur kuch examples se kai tasks kar leta tha. GPT-4 usse aage hai: multimodal (images bhi), reasoning aur instruction-following me zyada strong. Dono ka foundation wahi hai — Transformer aur next-token prediction. ChatGPT ki success me base model ke saath conversational interface aur RLHF fine-tuning bhi thi.
Q03 Generative, representation aur multimodal models me kya farak hai?
Generative model text produce karta hai (agla token predict karke). Representation model embeddings produce karta hai — dense vectors jo classification, clustering, search me use hote hain, bina text generate kiye. Multimodal model text ke saath image jaisi doosri modality bhi handle karta hai — jaise image describe karna ya us par sawaal ka jawab dena.
Q04 Token kya hai, aur tokenizer ka choice kyun matter karta hai?
Token model ki basic unit hai — poora word, word ka hissa, punctuation ya space. Tokenisation text ko tokens me todti hai. Choice matter karta hai kyunki ye tay karta hai: context window me kitna text aayega, prompt ki cost, aur rare words model ko kaise dikhte hain. GPT-2 ki vocabulary lagbhag 50,257 tokens ki hai; BERT ki lagbhag 30,522.
Q05 BPE, WordPiece, Unigram aur SentencePiece me kya farak hai?
WordPiece characters se shuru karke useful pairs merge karta hai (BERT); unknown word subwords me toot jata hai, ## marker ke saath. Unigram badi vocabulary se kam-useful tokens hatata hai (ALBERT, T5). SentencePiece raw stream treat karta hai, spaces samet (LLaMA). BPE frequent adjacent pairs merge karta hai (GPT-2/3); byte-level BPE bytes par kaam karke unknown-token problem khatm karta hai.
Q06 Embedding kya hai? Word2Vec kaise kaam karta hai?
Embedding ek dense vector hai jo word/sentence/document ka matlab numbers me dikhata hai — similar matlab paas-paas hota hai. Word2Vec co-occurrence se seekhta hai: similar contexts me aane wale words ke vectors similar hote hain. CBOW context se word predict karta hai; Skip-gram word se context predict karta hai. Negative sampling me real pairs ke saath random negative pairs dikhaye jate hain.
Q07 CBOW aur Skip-gram me kaun sa kab behtar hai?
CBOW context se centre word predict karta hai — chhote datasets aur frequent words par tez aur effective. Skip-gram word se context predict karta hai — rare words aur bade datasets par behtar representation deta hai, par slow hota hai. Dono ke baad Transformer-based contextual embeddings aaye, jo word ka matlab uske aas-paas ke words se tay karte hain.
Q08 Attention me Query, Key aur Value kya hote hain?
Har token ke liye teen vectors bante hain: query ('main kya dhoondh raha hoon?'), key ('main kya offer karta hoon?') aur value ('main kya deta hoon?'). Attention score ek token ki query aur doosre ki key ki similarity hoti hai, scaled aur softmax se guzri hui. Output values ka weighted sum hota hai — har token un tokens ka blend ban jata hai jo uske liye relevant hain.
Q09 Multi-head attention kyun chahiye?
Ek attention head ek tarah ka rishta seekhta hai — jaise subject-verb agreement ya pronoun ka referent. Kai heads parallel chalakar model ek saath kai tarah ke rishte capture kar leta hai. Saare heads ke outputs concatenate karke wapas hidden size par project kar diye jate hain.
Q10 Feed-forward network, layer norm aur residual connections ka kya role hai?
Attention ke baad har token ka vector feed-forward network (do linear layers, beech me non-linear activation) se guzarta hai — yahin model seekhi hui baatein apply karta hai. Residual connection input ko output me jodti hai aur layer normalisation vector ko stable mean/variance par rescale karti hai — dono milkar bahut gehre networks ki training ko stable rakhte hain.
Q11 Positional encoding kyun chahiye? RoPE kya hai?
Attention ko akele me word-order ka sense nahi hota, isliye position ki information add karni padti hai. Original sinusoidal encoding ke alawa learned aur rotary variants hain — RoPE (rotary positional embedding) position ko rotation ke roop me encode karta hai aur modern models (jaise LLaMA family) me use hota hai.
Q12 Decoder-only, encoder-only aur encoder-decoder me kya farak hai?
Decoder-only (GPT): masked self-attention, har token sirf pehle wale tokens ko dekhta hai — generation ke liye natural. Encoder-only (BERT): bidirectional attention, dono taraf dekhta hai — representation ke liye natural. Encoder-decoder (T5): encoder input padhta hai, decoder cross-attention se output generate karta hai — translation/summarisation jaise sequence-to-sequence tasks ke liye.
Q13 GPT, BERT, T5 ya LLaMA — kab kaun sa model chunna hai?
Text generate karna ho to decoder-only (GPT family, LLaMA family). Text ko represent/classify karna ho to encoder-only (BERT family: BERT-base 110M, BERT-large 340M). Input ko output me transform karna ho (translation, summarisation) to encoder-decoder (T5 family, Flan-T5). Open efficient alternatives me Mistral 7B aur Mixtral (mixture-of-experts) hain.
Q14 Text classification ke chaar approaches kaun se hain?
Classic ML: TF-IDF features + logistic regression — strong, fast, interpretable baseline. Representation: pretrained embeddings + logistic regression — bina LLM fine-tune kiye. Generative zero-shot: prompt se label chunwana — bina training examples ke. Generative few-shot/instruction-tuned: Flan-T5 ya GPT-3.5 API — prompt me label set aur review do.
Q15 Rotten Tomatoes par embedding approach ne fine-tuned RoBERTa ko kaise haraya?
Numbers: TF-IDF + LR 0.78, RoBERTa fine-tune 0.80, embedding + LR 0.85, zero-shot 0.78, Flan-T5 0.84, GPT-3.5 0.91. Sentence-transformer embeddings me matlab pehle se encoded hota hai, isliye unpar simple classifier bhi fine-tuned model se aage nikal gaya — bina koi LLM fine-tune kiye. Ye dikhata hai ki classification ke liye text generate karna zaroori nahi.
Q16 Clustering aur topic modelling me kya farak hai?
Clustering bina labels ke documents ko similarity se group karta hai — groups data se emerge hote hain. Topic modelling ek kadam aage hai: har group ko interpretable label deta hai — topic matlab cluster + use describe karne wale words. Classical LDA topics ko words ka distribution maanta hai, par uske topics interpret karna mushkil hota hai; BERTopic modern embeddings + c-TF-IDF se readable topics deta hai.
Q17 BERTopic ka pipeline kya hai?
Chaar steps: (1) documents embed karo, (2) UMAP se dimensionality reduce karo, (3) HDBSCAN se cluster karo, (4) class-based TF-IDF (c-TF-IDF) se har topic represent karo — cluster ke saare documents ko ek bada document maan kar distinctive words dhoondho. Result: har topic ke saath representative words aur document count.
Q18 UMAP aur HDBSCAN kya karte hain?
UMAP high-dimensional embeddings ko low dimensions me le aata hai, structure bachate hue — zyada neighbours se global structure, kam se local structure. HDBSCAN density-based clustering hai: dense areas ko cluster banata hai, outliers ko noise chhod deta hai. Minimum cluster size badhane se kam, bade topics bante hain.
Q19 Zero-shot aur few-shot prompting me kya farak hai?
Zero-shot me bina examples ke seedha sawaal poochhte ho — tab kaam karta hai jab task clear ho. Few-shot me prompt me kuch input-output examples dete ho — model pattern imitate karta hai. Dono ke saath role prompting (model ko role dena) aur instruction prompting (clear instruction + desired output format) milakar prompt design hota hai.
Q20 Temperature, top_p, top_k aur max_new_tokens kya hain?
Temperature randomness control karta hai — low (0.1–0.3) deterministic, high (1.0–2.0) creative. top_p (nucleus sampling) most-likely tokens ka cumulative-probability set tay karta hai. top_k choice ko k sabse likely tokens tak limit karta hai. max_new_tokens output ki lambai limit karta hai. Fact-based kaam me low temperature, creative kaam me high temperature use hota hai.
Q21 Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting kya hai?
Model se final answer se pehle step-by-step reason karne ko kehna. Bina CoT ke model seedha jawab par koodta hai aur reasoning wale sawaal me aksar galat hota hai; CoT se wo intermediate steps likhta hai aur sahi jawab tak pahunchta hai. Zero-shot CoT me sirf 'step by step socho' jaisa phrase jodte hain.
Q22 Self-consistency aur Tree-of-Thought kya hain?
Self-consistency: kai CoT paths sample karo (jaise 5–10, temperature > 0) aur majority vote lo — ek galat path ka asar khatm ho jata hai, keemat hai zyada generation. Tree-of-Thought: CoT ko tree banao — kai possible next thoughts generate karo, evaluate karo, promising branches explore karo (breadth-first ya depth-first search). Creative/planning tasks me ToT behtar hota hai.
Q23 Structured output kyun chahiye? Outlines kya karta hai?
Software me model ka output parseable hona chahiye, warna program toot jata hai. Do routes: prompt me JSON maango aur parse karo (schema prompt me do), ya generation ko grammar/JSON schema se constrain karo — Outlines library model ko sirf wahi tokens produce karne deti hai jo output ko valid JSON rakhein. Isse parse hona guaranteed ho jata hai.
Q24 LangChain ke building blocks kya hain? ReAct pattern kya hai?
Models (LLM/chat), Prompts (templates), Chains (steps ke sequences), Memory (conversation history), Agents (tools choose karne wale models). ReAct pattern agent ka loop hai: Thought (kya karna hai?) → Action (tool call) → Observation (tool ka result) → repeat, phir final answer. Chapter ke agent ke paas search aur calculator tools hain.
Q25 Semantic search keyword search se kaise alag hai?
Keyword search words match karta hai; semantic search matlab match karta hai. Documents aur query dono ko embed karke cosine similarity se kareebi documents laaye jate hain — top results semantically related hote hain, bhale hi query se unke common words kam hon. Iske liye embeddings ko vector database (jaise Chroma) me store karte hain.
Q26 RAG kya hai, aur chunking kyun zaroori hai?
RAG = Retrieval-Augmented Generation: (1) sawaal ke liye relevant chunks retrieve karo, (2) unhe prompt me context ke roop me daalo, (3) generative model se jawab banwao. Isse model un facts par jawab de sakta hai jin par wo train nahi hua. Chunking zaroori hai kyunki embedding models ki max input length hoti hai, aur chhota chunk ek query se relevant hone ki zyada sambhavna rakhta hai — bada chunk irrelevant material le aata hai, chhota context kho deta hai.
Q27 Vector database kya hai? Chroma, Pinecone, Weaviate, FAISS me kya farak hai?
Vector database embeddings ko index karke fast nearest-neighbour search deti hai, original text aur metadata samet. Chroma chapter me use hua — local aur aasan setup. Pinecone hosted managed service hai. Weaviate open-source hai, local aur hosted dono. FAISS ek library hai (Meta ki) — fast similarity search ke liye, database nahi.
Q28 RAG ko evaluate kaise karte hain? RAGAS kya hai?
Dono hisson ki alag evaluation: retrieval (kya retrieved chunks relevant hain? precision/recall-style metrics + human judgement) aur generation (kya jawab sahi, faithful aur complete hai?). RAGAS framework ise automate karta hai — faithfulness, answer relevance aur context precision/recall metrics ke saath. System ki quality zyada tar retrieval ki quality hoti hai.
Q29 Vision Transformer (ViT) image ko kaise process karta hai?
Image ko patches me toda jata hai (jaise 16×16 pixels), har patch flatten karke linear project kiya jata hai vector me, positional embeddings jode jate hain, aur patch vectors ki sequence Transformer encoder se process hoti hai — bilkul token embeddings ki tarah. [CLS] token ka output image ki representation hoti hai.
Q30 CLIP aur BLIP-2 me kya farak hai?
CLIP image-caption pairs par contrastively train hota hai — sahi pairs ki similarity maximise, galat ki minimise; isse image aur text shared space me aa jate hain. BLIP-2 frozen image encoder aur frozen language model ko Q-Former (Querying Transformer) bridge se jodta hai — learnable query vectors image features se visual tokens banate hain. Bade encoders frozen rehte hain, isliye BLIP-2 train karna bahut sasta hai.
Q31 MTEB kya hai? MNR loss sabse behtar kyun tha?
MTEB (Massive Text Embedding Benchmark) embedding models ka benchmark hai — 8 tasks, 58 datasets, 112 languages. STS task par softmax loss 0.598, cosine loss 0.722, MNR loss 0.809, aur fine-tuned all-MiniLM-L6-v2 0.851 (validation scores). MNR behtar tha kyunki har anchor ke liye batch ke baaki saare examples negatives ban jate hain — efficient aur informative training.
Q32 Augmented SBERT aur TSDAE kab use karte hain?
Jab labelled data kam ho: Augmented SBERT strong cross-encoder se silver labels banata hai (kitab me 10,000 gold + 40,000 silver pairs), phir fast bi-encoder train karta hai — result 0.710. Jab bilkul labelled data na ho: TSDAE unsupervised hai — sentence corrupt karo, encode karo, original reconstruct karo — result 0.699. Dono fully fine-tuned model (0.851) se neeche hain, par data-scarce situations ke practical solutions hain.
Q33 BERT fine-tune karte time layers freeze kyun karte hain?
Freeze karne se wo layers ke weights update nahi hote — training tez hoti hai aur chhote dataset par overfitting kam hota hai. Numbers: full fine-tuning F1 0.85, pehle 10 blocks frozen 0.80, sab frozen 0.63. Early layers general language features seekhte hain jo achhe se transfer hote hain; baad wale layers task-specific hote hain — isliye kuch early layers freeze karne par nuksaan kam hota hai.
Q34 Continued pretraining aur SetFit kya hain?
Continued pretraining: fine-tune se pehle masked language modelling (15% tokens mask) target domain ke text par continue karo — model domain ki vocabulary/style apna leta hai. Example: masked word ke predictions general words (idea, dream) se movie-review words (movie, film) me badal gaye. SetFit: few-shot method — contrastive learning se embeddings fine-tune karo, phir logistic regression. Sirf 16 examples per class se F1 0.836 — full BERT fine-tuning (0.85) ke kareeb.
Q35 NER me label alignment ki problem kya hai? -100 kya hai?
NER token-classification hai — har token ko B-PER/I-PER/B-ORG/I-ORG/B-LOC/I-LOC/B-MISC/I-MISC/O me se label milta hai (CoNLL-2003, kul 9 labels). Problem: ek word kai subword tokens me toot sakta hai, par label sirf pehle subword ko milta hai; baaki ko -100 label diya jata hai, jise loss function ignore karta hai. Fine-tune ke baad model test sentence me naam ko B-PER label deta hai, score 0.9953 ke saath.
Q36 SFT, LoRA, QLoRA, RLHF aur DPO me kya farak hai?
SFT: prompt-completion pairs par next-token loss se train karo — format/style sikhata hai (Dolly 15,000 pairs). LoRA: bade matrix W ke bajaye do chhoti matrices A aur B seekho (r=16) — sirf wohi train, W frozen, inference par merge — sasta aur asar full fine-tuning jaisa. QLoRA: 4-bit base model + LoRA — consumer GPU par bada model. RLHF: SFT → reward model (human rankings) → PPO — powerful par complex aur mehenga. DPO: chosen vs rejected pairs par direct optimisation — RLHF se simple, aksar barabar effective.
