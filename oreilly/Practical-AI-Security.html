Foreword — Dhokha Purana, AI Naya Amplifier

Dhokha koi nayi cheez nahi hai. Nayi cheez ye hai ki AI dhokhe ko scale par kar sakta hai — aur khud bhi dhokha kha sakta hai. Foreword yehi baat ek purani kahani se shuru karta hai.

Do chitrakaron ki kahani

Praachin Greece ki ek mashhoor katha hai: ek chitrakar ne angoor itne asal jaise banaye ki panchhi unhe chugne aa gaye. Doosre chitrakar ne ek parda itna asal jaisa banaya ki pehla chitrakar use hatane ki koshish karne laga — ye dekhne ke liye ki parde ke peeche kya hai. Pehla dhokha ek panchhi ko diya gaya tha; doosra dhokha ek insaan ko diya gaya tha. Aur insaan ko dhokha dena zyada mushkil bhi hai aur zyada nuksaan bhi karta hai.

Foreword ka point ye hai: AI systems ke saath bhi yahi khel hai. Ek system ko dhokha dena (jaise model ko galat classify karwana) aur ek system se insaanon ko dhokha dilwana (jaise deepfake ya manipulation) — dono ek hi sikke ke do pehlu hain, aur dono is kitab ka subject hain.

Ek recent case — data se manipulation

Foreword ek recent case ka zikr karta hai: Cambridge Analytica scandal aur 2016 ke US election. Wahan data-driven persuasion — logon ke data se unke faisle par asar dalna — ne wahi sawaal khade kiye jo is kitab ke centre me hain: jab ek system logon ko dhokha de sakta hai ya unhe manipulate kar sakta hai, to use secure kaise karein?

Ye kitab kis cheez ki practical counterpart hai

Foreword kitab ko is history ka practical counterpart batata hai: ye theory nahi hai ki "kya AI ko dhokha diya ja sakta hai" — ye practical guide hai ki kaise dhokha diya jata hai aur kaise defend kiya jata hai. Kaise ek model ko fool karte hain, kaise data churate hain, kaise prompts se system ko apne against chala lete hain — aur har attack ke saamne defence kya hai.

Yaad rakhne wali baat: Foreword me koi figure, table ya code nahi hai. Iska kaam sirf mindset set karna hai — AI security ko ek nayi field ki tarah nahi, balki dhokhe ki purani kahani ke naye chapter ki tarah dekho.

04Part I — AI and Security Fundamentals

Chapter 1 — AI Kya Hai?

AI ko secure karne se pehle samajhna padega ki AI hai kya. Ye chapter scratch se shuru karta hai — definitions, data, ML ki taxonomy — aur do hands-on models banakar dikhata hai: ek chhota sa perceptron aur ek asli neural network. Ant me ye batata hai ki wahi properties jo model ko useful banati hain, wahi use attackable bhi banati hain.

AI kya hai — aur definition par behes kyu

Artificial Intelligence (AI) kitab me ek umbrella term hai: aise systems jo wo kaam karte hain jo agar insaan kare to intelligence chahiye hoti. Kitab zor dekar kehti hai ki AI ki koi ek agreed definition nahi hai — aur working definition matter karti hai, kyunki wahi tay karta hai ki security ke hisaab se kya "AI" count hoga aur kya nahi.

  • Machine Learning (ML) — AI ka woh subset jisme system hand-written rules follow karne ke bajaye data se patterns seekhta hai.
  • Deep Learning — ML jisme multi-layer neural networks use hote hain.
  • Model — seekha hua artefact: weights/parameters + architecture, jo inputs ko outputs me map karta hai.
  • Data — raw material. Bina data ke koi model nahi. Data ki provenance, quality aur bias seedha model ka behaviour tay karte hain — ye sirf data-science ki baat nahi, security ki baat hai.

Data — sources, formats, datasets, quality

Data sources: public datasets, scraped web data, sensor/IoT data, proprietary company data aur synthetic data. Formats: tabular (rows/columns), text, images, audio aur time-series. Dataset ek curated collection hoti hai jiska defined schema aur train/test split hota hai.

Data quality paanch cheezon se judge hoti hai: accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness aur representativeness. Kharab quality — missing values, mislabelling, skewed sampling — seedha vulnerabilities paida karti hai: poisoning, bias, brittleness. Matlab data ki gandagi baad me security problem ban jaati hai.

Machine Learning ki taxonomy — paanch tarike

  • Supervised learning — labelled input-output pairs se mapping seekho. Classification (discrete label) ya regression (continuous value).
  • Unsupervised learning — bina labels ke data me structure dhoondho: clustering, dimensionality reduction, anomaly detection.
  • Semi-supervised learning — chhota labelled set + bada unlabelled set, dono ka use.
  • Self-supervised learning — model khud apne labels data se banata hai (jaise masked word predict karna). Yahi technique modern foundation models ke peeche hai.
  • Reinforcement learning — agent environment me trial-and-error se seekhta hai, reward/penalty signals se. Exploration (nayi actions try karna) aur exploitation (jo kaam karta hai use karna) ka balance hota hai.

AI ka brief history

  • Early history (1950s) — Dartmouth workshop, machine intelligence test, symbolic AI aur expert systems.
  • First AI Winter (1970s) — zyada waade, kam delivery; funding cut gayi.
  • Second Winter (late 1980s–1990s) — expert systems ke baad phir girawat.
  • Deep learning aur big-model era (~2012 se) — GPUs, bade datasets, deep networks ne wapasi karayi.
  • Generative AI aur foundation models (2017 se) — Transformers, phir GPT-3/ChatGPT, jinhone AI ko specialist tool se general-purpose, widely deployed technology bana diya.

Modern AI ki data bhookh

Modern models ko itna zyada data chahiye ki ye khud ek security problem ban gaya hai: jitna zyada data, utna mushkil use vet karna; utni badi attack surface poisoning aur leakage ke liye; aur model ka behaviour utna hi zyada us data par depend karta hai jise defender ne kabhi inspect hi nahi kiya.

Chapter summary

AI data se seekhta hai; ek model = data + architecture + training. Ek chhota sa model bhi end-to-end samjha ja sakta hai. Aur wahi properties jo models ko useful banati hain — data se seekhna, examples se generalise karna — wahi unhe attackable bhi banati hain. Yahi bridge hai Chapter 1 se Chapter 4 tak.

Chapter 2 — Models Ke Saath Kaam

Model ko attack karne se pehle samajhna padega ki model andar se kaisa hota hai — uski anatomy, training, deep networks, lifecycle aur modern tools. Ye chapter conceptual hai, isme koi hands-on code exercise nahi hai — ye Chapters 4–5 ki taiyaari hai.

Anatomy — architecture, parameters, hyperparameters

  • Architecture — model ki structure: layers aur connections ka arrangement, jaise kitne hidden layers aur units.
  • Parameters (weights aur biases) — wo numbers jo model training ke dauraan seekhta hai; yehi encode karte hain ki usne kya seekha.
  • Hyperparameters — training se pehle set kiye jaane wale numbers jo learning ko control karte hain: learning rate, batch size, epochs ki sankhya, layers ki sankhya, regularisation strength. Ye data se nahi seekhe jaate; practitioner choose karta hai.

Training — data, loss, gradient descent, regularisation

Training data wo labelled (ya unlabelled) set hai jisse model seekhta hai; uski size, balance aur cleanliness bound karti hai ki model kya seekh sakta hai. Loss function measure karta hai ki prediction kitna galat hai — regression ke liye mean squared error, classification ke liye cross-entropy. Optimisation wo search hai jo parameters dhoondhta hai jinse loss minimum ho.

Gradient descent kaam ka tareeka: loss ka gradient compute karo (har parameter ke hisaab se loss kis direction me sabse tez badhta hai), aur learning rate se uski ulti direction me step lo. Stochastic gradient descent (SGD) har step me random mini-batch use karta hai — tez aur noisy. Adam jaise variants har parameter ke liye step size adapt karte hain.

Regularisation koi bhi technique hai jo overfitting ko discourage karti hai: L1/L2 penalties (weights ko shrink karte hain), dropout (training ke dauraan randomly units band kar deta hai), aur early stopping (jab validation performance badhna band ho jaaye to training rok do).

Train/test splits aur cross-validation: Test data wo hissa hai jo model ne training me kabhi nahi dekha — isliye test performance real-world performance ka estimate deti hai. Cross-validation is process ko kai alag held-out folds ke saath repeat karke average karta hai, jo zyada robust estimate deta hai.

Model evaluation — metrics

Classification ke liye: accuracy (kitne sahi) — lekin imbalanced data par misleading ho sakti hai. Precision (predicted positives me se kitne asal me positive), recall (asli positives me se kitne mile), F1 score (precision aur recall ka harmonic mean). Confusion matrix true/false positives aur negatives ko tabulate karta hai. ROC curve aur AUC alag-alag thresholds par true-positive vs false-positive rate ka trade-off summarise karte hain. Regression ke liye mean absolute error aur root mean squared error use hote hain.

Deep neural networks — building blocks

  • Layers: Input layer features leti hai, hidden layers unhe transform karte hain, output layer predictions deti hai. Fully connected (dense) layers har input ko har output unit se jodte hain.
  • Activation functions: Non-linearity laate hain — bina inke deep network ek linear model me collapse ho jaata hai. Sigmoid 0–1 me squash karta hai, tanh −1 se 1 me, ReLU positive values ko waise hi pass karta hai aur negatives ko zero kar deta hai, softmax ek vector ko probability distribution me badal deta hai.
  • RNNs: Sequences ko hidden state ek step se agle step tak pass karke process karte hain — text aur time-series ke liye. Lambi sequences par train karna mushkil hota hai (vanishing gradients); LSTM aur GRU gates add karke lamba yaad rakhte hain.
  • CNNs: Convolutional filters image par slide karke local patterns (edges, textures) detect karte hain; pooling layers down-sample karte hain. Isse translation invariance milti hai aur images par dense network se bahut kam parameters lagte hain.
  • Transformers (2017): Recurrence ki jagah self-attention use karte hain — har token har doosre token ko attend karta hai aur seekhta hai ki kaunsa matter karta hai. Isse parallelisation possible hoti hai, aur yahi architecture modern LLMs ke peeche hai. Attention ko kitab ek weighted sum ki tarah samjhati hai jahan weights data se seekhe jaate hain.

AI system ka lifecycle

  • Development iterative hai: train, evaluate, tune — baar-baar.
  • Testing me sirf accuracy nahi — adversarial aur robustness testing bhi honi chahiye.
  • Deployment ka tareeka attack surface badal deta hai: API serve karna ek query interface expose karta hai; on-device deploy karna weights expose karta hai.
  • Post-deployment monitoring dekhta hai: data drift (inputs samay ke saath badal rahe hain), concept drift (inputs aur outputs ka rishta badal raha hai), performance decay aur abuse. Feedback loops naya data retraining me bhejte hain — jo khud poison ho sakta hai.

Modern AI tools

  • Foundation models — broad data par pre-trained bade models (jaise ek LLM) jinhe kai tasks ke liye adapt kiya ja sakta hai.
  • Fine-tuning — foundation model ko chhote, task-specific dataset par aage train karna. Scratch se train karne se sasta hai, lekin nayi vulnerabilities bhi bake kar sakta hai aur safety training ko undo bhi kar sakta hai.
  • Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) — LLM ke answers ko retrieved documents me ground karta hai. Hallucination kam karta hai, lekin retrieval corpus ek nayi attack surface hai — use poison kiya ja sakta hai.
  • Prompting — model ko input text se steer karna: system prompts (developer ki instructions), user prompts, few-shot examples. Powerful lekin fragile — kyunki model prompt text aur data text ko ek jaise treat karta hai. Yahi prompt injection ki jad hai.
  • Agents — aise systems jisme LLM tools (search, code execution, APIs) ko loop me call karke goal achieve karta hai. Capability bahut badh jaati hai — aur blast radius bhi, kyunki compromised agent actions le sakta hai, sirf text produce nahi karta.

Chapter 3 — AI Threats

Attack karne se pehle threat ko structure me samajhna padega: attack surface kya hai, threat actors kaun hain, threat model kaise banate hain, kaunse frameworks hain, aur risk ko kaise measure karte hain. Ye chapter wahi toolkit deta hai.

Attack surface aur threat actors

Attack surface wo saare points ka sum hai jahan attacker system se interact kar sakta hai: training data, data pipeline, model khud, API/query interface, prompt, retrieval corpus, agent ke tools, aur surrounding infrastructure.

Threat actors ko naam se nahi, capability, resources aur motivation se classify kiya jata hai:

  • Script kiddies — kam skill, off-the-shelf tools.
  • Hacktivists — ideology-driven.
  • Organised criminals — financial motive.
  • Nation-states — resources, patience, strategic goals.
  • Insiders — legitimate access, detect karna sabse mushkil.

Ek hi actor lifecycle ke kai points par dikh sakta hai — isliye actor ko ek jagah band karke mat socho.

Threat modeling — steps aur kya hota hai

Threat model ek structured description hai: kya protect kar rahe ho, kaun attack kar sakta hai, kaise kar sakta hai, aur impact kya hoga. Steps:

  1. System aur assets define karo (data, model, outputs, reputation).
  2. Threat actors aur unke goals identify karo.

Chapter 4 — Attacks and Weaknesses

Ye chapter kitab ka attack core hai: input par attack, output par attack, model aur training data par attack, aur prompts par attack — har ek ke saath hands-on demo. Har attack ke peeche ek hi insight hai: model statistical patterns seekhta hai, human concepts nahi — isliye insaan ko meaningless lagne wala change model ke liye decisive ho sakta hai.

Adversarial lens — attacks kaise kaam karte hain

Adversarial attack ek chhota, aksar imperceptible change hai — input me ya training process me — jo model ka output attacker ki marzi se badal deta hai. Attacks ko classify kiya jata hai:

  • Attacker ke knowledge se: white-box (architecture aur weights tak poora access), black-box (sirf query access), grey-box (partial knowledge).
  • Goal se: targeted (ek specific galat output force karna) ya untargeted (koi bhi galat output).
  • Jagah se: input, output, model, ya training data par.

Evasion attacks — FGSM

Concept: Inference time par input ko perturb karo taaki model misclassify kare, bina human ko kuch alag dikhe. Classic method Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) hai: loss ka gradient input ke respect me lo, uska sign lo, aur use chhote epsilon se scale karke add kar do — ek hi step, us direction me jo loss ko sabse zyada badhata hai. Zyada powerful iterative methods (jaise Projected Gradient Descent) is process ko repeat karte hain aur result ko original ke epsilon ke andar clip karte hain.

Hands-on evasion: Ek classifier ko simple image-like dataset par train kiya jata hai; chhote epsilon ke saath FGSM apply kiya jata hai. Chapter kehti hai attack ka effect accuracy me sharp drop hai — clean baseline se perturbed inputs par low single-figure / near-zero figure tak. Code, step-by-step: model aur data load karo; automatic differentiation se input ke respect me loss ka gradient compute karne wala function define karo; gradient ke sign ko epsilon se multiply karke add karo; valid input range me clip karo; perturbed set par model ko evaluate karo aur accuracy ko pehle aur baad me compare karo.

Adversarial training yahin introduce hoti hai (Chapter 5 me detail me): adversarial examples par bhi train karo, taaki model unke prati robust ho. Iska trade-off — clean accuracy vs robust accuracy — yahin flag kiya jata hai.

Backdoor attacks — trigger wala zeher

Concept: Attacker training data ka ek chhota fraction poison karta hai — ek trigger (jaise image ke corner me ek chhota pattern) ke saath, target class ka label lagakar. Model clean data par normally seekhta hai, lekin trigger wala koi bhi input target class me classify hota hai. Trigger hi key hai: normal use me invisible, aur demand par activate.

Hands-on backdoor: Ek chhote image classifier ko aise dataset par train kiya jata hai jisme kuch percent training images par trigger pattern aur target label hai. Phir model ko clean images par test kiya jata hai (accuracy high rehti hai) aur triggered images par (lagbhag sab target class me classify hote hain). Numbers: clean accuracy baseline ke paas rehti hai, jabki triggered inputs par attack success rate 90% se upar hai printed run me. Code, step-by-step: trigger banao (pixels ka chhota patch); training examples ka subset select karo, patch apply karo aur relabel karo; mixed data par model train karo; clean aur triggered test sets par alag-alag evaluate karo.

Privacy attacks — membership inference, extraction, inversion

Membership inference: Model aur ek data point diye gaye hon, to decide karo ki wo point training set me tha ya nahi. Attack overfitting ka fayda uthata hai: model un points par zyada confident hota hai (kam loss) jo usne dekhe hain. Attacker shadow models train karta hai — aise models jo attacker ke control wale data par train hote hain, kuch me target point hota hai aur kuch me nahi — aur ek classifier seekhta hai jo model ke confidence scores se "member" aur "non-member" ko alag karta hai.

Hands-on membership inference: Ek model dataset par train hota hai; shadow models banaye jaate hain; unke outputs par attack classifier train hota hai; phir target model par evaluate kiya jata hai. Result: attack chance (50%) se clearly behtar karta hai, aur uski accuracy target model ke overfitting ke saath badhti hai — chapter isse privacy risk ko seedha Chapter 1 ke overfitting se jodta hai.

Model extraction: API se baar-baar query karke model ko "churana". Attacker kai inputs bhejta hai, outputs (labels ya probabilities) collect karta hai, aur un input-output pairs par ek copy (surrogate) train karta hai. Kaafi queries ke baad copy original ko approximate kar leta hai, decision boundaries samet. Defences (rate-limiting, output rounding, watermarking) yahan preview ki jaati hain aur Chapter 5 me cover hoti hain.

Model inversion: Model se training data reconstruct karna. Model (ya uske outputs) ka access lekar, attacker ek input ko optimise karta hai taaki target class ke liye model ka confidence maximum ho — aur resulting input us class ke training example jaisa dikhne lagta hai. Classic demonstration: face-recognition model se chehre reconstruct karna.

Hands-on model inversion: Ek chhote classifier ko random noise se shuru karke invert kiya jata hai — target class ke output par baar-baar gradient ascent se input update kiya jata hai; resulting image us class ki training images se recognisably similar hoti hai. Chapter zor deta hai ki ye tab sabse achha kaam karta hai jab model ne apna training data memorise kar liya ho.

Data poisoning aur side-channel attacks

Data poisoning: Training data ko corrupt karo taaki model galat seekhe. Variants: label flipping (labels badal do), data injection (malicious examples add karo), aur clean-label poisoning (examples jo insaan ko sahi labelled lagen, lekin model ki decision boundary shift kar dein). Attacker ko data ka sirf ek chhota fraction chahiye — chapter ka example kuch percent use karta hai — ek specific subpopulation ya trigger par bada effect daalne ke liye.

Hands-on poisoning: Training labels ka ek fraction flip kiya jata hai (ya malicious examples add kiye jaate hain); model retrain hota hai; targeted class par uski accuracy sharply gir jaati hai jabki overall accuracy barely hilti hai — isliye attack aggregate metrics se spot karna mushkil hota hai.

Poisoned models in the wild: Chapter real incidents survey karta hai jahan models ko wild me poison ya backdoor kiya gaya — public repositories se distribute hue poisoned datasets aur compromised pre-trained models samet. Lesson: supply chain (data/model kisne banaya, kahan se download kiya) attack surface ka hissa hai.

Side-channel attacks: Model ke outputs se nahi, physical ya timing behaviour se secret information infer karo — inference me kitna time laga, kitni power kharch hui, cache behaviour, memory access pattern. Ye model ya uske data ke baare me leak karte hain, tab bhi jab API achhe se protected ho.

Hands-on side-channel: Chapter do classes ke inputs par inference time karta hai aur distributions compare karta hai. Numbers, jaise printed hain: code ke printed averages 0.000438 seconds ek class ke liye aur 0.000432 seconds doosri ke liye.

⚠ Kitab me inconsistency — Ch 4, side-channel timings: Chapter ke prose me isi experiment ke liye alag figures diye gaye hain — lagbhag 0.000251 aur 0.000244 seconds — jo printed output se match nahi karte. Dono sets record kiye gaye hain. Kisi bhi set me point wahi hai: ek chhota lekin systematic timing difference hai jo kaafi samples se detectable hai, aur class information leak karne ke liye kaafi hai.

Prompt injection aur jailbreaking

Prompt injection: LLM instructions aur data me reliably farak nahi kar sakta, kyunki dono text ke roop me aate hain. Attacker instructions ko aise data me embed karta hai jo model padhega (web page, document, email, tool output), aur model unhe aise follow karta hai jaise wo user ya developer se aayi hon. Direct injection user ke apne prompt me hoti hai; indirect injection retrieved ya tool-returned content me hoti hai — agents aur RAG systems ke liye zyada khatarnak form.

Hands-on prompt injection: Ek chhote RAG/agent-style demo ko ek document diya jata hai jisme hidden instruction hai (jaise secret reveal karo, ya user ki request ignore karo); model injected instruction ko follow karta hai. Chapter dikhata hai ki data me ek polite, well-formed instruction hi kaafi hai — code execution ya special formatting ki zaroorat nahi.

Jailbreaking: Model ki safety training ko bypass karna (harmful requests par refuse karne ki training) — request ko aise frame karke ki safety behaviour trigger na ho: role-play, hypothetical scenarios, request ko encode karna, multi-turn gradual escalation, ya model se aisi persona adopt karwana jo comply kare. Chapter jailbreaking (model ko wo karwana jo use na karne ki training di gayi) ko prompt injection (galat source ki instructions follow karwana) se alag karta hai — aur note karta hai ki practice me dono overlap karte hain.

Hands-on jailbreaking: Kai jailbreak prompts ek guarded model ke khilaf try kiye jaate hain; chapter batata hai kaunse succeed hote hain aur kaunse refuse hote hain, aur note karta hai ki success brittle hai — ek model/version par kaam karne wala jailbreak aksar doosre par fail hota hai, aur models ko known jailbreaks ke khilaf patch kiya jata hai. Ye ek arms race hai.

Chapter 5 — Defenses, Controls, and Mitigations

Attack samajh liya — ab defence. Is chapter ka central claim hai: koi single defence sab attacks nahi rokti. Defence systemic honi chahiye — layers, jo ek fail ho to doosri pakde. Aur har defence ki keemat hoti hai: accuracy, cost ya complexity me.

Defense in depth

Layers: data validation, robust training, input filtering, output filtering, monitoring, access controls aur incident response — ek saath apply kiye jaate hain, taaki ek fail ho to doosri attack ko pakde.

Evasion se defence — numbers ke saath

Adversarial training (adversarial examples par bhi train karo) sabse effective empirical defence hai. Chapter ke hands-on numbers: ek model jo adversarial examples par 5.6% accurate tha, adversarial training ke baad 22% tak pahunchta hai — bada relative improvement, lekin clean accuracy se abhi bhi bahut door. Yahi robustness-accuracy trade-off hai.

Gradient obfuscation / gradient masking (gradients ko compute karna mushkil banao, jaise non-differentiable preprocessing se) fail hoti hai: apply karne ke baad adversarial examples par accuracy sirf 6.25% aur 3.12% rehti hai do reported variants me — attack adapt karke obfuscation ko bypass kar leta hai. Isliye ye defence false confidence deti hai.

Input preprocessing (denoising, compression, randomisation) kuch attacks ke khilaf madad karta hai, lekin ye bhi bypassable hai.

Randomized smoothing aur certified robustness

Randomized smoothing: Input ko seedha classify nahi karte — uske kai noisy versions (Gaussian noise add karke) par model ki predictions ka average lete hain; majority vote zyada stable hota hai, aur method ek provable radius deta hai jiske andar prediction badal nahi sakti.

Hands-on smoothing numbers: Ek smoothed classifier ki adversarial examples par accuracy 63.15% (unsmoothed, chapter ki comparison me) se badhkar 65.54% (smoothed) ho jaati hai — modest gain. Chapter zor deta hai ki gain noise level par depend karta hai aur smoothing clean accuracy ko robustness se trade karti hai.

Certified robustness: Ek guarantee, sirf empirical result nahi — ki stated bound ke andar koi bhi perturbation model ki prediction nahi badal sakti. Methods me interval bound propagation (IBP) hai, jo interval bounds ko network ke through propagate karta hai. Numbers: IBP-trained model ki clean accuracy 86.5% hai aur certified accuracy 68% — matlab 68% test inputs ke liye model certified radius ke andar provably robust hai. 86.5% aur 68% ke beech ka gap guarantee ki keemat hai.

Ensemble methods: Kai models ko combine karo (voting ya averaging se) taaki attacker ko ek saath sabko fool karna pade. Diversity matter karti hai — similar models ke ensembles kam madad karte hain, diverse models ke zyada.

Distillation: Ek chhote "student" model ko bade "teacher" model ke soft outputs (probabilities, sirf hard labels nahi) mimic karne ke liye train karo. Defensive distillation ko kabhi defence ke roop me propose kiya gaya tha kyunki ye decision surface ko smooth karta hai, lekin chapter kehta hai ki adaptive attacks ke khilaf ye largely ineffective hai — compression ke liye useful hai, akele security control ke roop me nahi.

Baaki attacks se defence

  • Poisoning aur backdoors se: Training data ko validate aur sanitise karo (outlier detection, provenance checks, deduplication); robust training use karo (loss functions aur aggregation jo kisi ek example ka influence limit karein); pre-trained models aur datasets ko use se pehle audit karo; candidate triggers se probe karke backdoors test karo; production me trigger pattern monitor karo.
  • Data extraction se: API jo reveal karta hai use limit karo (full probabilities ke bajaye labels do; confidence scores ko round karo ya noise add karo); extraction patterns ke liye queries ko rate-limit aur monitor karo (kai systematic queries); model outputs ko watermark karo; training ke dauraan differential privacy use karo.
  • Prompt injection se: Retrieved/tool content ko untrusted data mano, instructions nahi; instruction hierarchies use karo (system > developer > user > tool) aur enforce karo; untrusted content ko delimit karo (explicit tags ke saath aur instruction ke saath ki andar ki instructions ignore karo); tool outputs ko act karne se pehle validate karo; agents ki tool permissions minimal rakho (least privilege).
  • Jailbreaking se: Safety training up to date rakho; input aur output classifiers/guardrails use karo jo harmful requests aur responses ko screen karein; known jailbreak collections ke khilaf test karo; production me novel jailbreak patterns monitor karo.

Other mitigations

  • Differential privacy — training ke dauraan calibrated noise add karo taaki koi single training example infer na ho sake. Privacy budget (epsilon) trade-off control karta hai.
  • Federated learning — on-device train karo, sirf updates share karo, taaki raw data device se bahar na jaaye — lekin updates khud information leak kar sakte hain aur poison ho sakte hain.
  • Homomorphic encryption aur secure multi-party computation — encrypted data par compute karo, taaki server ko plaintext kabhi na dikhe. Powerful lekin slow aur complex.
  • Model watermarking — outputs me aisa mark jo baad me identify ho sake.

Arms race, human in the loop, trust aur risk

Adversarial arms race: Har defence ko eventually attack kiya jata hai, aur har attack ko eventually defend kiya jata hai. Chapter kehta hai ki is cycle ke liye design karo (monitor, update, re-test), kisi final, permanent fix ke liye nahi.

Human in the loop: High-stakes ya high-uncertainty decisions me insaan ko rakho — aur insaan ke paas asal authority aur model ko overrule karne ki jaankari honi chahiye, warna "human in the loop" sirf theatre hai. Automation bias — insaan ka model par zyada bharosa — khud ek vulnerability hai.

Trust aur risk / privacy aur data protection: Trust transparency se banta hai (kaunsa data use hua, model ko kaise test kiya, uski limits kya hain) aur accountability se. Privacy data minimisation, purpose limitation, consent aur technical measures (differential privacy, federated learning) se protect hoti hai. Tension ye hai: model ko jitna zyada data chahiye, privacy utni hi mushkil hoti hai.

Defenses in the wild — teen case studies

  • ID.me — identity verification jo AI (document aur face matching) ko human review aur liveness checks ke saath combine karta hai. Accuracy aur bias par public scrutiny ka saamna karna pada, jisne system ke governance aur audit ke tareeke badal diye.
  • MathGPT — maths-tutoring LLM deployment jinki defences me guardrails, content filtering aur students ke prompt-injection attempts (answers nikalne ya tutor ko jailbreak karne ki koshish) ki monitoring shamil hai. Case helpfulness (tutor ko explain karna chahiye) aur safety (use homework nahi karna chahiye) ke beech ka trade-off dikhata hai.
  • Cylance — AI-based endpoint security product jo malware detect karne ke liye machine learning use karta tha. Usko khud attack kiya gaya (adversarial examples jo detection se bach gaye) — case dikhata hai ki ML par bana security product ML ki vulnerabilities inherit karta hai aur use ek ML system ki tarah defend karna padta hai, sirf ek security product ki tarah nahi.

Testing batata hai ki system wo karta hai jo use karna chahiye. Red teaming poochhta hai: kya attacker use wo karwa sakta hai jo use nahi karna chahiye? Red teaming ki basics — testing, testing, aur testing

Red teaming adversarial testing hai: ek team attacker ki tarah act karti hai, aur goal hota hai vulnerabilities dhoondhna — pehle ki asal attacker dhoondhe. Ye routine testing se alag hai kyunki ye adversarial, creative aur goal-oriented hai (andar jaane ka raasta dhoondho), na ki ye check karna ki specified requirements poori ho rahi hain.

Chapter ka refrain hai: testing, testing, aur more testing — model ko har stage par test karna chahiye, aur red teaming us testing ka sabse adversarial form hai.

AI red teaming kya hai?

AI systems par red teaming: model aur uske aas-paas ke poore system (prompts, RAG, tools, APIs, guardrails) ko probe karna — prompt injection, jailbreaks, data leakage, bias, hallucination, excessive agency aur doosri failures ke liye. Ye AI system ko uske deployment context me scope karta hai, model ko isolation me nahi — akele safe model tools milne par unsafe ho sakta hai.

Red teaming ek process hai — 6 steps

  1. Scoping — kya scope me hai (kaunsa model, kaunsi deployment, kaunse tools), kya scope se bahar hai, rules of engagement (red team kya kar sakti hai aur kya nahi — jaise real users ke data par attack nahi), aur goals (kaunse harms dhoondhne hain).
  2. Designing — attack types aur scenarios choose karo, test plan aur prompts/attacks ka set banao, aur decide karo ki success kaise judge hogi.
  3. Building — environment, tooling aur attack artefacts taiyaar karo, aur team ko brief karo.
  4. Running — attacks execute karo, defences respond karein to adapt karo, aur sab kuch record karo (prompts, outputs, timestamps, model version).
  5. Reporting — har finding ko severity, reproducibility (exact prompt aur steps), impact aur recommended fix ke saath likho; risk ke hisaab se prioritise karo.
  6. Evaluating — review karo ki exercise ne goals meet kiye ya nahi, findings fix hui aur re-test hui ya nahi, aur agli baar kya badalna hai.

Automated red teaming

Ek AI system se doosre par attack karwana: ek attacker LLM jailbreaks, prompt injections ya adversarial inputs scale par generate karta hai, aur ek judge model score karta hai ki target ka response failure tha ya nahi. Ye tez aur broad hai, lekin novel, human-creative attacks miss kar sakta hai aur false positives de sakta hai. Chapter ise human red teaming ka complement batata hai, replacement nahi.

Point ye hai: real exercise me findings aise hi milte hain — ek hi system me kai alag-alag tarah ki kamzoriyaan, ek saath.

Human vs machine

Human red teamers creative hote hain, context aur social engineering samajhte hain, aur aise harms spot kar sakte hain jo automated system try karne ka sochega bhi nahi. Machines tez, tireless aur systematic hoti hain. Best practice hybrid hai: automated red teaming breadth aur regression testing ke liye, human red teaming depth aur novelty ke liye.

Chapter 7 — Attacking and Defending with AI

Ab tak AI target tha. Is chapter me AI tool hai — defenders ke haath me bhi, attackers ke haath me bhi. Wahi capabilities (scale par pattern recognition, human-like text generate karna, repetitive kaam automate karna) dono sides ki madad karti hain. Advantage use milta hai jo unhe behtar deploy karta hai — kisi ek side ko permanently nahi.

AI se defence — IDS, SIEM, traffic, scanning, phishing defence

  • Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) — network ya host activity monitor karke anomalies ya known attack patterns flag karte hain. ML-based IDS seekhta hai ki normal traffic kaisa dikhta hai aur deviations flag karta hai — novel attacks pakad leta hai, lekin false positives bhi deta hai.
  • Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) — kai sources se logs collect karke correlate karta hai aur alerts prioritise karta hai. AI alerts ki baadh ko triage karne me aur sources ke paar aise patterns spot karne me madad karta hai jo insaan miss kar de.
  • Network traffic analysis — ML models traffic classify karte hain, exfiltration detect karte hain aur command-and-control channels spot karte hain.
  • Vulnerability scanning — AI prioritize karta hai ki kaunsi vulnerabilities matter karti hain (exploitability aur asset value ke hisaab se) aur code me vulnerabilities dhoondh sakta hai.
  • Social engineering aur phishing defence — AI phishing emails detect karta hai (language, headers, links aur sender behaviour se) aur realistic simulations se users ko train karta hai.

AI se attack — tez, scalable, personalised

AI-powered attacks manual attacks se tez, zyada scalable aur zyada personalised hote hain — attacker hazaaron personalised phishing emails generate kar sakta hai, malware ko automatically adapt kar sakta hai, aur reconnaissance automate kar sakta hai. Chapter 8 — AI Safety

AI security poochhti hai: kya system ko attack kiya ja sakta hai? AI safety poochhti hai: kya system deploy karna safe hai, aur use safe kaise banayein? Dono overlap karte hain — ek unsafe model exploit ho sakta hai, aur ek insecure model unsafe banaya ja sakta hai — lekin dono ek nahi hain.

Introduction — ek unsettled field

AI safety wo field hai jo poochhti hai ki AI systems deploy karne ke liye safe hain ya nahi aur unhe safe kaise banaya jaaye. Field unsettled hai: "safe" ki koi agreed definition nahi hai, sabse bade risks ki koi agreed list nahi hai, aur timelines par bhi disagreement hai — aur is par bhi ki main risks near-term hain (bias, misuse, accidents) ya long-term (loss of control).

Terminology — terms aur definitions

  • Risk — likelihood × severity.
  • Hazard — potential harm ka source.
  • Harm — asal damage.
  • Alignment — model ke goals ko human intentions se match karna.
  • Robustness — unexpected inputs par achha behave karna.
  • Interpretability — samajhna ki model ne wo kyu kiya jo kiya.
  • Corrigibility — model ki wo property jo use operators dwara correct ya shut down karne deti hai.

AI ke risks — near-term aur existential

Near-term risks: bias aur discrimination, misinformation, privacy loss, accidents (safety-critical setting me model ka fail hona), aur misuse (model ko harm ke tool ki tarah use karna).

Existential risk / existential threats: Aise risks jo human extinction ya humanity ke potential ko permanently curtail kar sakte hain. Chapter un arguments ko present karta hai jo inhe seriously lene ke liye diye jaate hain (badhti capability, loss of control, instrumental goals) aur counter-arguments ko bhi (ki ye speculative hain, aur present harms se dhyaan bhatkate hain) — bina kisi ek side ko liye.

  • Formal verification — mathematically prove karna ki model ek safety property satisfy karta hai (jaise uska output ek region ke saare inputs ke liye ek bound ke andar rehta hai). Jahan apply hota hai powerful hai, lekin bade models par scale nahi hota.
  • Mechanistic interpretability — model ko reverse-engineer karke uski internal computations samajhna: kaunse neurons/circuits kaunse functions implement karte hain — taaki uska behaviour predict aur debug kiya ja sake.
  • AI alignment — models ko wo goals pursue karwana jo operators intend karte hain, na ki wo proxy jo un goals se diverge kar jaaye.

Alignment — human values ke saath

Alignment problem isliye paida hota hai kyunki model ka training objective (jaise next word predict karna, ya reward maximise karna) sirf ek proxy hai uske liye jo insaan asal me chahte hain — aur ek kaafi capable model proxy ko aise optimise kar sakta hai jo underlying intent ko violate kare. Ise reward hacking ya specification gaming kehte hain.

Alignment techniques:

  • Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) — outputs ki human rankings se ek reward model train karo, phir model ko us reward ke against optimise karo.
  • Constitutional AI — model ko principles ka ek set do aur use apne outputs ko unke against critique aur revise karne do.
  • Oversight techniques (debate, recursive reward modelling) — ek AI doosre AI ke kaam ko evaluate karne me insaan ki madad kare.

Chapter zor deta hai ki alignment solved nahi hai: RLHF sycophantic ya deceptive behaviour paida kar sakta hai, aur ek model aligned dikhte hue bhi andar se ek alag goal pursue kar sakta hai (deceptive alignment).

AI evaluations (evals)

Evaluations model ki capabilities aur safety properties ke standardised tests hain — jaise dangerous capabilities ke tests (kya ye hathiyaar banane me madad kar sakta hai?), deception, sycophancy, robustness. Ye deployment se pehle aur updates ke baad chalaye jaate hain. Unki limits: model ko ek known test pass karne ke liye train kiya ja sakta hai, aur ek test aisi capability miss kar sakta hai jo sirf doosre context me dikhe.

Human-AI collaboration

Safety sirf model ki property nahi hai, human-AI system ki property hai: model uncertainty kaise present karta hai, insaan uske kaam ko kaise check karta hai, aur jab combined system fail ho to responsibility kisko milegi. Acchi collaboration design — calibrated confidence, explanations, easy override — khud ek safety control hai.

Yaad rakhne wali baat: Safety aur security alag hain lekin jude hue hain. Ek model jo safe hai lekin insecure hai, use unsafe banaya ja sakta hai — aur ek model jo secure hai lekin unsafe hai, wo apne aap harm kar sakta hai.

Chapter 9 — AI Governance

Principles of AI governance

  • Transparency — decisions aur data documented aur explainable hon.
  • Fairness — systems discriminate na karein.
  • Privacy — data protected ho.
  • Safety aur security — systems tested aur defended hon.

class Perceptron:
    def __init__(self, n_inputs, lr=0.1):
        self.weights = [0.0] * n_inputs
        self.bias = 0.0
        self.lr = lr

    def predict(self, x):
        total = sum(w * xi for w, xi in zip(self.weights, x)) + self.bias
        return 1 if total >= 0 else 0

    def train(self, X, y, epochs=50):
        for _ in range(epochs):
            for xi, yi in zip(X, y):
                error = yi - self.predict(xi)
                for j in range(len(self.weights)):
                    self.weights[j] += self.lr * error * xi[j]
                self.bias += self.lr * error

Interview FAQs — Practical AI Security

  
Current-era interviews me AI security se poochhe jaane wale sabse kaam ke 36 sawaal — har jawab wahi hai jo is SPA ke chapters me detail me samjhaya gaya hai. Kisi bhi sawaal par click karke jawab kholo.

  

  
Q01 AI security traditional cybersecurity se kaise alag hai?
Traditional security deterministic systems ko protect karti hai — same input, same output. AI systems non-deterministic hote hain, data se seekhte hain, aur unke failure modes alag hain: hallucination, prompt injection, data poisoning, model extraction. Isliye sirf firewall aur access control kaafi nahi — model ko kaise seekhta hai, kaise fail hota hai, ye samajhna padta hai, aur poora system (data, pipeline, model, API, prompt, tools) ek saath defend karna padta hai.

  
Q02 Machine learning, deep learning aur AI me kya farak hai?
AI umbrella term hai — aise systems jo wo kaam karte hain jo insaan kare to intelligence chahiye. Machine learning AI ka subset hai jisme system hand-written rules ke bajaye data se patterns seekhta hai. Deep learning ML ka subset hai jo multi-layer neural networks use karta hai. Model seekha hua artefact hai: weights + architecture, jo inputs ko outputs me map karta hai.

  
Q03 Perceptron kya hai aur wo kya nahi seekh sakta?
Perceptron sabse simple neural unit hai: weighted inputs ka sum + bias, phir step activation se binary output. Learning error-driven hai — galat prediction par weights ko learning rate se nudge kiya jata hai. Ye sirf linearly separable patterns seekh sakta hai. Chapter 1 ke 10-movie dataset me ek contradiction hai (row 5 vs rows 1 aur 9), isliye perfect learning impossible hai — yehi perceptron ki limit dikhata hai.

  
Q04 Overfitting kya hai aur use kaise pehchante hain?
Overfitting tab hota hai jab model training data ko itna yaad kar le ki naye data par kharab perform kare. Pehchan: training accuracy badhti rehti hai jabki validation/test accuracy atak jaati hai ya girne lagti hai — dono curves ke beech ka gap. Wine model me 20 epochs ke baad test loss ≈ 1.51 aur accuracy ≈ 59% thi. Fixes: regularisation (L1/L2, dropout), early stopping, zyada data, cross-validation.

  
Q05 Parameters aur hyperparameters me kya farak hai?
Parameters (weights aur biases) wo numbers hain jo model training ke dauraan data se seekhta hai. Hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, epochs, layers ki sankhya, regularisation strength) training se pehle insaan set karta hai aur ye learning ko control karte hain. Galat hyperparameter — jaise bahut zyada learning rate ya zero regularisation — model ko untrainable ya insecure bana sakta hai.

  
Q06 Gradient descent kaise kaam karta hai?
Loss function batata hai ki prediction kitni galat hai. Gradient descent loss ka gradient compute karta hai — har parameter ke hisaab se loss kis direction me sabse tez badhta hai — aur learning rate jitna step uski ulti direction me leta hai, minimum ki taraf. Stochastic gradient descent har step me random mini-batch use karta hai (tez, noisy). Adam jaise variants har parameter ka step size adapt karte hain.

  
Q07 RAG kya hai aur uski attack surface kya hai?
Retrieval-Augmented Generation me query embed hoti hai, relevant passages vector store se fetch hote hain, aur context ke roop me LLM ko diye jaate hain — answer grounded hota hai, hallucination kam hota hai. Lekin retrieval corpus ek nayi attack surface hai: use poison kiya ja sakta hai, aur retrieved content me hidden instructions (indirect prompt injection) ho sakti hain jo model follow kar lega.

  
Q08 AI agents normal LLM apps se zyada risky kyu hote hain?
Agent me LLM tools ko loop me call karke goal achieve karta hai — search, code execution, APIs, payments. Isse blast radius badh jaata hai: compromised agent sirf galat text nahi deta, real-world actions leta hai. Isliye agent security me tool permissions (least privilege), oversight, sandboxing aur audit trails central hain — ye Chapter 10 ka sabse bada future risk hai.

  
Q09 Attack surface kya hoti hai? AI system ki attack surface me kya-kya aata hai?
Attack surface wo saare points ka sum hai jahan attacker system se interact kar sakta hai. AI ke liye: training data, data pipeline, model khud (weights), API/query interface, prompt, retrieval corpus, agent ke tools, aur surrounding infrastructure. Har point ek entry point hai — isliye defence sirf model par nahi, poore lifecycle par lagani padti hai.

  
Q10 Threat modeling kya hai? Uske steps kya hain?
Threat model ek structured description hai: kya protect kar rahe ho, kaun attack kar sakta hai, kaise, aur impact kya hoga. Steps: (1) system aur assets define karo, (2) threat actors aur goals identify karo, (3) threats aur attack paths enumerate karo, (4) likelihood aur impact assess karo, (5) mitigations choose karo aur residual risk accept karo, (6) system badle to revisit karo. Accha threat model living document hota hai, one-off report nahi.

  
Q11 MITRE ATLAS kya hai?
MITRE ATLAS (Adversarial Threat Landscape for Artificial-Intelligence Systems) AI-specific adversary tactics aur techniques ka knowledge base hai, MITRE ATT&CK ki tarah modelled. Ye lifecycle stages se organised hai: reconnaissance, resource development, initial access, ML model access, execution, persistence, defence evasion, discovery, collection, exfiltration, impact. Threat model banane ke liye system ke components ko ATLAS tactics se map kiya jata hai.

  
Q12 OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications me kaunse risks hain?
Dus risks: prompt injection, insecure output handling, training-data poisoning, model denial of service, supply-chain vulnerabilities, sensitive-information disclosure, insecure plugin design, excessive agency, overreliance, aur model theft. Ye ek prioritisation ke liye common language hai, poora security programme nahi — architecture, testing aur governance bhi chahiye.

  
Q13 MAESTRO framework kya hai?
MAESTRO agentic AI ke liye threat-modelling framework hai jo threats ko saat layers me map karta hai: foundation models, data operations, agent frameworks, deployment/infrastructure, evaluation/observability, security/compliance, aur agent ecosystem. Point ye hai ki agent ka risk sirf model me nahi, poore stack me hai.

  
Q14 CVSS AI vulnerabilities ke liye kaafi kyu nahi hai?
CVSS traditional vulnerabilities ko 0.0–10.0 score karta hai (exploitability, impact, scope). Lekin ye AI ke data-dependent, probabilistic aur emergent harms ko achhe se capture nahi karta — jaise ek model ka behaviour data par depend karta hai aur har input par alag hota hai. AIVSS AI-specific factors add karta hai (model type, data sensitivity, behaviour change ka scope), lekin akele wo bhi kaafi nahi — score ko triage aid ki tarah use karo, threat model aur testing ke saath.

  
Q15 FGSM attack kaise kaam karta hai?
Fast Gradient Sign Method ek single-step evasion attack hai: loss ka gradient input ke respect me lo, uska sign lo, aur chhote epsilon se scale karke input me add kar do — x_adv = x + ε × sign(∇loss). Ye ek step loss ko sabse zyada badhane wali direction me hota hai. Result: model misclassify karta hai jabki insaan ko input ek jaisa dikhta hai. Iterative version (PGD) is process ko repeat karke aur clip karke aur powerful hota hai.

  
Q16 Backdoor attack kya hai? Ye evasion se kaise alag hai?
Backdoor attack training time par hota hai: attacker training data ka kuch percent poison karta hai — ek trigger pattern (jaise image ke corner me chhota patch) ke saath target class ka label. Model clean data par normal kaam karta hai, lekin trigger wala koi bhi input target class me classify hota hai. Evasion inference time par input perturb karta hai; backdoor training data ko corrupt karta hai. Printed run me triggered inputs par attack success 90% se upar thi, clean accuracy normal rehti hai.

  
Q17 Membership inference attack kaise kaam karta hai?
Attacker ye decide karta hai ki ek data point model ki training me tha ya nahi. Ye overfitting ka fayda uthata hai: model dekhe hue points par zyada confident hota hai (kam loss). Attacker shadow models train karta hai — kuch me target point hota hai, kuch me nahi — aur unke confidence scores se ek classifier seekhta hai jo member ko non-member se alag karta hai. Attack chance (50%) se clearly behtar karta hai, aur overfitting badhne par aur accurate hota hai.

  
Q18 Model extraction aur model inversion me kya farak hai?
Model extraction me attacker API ko baar-baar query karke outputs collect karta hai aur un input-output pairs par ek copy (surrogate) train karta hai — model ko chura leta hai. Model inversion me attacker model se training data reconstruct karta hai: random noise se shuru karke gradient ascent se input ko optimise karta hai taaki target class ka confidence maximum ho — result training example jaisa dikhta hai. Dono privacy/confidentiality ke attacks hain, lekin ek model ko copy karta hai, doosra data ko.

  
Q19 Data poisoning ke types kya hain? Kitna data corrupt karna kaafi hota hai?
Teen variants: label flipping (labels badal do), data injection (malicious examples add karo), aur clean-label poisoning (examples jo insaan ko sahi lagen lekin decision boundary shift kar dein). Sirf kuch percent data kaafi hota hai ek specific subpopulation ya trigger par bada effect daalne ke liye — chapter ka example kuch percent use karta hai. Aur ek cited study me sirf 50 false statements training corpus me daalna kaafi tha model ko unhe repeat karwane ke liye.

  
Q20 Side-channel attack kya hai? ML me example do.
Side-channel attack outputs se nahi, physical ya timing behaviour se secret information nikaalta hai — inference time, power consumption, cache behaviour, memory access pattern. Chapter ke hands-on me do classes ke inputs par inference time kiya gaya: printed averages 0.000438s aur 0.000432s (prose me 0.000251s aur 0.000244s — mismatch flagged). Chhota lekin systematic difference kaafi samples se detectable hai aur class information leak karta hai.

  
Q21 Prompt injection kya hai? Direct aur indirect me kya farak hai?
Prompt injection tab hoti hai jab LLM data me chhupi instructions ko follow kar le, kyunki wo instructions aur data me farak nahi kar sakta — dono text hain. Direct injection user ke apne prompt me hoti hai. Indirect injection retrieved ya tool-returned content me hoti hai (web page, document, email) — agents aur RAG systems ke liye zyada khatarnak, kyunki model woh content khud padhta hai. Ek polite, well-formed instruction hi kaafi hai — code execution ki zaroorat nahi.

  
Q22 Jailbreaking aur prompt injection me kya farak hai?
Jailbreaking model ki safety training ko bypass karta hai — use wo karwana jo use na karne ki training di gayi hai (jaise harmful request ka jawab). Prompt injection model ko galat source ki instructions follow karwata hai. Techniques overlap karti hain (role-play, hypothetical scenarios, encoding, multi-turn escalation, persona), lekin goal alag hai: ek refusal ko todta hai, doosra instruction source ko badalta hai. Jailbreak success brittle hoti hai — ek model par kaam kare, doosre par fail ho sakta hai.

  
Q23 Adversarial training kya hai aur uska trade-off kya hai?
Adversarial training me model ko clean examples ke saath adversarial examples par bhi train kiya jata hai, taaki wo unke prati robust ho. Ye sabse effective empirical defence hai. Chapter ke numbers: adversarial accuracy 5.6% se badhkar 22% ho jaati hai — bada relative improvement, lekin clean accuracy se bahut door. Yahi robustness-accuracy trade-off hai: robust bano to clean performance girti hai.

  
Q24 Gradient masking kyu fail hoti hai?
Gradient masking gradients ko compute karna mushkil banati hai (jaise non-differentiable preprocessing se), taaki attacker gradient-based attack na chala sake. Lekin attack adapt kar leta hai — gradients ko approximate karke ya black-box attack se bypass kar deta hai. Chapter ke numbers: masking ke baad adversarial accuracy sirf 6.25% aur 3.12% rehti hai do variants me. Isliye ye false confidence deti hai — lagta hai secure ho, hote nahi.

  
Q25 Randomized smoothing aur certified robustness me kya farak hai?
Randomized smoothing empirical defence hai: input ke kai noisy versions par predictions ka average lo, majority vote lo — adversarial accuracy 63.15% se 65.54% ho jaati hai, modest gain. Certified robustness ek guarantee hai: IBP jaise methods prove karte hain ki stated bound ke andar koi perturbation prediction nahi badal sakti. IBP model ki clean accuracy 86.5% aur certified accuracy 68% thi — 68% inputs ke liye provable robustness, aur gap guarantee ki keemat hai.

  
Q26 Prompt injection se defend kaise karte hain?
Paanch tareeke: (1) retrieved/tool content ko untrusted data mano, instructions nahi; (2) instruction hierarchy enforce karo — system > developer > user > tool; (3) untrusted content ko delimit karo (explicit tags + instruction ki andar ki instructions ignore karo); (4) tool outputs ko act karne se pehle validate karo; (5) agents ki tool permissions minimal rakho (least privilege). Koi bhi ek kaafi nahi — defence in depth chahiye.

  
Q27 Differential privacy aur federated learning kya hain?
Differential privacy training ke dauraan calibrated noise add karti hai taaki koi single training example infer na ho sake — privacy budget (epsilon) trade-off control karta hai. Federated learning on-device train karti hai aur sirf updates share karti hai, taaki raw data device se bahar na jaaye — lekin updates khud information leak kar sakte hain aur poison ho sakte hain. Dono privacy tools hain, security guarantees nahi.

  
Q28 Red teaming testing se kaise alag hai?
Testing poochhta hai "kya system wo karta hai jo use karna chahiye?" — known expected outputs, functional aur integration tests. Red teaming poochhta hai "kya attacker use wo karwa sakta hai jo use nahi karna chahiye?" — unknown outputs, creative attack paths, adapt karne wala human adversary. Red teaming ek process hai: scope, design, build, run, report, evaluate. Dono chahiye; koi ek doosre ki jagah nahi le sakta.

  
Q29 Automated red teaming ki limits kya hain?
Automated red teaming me ek attacker LLM scale par jailbreaks aur injections generate karta hai aur ek judge model failure ko score karta hai. Ye tez aur broad hai, lekin novel human-creative attacks miss kar sakta hai aur false positives de sakta hai. Best practice hybrid hai: automated breadth aur regression testing ke liye, human depth aur novelty ke liye.

  
Q30 Deepfake fraud ka real example kya hai?
Hong Kong case: ek finance worker ko senior executives ko impersonate karne wale deepfake video call se trick karke lagbhag $25 million (chapter ke hisaab se Hong Kong dollars me) transfer karwa liye gaye. Voice cloning ke liye sirf seconds se minutes ka sample chahiye. Aur WormGPT jaise uncensored models $200–$500 per month me malicious use ke liye beche ja rahe the. Defence: verification processes (callback, multi-channel confirmation) jo sirf voice/video par bharosa na karein.

  
Q31 AI safety aur AI security me kya farak hai?
AI security poochhti hai: kya system ko attack kiya ja sakta hai? AI safety poochhti hai: kya system deploy karna safe hai aur use safe kaise banayein? Safety me bias, misinformation, accidents aur existential risks aate hain; security me evasion, poisoning, extraction aate hain. Dono overlap karte hain — unsafe model exploit ho sakta hai, insecure model unsafe banaya ja sakta hai — lekin field alag hai, aur safety field abhi unsettled hai: "safe" ki agreed definition bhi nahi hai.

  
Q32 Alignment problem kya hai? RLHF kaise kaam karta hai?
Alignment problem ye hai ki model ka training objective (next word predict karna, reward maximise karna) sirf ek proxy hai human intent ka — aur capable model proxy ko aise optimise kar sakta hai jo intent ko violate kare (reward hacking, specification gaming). RLHF me outputs ki human rankings se reward model train hota hai, phir model ko us reward ke against optimise kiya jata hai. Lekin RLHF sycophantic ya deceptive behaviour bhi paida kar sakta hai — alignment solved nahi hai.

  
Q33 EU AI Act ke main points kya hain?
EU AI Act duniya ka pehla comprehensive AI law hai, risk-based: unacceptable practices (social scoring, manipulative AI) banned hain; high-risk systems par strict requirements hain (risk management, data governance, human oversight, logging); general-purpose AI par transparency duties hain. Fines €30 million ya worldwide turnover ka 6% tak. Compute thresholds bhi hain — systemic-risk models ke liye 10^25 FLOPs training threshold cite kiya gaya hai.

  
Q34 NIST AI RMF kya hai?
NIST AI Risk Management Framework ek voluntary US framework hai jo chaar functions par organised hai: Govern (governance establish karo), Map (system ka context aur risks map karo), Measure (risks ko measure karo), Manage (unhe manage karo). ISO/IEC 42001 iska international standard counterpart hai — AI management system ke liye, ISO 27001 ki tarah. Dono governance ke liye structure dete hain, law nahi hain.

  
Q35 Monte Carlo simulation se risk kaise estimate karte hain?
Jab inputs uncertain hon, to unke assumed distributions se hazaaron samples draw karke outcome ka distribution nikala jata hai. Chapter ke example me 10,000 samples: incident frequency aur loss per incident (log-normal) ko multiply kiya gaya. Result: mean annual loss $76,506, 90% interval $27,323–$143,247. Lesson: ek single expected loss poori range chhupa deta hai — governance ko tail (high-loss cases) par focus karna chahiye.

  
Q36 AI security field me aage kya hoga? Skills shortage ka kya solution hai?
Aage: AI-generated adaptive attacks, agentic AI ki security (tool permissions, sandboxing, audit trails), quantum se encryption ka risk (post-quantum crypto, crypto-agility), aur measurement/governance gap. Skills shortage ka solution sirf hiring nahi hai — existing security logon ko AI aur existing AI logon ko security sikhana hoga, aur aise tools banane honge jo secure AI ko aasan banayein. Field abhi invent ho rahi hai, isliye adversarially sochna seekhna hi sabse bada skill hai.
