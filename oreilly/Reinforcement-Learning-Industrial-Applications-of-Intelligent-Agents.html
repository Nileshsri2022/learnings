Why Reinforcement Learning?

Reinforcement learning (RL) wo machine learning hai jo sequential decisions lena sikhati hai — ek ke baad ek faisle, jinka asar aage jaake dikhta hai. Normal ML ek hi step me sahi jawab dhoondhta hai; RL poori strategy seekhta hai — jaise chess khelna, robot chalana, ya customer ko lambe time tak khush rakhna.

RL ke 4 components

Har RL problem me 4 cheezein hoti hain:

  • Agent — jo faisle leta hai (robot, software, recommendation system).
  • Environment — jiske saath agent interact karta hai (maze, market, website).
  • Action — agent jo karta hai (aage badho, product suggest karo, price badhao).
  • Reward — environment ka feedback (coin mila, profit hua, click aaya).

Agent ka goal hai total reward maximize karna — sirf aaj ka nahi, lambe time ka. Isiliye RL business goals se seedha judta hai: profit, users, retention — accuracy ya F1-score se nahi.

Book ka approach

Ye book do hisson me hai: pehla hissa fundamental algorithms (Chapters 1–8) — bandits se lekar SAC tak; doosra hissa practical aur operational RL (Chapters 9–11) — project kaise chalaye, production me kaise le jaayein, safe aur ethical kaise rakhein. Har chapter me industrial examples hain — auto-scaling, real-time bidding, traffic, RC car, servo motor.

RL kahan use hota hai?

Robotics, recommendation systems, advertising (real-time bidding), traffic management, energy optimization (HVAC), inventory control, finance, healthcare — jahan bhi sequence of decisions se long-term outcome badalta ho, wahan RL fit hota hai.

Yaad rakho: RL = strategy seekhna. ML = decision seekhna. Software = process automate karna. Ye teeno alag levels hain — RL sabse upar, strategy wale level pe hai.

RL ka Itihaas — Plato se Deep RL tak

RL ka idea naya nahi hai — ye seekhne ki sabse purani kahani hai. Bachche trial-and-error se seekhte hain; philosophers ne centuries pehle yehi baat likhi thi. Bas ab humne isey mathematics aur algorithms me badal diya hai.

Timeline — bade milestones

  • Ancient Greece: Plato aur Aristotle ne likha ki seekhna experience aur reward se hota hai.
  • 1927 — Pavlov: conditioning experiments — stimulus aur response ka connection.
  • 1948 — Turing: learning machines ka idea; 1952 — Prinz: Ferranti Mark 1 computer par chess khelne wala program.
  • 1951/1953 — Grey Walter ka CORA tortoise: pehle robots me se ek, jo conditioned reflex se seekhta tha.
  • 1957 — Bellman: Markov decision processes (MDP) aur Bellman equation — poori RL theory ki neev.
  • 1972 — Rescorla–Wagner: value estimation ka pehla algorithm — prediction error se seekhna.
  • 1973 — AI winter: Lighthill report ke baad funding aur interest gir gaya.
  • 1989 — Q-learning: Watkins ka algorithm — bina model ke optimal policy seekhna.
  • 1990s — Sutton aur Barto: temporal-difference (TD) learning ko formalize kiya.
  • 2010s — Deep RL: neural networks + RL = DQN, Atari games, AlphaGo.

Biology se inspiration

Brain me dopamine prediction error ki tarah kaam karta hai — jab reward expectation se zyada milta hai, dopamine badhta hai; jab kam milta hai, girta hai. Yehi Rescorla–Wagner ka delta (δ) hai. Aur actor-critic architecture ka idea psychology ke System 1 (fast, automatic) aur System 2 (slow, deliberate) thinking se aaya hai.

Thorndike ka Law of Effect

Jo action achha result deta hai, use dobara karne ki probability badhti hai; jo bura result deta hai, use kam karte hain. Yehi RL ka core loop hai — policy evaluation (kitna achha kar rahe hain?) aur policy improvement (behtar kaise karein?).

RL Algorithms ki Taxonomy

RL algorithms bahut saare hain, lekin unhe 5 sawaalon se classify kar sakte ho. Ye taxonomy poori book ka map hai — har chapter iska ek hissa cover karta hai.

5 bade sawaal

  • Model-free ya model-based? Model-free algorithms environment ka model nahi banate — seedha experience se seekhte hain (Q-learning, PPO). Model-based pehle environment ka model seekhte hain, phir usme plan karte hain.
  • Online ya offline? Online agent live environment se interact karke seekhta hai. Offline (batch) agent purane logged data se seekhta hai — bina environment ko touch kiye.
  • On-policy ya off-policy? On-policy agent usi policy se seekhta hai jisse wo behave kar raha hai (SARSA). Off-policy kisi aur policy ke data se bhi seekh sakta hai (Q-learning) — purane experience reuse kar sakta hai.
  • Discrete ya continuous actions? Discrete actions gin-ti me hote hain (left/right, buy/sell). Continuous actions koi bhi real value le sakte hain (servo ki position, bid amount).
  • Value-based, policy-based, ya imitation? Value-based pehle har action ki value seekhta hai, phir best action chunta hai. Policy-based seedha policy (action probabilities) seekhta hai. Imitation expert ko copy karta hai.

Policy evaluation + improvement loop

Har RL algorithm ka core loop yehi hai: pehle current policy ko evaluate karo (kitna reward mil raha hai?), phir policy ko improve karo (behtar actions ki taraf badho). Ye loop baar-baar chalao — jab tak policy optimal na ho jaaye. Isko generalized policy iteration kehte hain.

Interview point: "On-policy vs off-policy" — ye sawaal har RL interview me aata hai. Short answer: on-policy = jo policy behave kar rahi hai, usi se seekho; off-policy = kisi bhi policy ke data se seekho (replay buffer possible hai).

Value Estimation aur Reward Engineering

RL ka pehla algorithm value estimation tha — Rescorla–Wagner (1972). Iska idea aaj ke saare TD algorithms ki jad hai, isliye isey samajhna zaroori hai.

Rescorla–Wagner rule

Value estimate: V(s, w) = w · s — state s aur weights w ka dot product. Prediction error: δ = E − V(s, w), jahan E expected (actual) reward hai. Weight update: w ← w + α·δ·s — learning rate α se error ki direction me weights badhao.

  • Agar actual reward expectation se zyada hai (δ positive), to value badhao.
  • Agar kam hai (δ negative), to value ghatao.
  • Agar bilkul sahi predict kiya (δ = 0), to kuch mat badlo — seekhne ko kuch nahi bacha.

Reward engineering — sabse important kaam

Reward hi batata hai agent ko ki kya seekhna hai. Galat reward diya to agent galat cheez seekh lega — aur bahut achhe se seekh lega. Isiliye reward design karna RL engineer ka sabse bada kaam hai.

Delayed rewards aur reward shaping

Real problems me reward turant nahi milta — chess me jeet aakhri move ke baad pata chalti hai, business me profit mahino baad. Aise me reward shaping karte hain — beech-beech me chhote rewards dekar agent ko goal ki taraf guide karo. Lekin savdhaan: shaping se agent shortcut bhi seekh sakta hai (jaise goal ke paas ghumna, goal tak jaane ke bajaye).

Yaad rakho: δ (prediction error) hi seekhne ka engine hai. Dopamine bhi yahi karta hai brain me — expected se zyada reward mila to signal up, kam mila to down.

Multi-Arm Bandit — Exploration vs Exploitation

RL ka sabse simple problem hai multi-arm bandit: ek slot machine ke kayi levers hain, har lever ka alag (unknown) payout hai. Kaunsa lever kheenche? Yahi exploration vs exploitation ka sawaal hai — naye options try karo ya jo best lagta hai use repeat karo?

Real example — website button

Red button vs green button — kaunsa zyada clicks laata hai? A/B testing me aadhe users ko red, aadhe ko green dikhate ho. Lekin A/B testing me flaw hai: test khatam hone tak tum losing option bhi dikhate rehte ho. Bandit algorithms jeetne wale option ko jaldi zyada dikhana shuru kar dete hain.

Average reward aur online value

Har action ki value uska average reward hai. Online update karne ke liye exponentially weighted moving average use hota hai — wahi form jo Rescorla–Wagner me tha (Eq 2-3 = Eq 1-3). Recent rewards ko zyada weight milta hai, purane bhoolte jaate hain.

Exploration strategies

  • ε-greedy (Alg 2-1): ε probability se random action lo (explore), baaki time best action lo (exploit). Simple, effective, sabse zyada use hota hai.
  • Softmax: actions ko unki value ke hisaab se probabilities do — behtar action ki zyada probability, lekin kamzor ko bhi chance.
  • Annealing: shuru me zyada explore karo, dheere-dheere ε kam karo — jaise metal ko thanda karte hain.
  • UCB (Upper Confidence Bound): kam try kiye gaye actions ko bonus do — uncertainty ko reward karo. Jo action kam dekha hai, uska potential zyada ho sakta hai.
Interview point: "Exploration vs exploitation" ka jawab: ε-greedy simple hai lekin UCB smarter hai — wo uncertainty ko track karta hai, random nahi hota. Production me aksar ε-greedy se shuru karte hain.

Markov Decision Processes (MDP)

MDP wo mathematical framework hai jisme poori RL theory likhi jaati hai. Agar problem ko MDP me likh sakte ho, to RL algorithms apply kar sakte ho. Nahi likh sakte, to pehle problem ko samjho.

MDP ke parts

  • States (S): environment ki situation — jaise inventory me kitne items bache hain.
  • Actions (A): agent kya kar sakta hai — restock karo ya nahi.
  • Transition model p(s′, r | s, a): ek state-action se agle state aur reward ki probability. Ye environment ka "physics" hai.
  • Policy π(a | s): har state me kaunsa action lena hai.
  • Markov property: agla state sirf current state aur action par depend karta hai — poora past yaad rakhne ki zaroorat nahi.

Inventory control example

States: 0, 1, ya 2 items. Actions: restock karo ya nahi. Sale ki probability p_sale = 0.7. Transition ko table, graph, ya matrix se dikhate hain. Har state-action pair ke liye pata hai ki agle state me jaane ki kitni probability hai aur kitna reward milega.

POMDP — jab state poori dikhe nahi

Agar agent state ko poora observe nahi kar sakta (jaise camera se sirf aage dikhta hai, peeche nahi), to wo partially observable MDP (POMDP) hai. Iska detail Chapter 8 me hai.

Yaad rakho: Markov property ka matlab hai — "aaj" hi kaafi hai, "kal" yaad rakhne ki zaroorat nahi. Agar past ki zaroorat padti hai, to use state me daal do (jaise pichhle 4 frames stack karna Atari me).

import numpy as np

p_sale = 0.7 # Probability of a sale in one step
n_steps = 100 # Number of steps to run experiment for

np.random.seed(42)

# Cell 3 - actions and three example strategies (always buy, random, buy only when empty)
from enum import Enum
class Action(Enum):
    NONE = 0
    RESTOCK = 1

    @staticmethod
    def keep_buying_action(current_state) -> Enum:
        if current_state == 2:
            return Action.NONE
        else:
            return Action.RESTOCK

    @staticmethod
    def random_action(current_state) -> Enum:
        if current_state == 2:
            return Action.NONE
        if np.random.randint(len(Action)) == 0:
            return Action.NONE
        else:
            return Action.RESTOCK

    @staticmethod
    def zero_action(current_state) -> Enum:
        if current_state == 0:
            return Action.RESTOCK
        else:
            return Action.NONE

print("There are {} actions.".format(len(Action)))

# Cell 4 - transition matrix (states 0, 1, 2 = stock level)
# The Transition Matrix represents the following states:
# State 0
# State 1
# State 2
transition_matrix = [
    # No action
    [
        [1, 0, 0],
        [p_sale, 1 - p_sale, 0],
        [0, p_sale, 1 - p_sale],
    ],
    # Restock
    [
        [p_sale, 1 - p_sale, 0],
        [0, p_sale, 1 - p_sale],
    ],
]

# Cell 5 - reward matrix (reward 1 when a sale happens)
reward_matrix = [
    # No action
    [
        [0, 0, 0],
        [1, 0, 0],
        [0, 1, 0],
    ],
    # Restock
    [
        [1, 0, 0],
        [0, 1, 0],
        [0, 0, 0],
    ],
]

# Cell 6 - Experiment 1: the environment interface, run 10 single steps
def environment(current_state: int, action: int) -> (int, int):
    # Get the transition probabilities to each new state

    current_transition_probabilities = \
        transition_matrix[action.value][current_state]

    # Use the transition probabilitis to transition to a new state

    next_state = np.random.choice(
        a=[0, 1, 2],
        p=current_transition_probabilities
    )

    # Get the reward for the new state (was there a sale?)

    reward = reward_matrix[action.value][current_state][next_state]

    return (next_state, reward)

current_state = 1 # Current state, one product in stock
action = Action.RESTOCK # Current action, as chosen by a strategy
for i in range(10): # What happens if we run this multiple times?
    next_state, reward = environment(current_state, action) # Environment interface
    print(f"trial {i}: s={current_state}, a={action}, s'={next_state}, r={reward}")

# Cell 7 - Experiment 2: test the three restocking strategies over 100 steps (seed fixed)
import pandas as pd
# The different strategies
strategies = [("Keep Buying", Action.keep_buying_action),
            ("Upon Zero", Action.zero_action), ("Random", Action.random_action)]
result = [] # Results buffer

    np.random.seed(42) # This is really important, otherwise different strategies will experience sales
    reward_history = [] # Reward buffer
    current_state = 2 # Initial state
    total_reward = 0

              .format(current_state, next_state, action.name, reward))
        total_reward += reward

Return, Discounting aur Value Functions

Agent ko "achha" kaise naapein? Return se — future me milne wale saare rewards ka (discounted) total. Value functions isi return ka expected value batati hain.

Discounted return

G = r + γ·r′ + γ²·r″ + … — γ (gamma) discount factor hai, aam taur par 0.9–0.99. γ = 0 matlab sirf immediate reward matter karta hai (myopic agent). γ = 1 matlab future reward bhi utna hi valuable hai jitna aaj ka. Discounting se infinite-horizon problems bhi handle ho jaate hain — return infinite nahi hota.

Do value functions

  • State-value V^π(s) (Eq 2-8): policy π follow karke state s se kitna return milega, expected value.
  • Action-value Q^π(s, a) (Eq 2-9): state s me action a lene ke baad, phir policy π follow karke, kitna return milega.
  • Optimal policy π*: wo policy jo har state me sabse zyada value de. Bellman optimality equation (Eq 2-10) batati hai ki optimal value kya honi chahiye — V*(s) = max_a [expected reward + γ·V*(s′)].

Value functions kyun important hain?

Kyunki policy ko improve karne ka tareeka hai: jis action ki Q-value zyada hai, use zyada chuno. Value functions seekhna hi RL ka pehla kadam hai — Chapters 3–4 isi pe bane hain.

reward_cliff = 0 # Reward for falling off cliff

    if current_position >= end_position:

    return 0

    if current_position <= cliff_position:
        return True
    if current_position >= end_position:

    return False

# Cell 3 - the agent: a random strategy (step left or right with 50/50 chance)
def strategy() -> int:
    if np.random.random() >= 0.5:
        return 1 # Right
    else:
        return -1 # Left

    # Now add the reward to the buffers that allow you to calculate the average
    for pos in position_history:
        value_sum[pos] += current_reward
        n_hits[pos] += 1
    # Now calculate the average for this episode and print
    expected_return = ', '.join(f'{q:.2f}' for q in value_sum / n_hits)
    print("[{}] Average reward: [{}]".format(i, expected_return))

Monte Carlo Methods

Monte Carlo (MC) methods value seekhne ka sabse seedha tareeka hain: poora episode khelo, phir dekho ki kya return mila, aur usse value update karo. Koi model nahi chahiye, koi bootstrapping nahi — sirf experience.

MC policy generation (Alg 2-3)

Episode complete hone ke baad, har state ke liye return G calculate karo aur V(s) ko uski taraf move karo. Policy improve karo — greedy bano un actions ke liye jinki Q-value zyada hai. Phir dobara episode khelo. Repeat.

First-visit vs every-visit

  • First-visit MC: ek episode me state pehli baar aane par hi uska return use karo.
  • Every-visit MC: har baar jab state aaye, uska return use karo.
  • Dono converge karte hain, lekin every-visit thoda zyada data use karta hai.

MC ki kamzori

MC ko episode khatam hone ka wait karna padta hai — beech me kuch nahi seekhta. Agar episode lamba hai (ya kabhi khatam nahi hota), to MC bekaar hai. Isi liye TD learning aaya (Chapter 3) — jo har step pe seekhta hai.

MC vs TD — ek line me: MC poora episode dekh kar seekhta hai (high variance, no bias). TD har step pe guess karke seekhta hai (low variance, thoda bias). Dono ka combination n-step methods me hai.

# Cell 7 - Monte Carlo trajectory generation (random actions, record state/action/reward)
class MonteCarloGeneration(object):
  def __init__(self, env: object, max_steps: int = 1000, debug: bool = False):
    self.env = env
    self.max_steps = max_steps
    self.debug = debug
  def run(self) -> List:
    buffer = []
    n_steps = 0 # Keep track of the number of steps so I can bail out if it takes too long
    state, _, _ = self.env.reset() # Reset environment back to start
    terminal = False
    while not terminal: # Run until terminal state
      action = random.choice(self.env.action_space) # Random action. Try replacing this with Direction.EAST
      next_state, reward, terminal = self.env.step(action) # Take action in environment
      buffer.append((state, action, reward)) # Store the result
      state = next_state # Ready for the next step
      n_steps += 1
      if n_steps >= self.max_steps:
        if self.debug:
          print("Terminated early due to large number of steps")
        terminal = True # Bail out if we've been working for too long
    return buffer

# Cell 9 - Monte Carlo experiment: average action values over all visits
class MonteCarloExperiment(object):
  def __init__(self, generator: MonteCarloGeneration):
    self.generator = generator
    self.values = defaultdict(float)
    self.counts = defaultdict(float)
  def _to_key(self, state, action):
    return (state, action)
  def action_value(self, state, action) -> float:
    key = self._to_key(state, action)
    if self.counts[key] > 0:
      return self.values[key] / self.counts[key]
    else:
      return 0.0

# Cell 10 - store experience from a full trajectory, working backwards from the terminal state
class MonteCarloExperiment(MonteCarloExperiment):
  def run_episode(self) -> None:
    trajectory = self.generator.run() # Generate a trajectory
    episode_reward = 0
    for i, t in enumerate(reversed(trajectory)): # Starting from the terminal state
      state, action, reward = t
      key = self._to_key(state, action)
      episode_reward += reward  # Add the reward to the buffer
      self.values[key] += episode_reward # And add this to the value of this action
      self.counts[key] += 1 # Increment counter

Value Iteration aur Dynamic Programming

Agar environment ka model (transition probabilities) pata hai, to dynamic programming (DP) se optimal policy calculate kar sakte ho — bina ek bhi real episode khele. Value iteration iska sabse seedha algorithm hai.

Value iteration (Alg 2-4)

Har state ki value ko Bellman optimality equation se baar-baar update karo: V(s) ← max_a [r + γ·Σ p(s′|s,a)·V(s′)]. Har iteration me values optimal ke kareeb aati hain. Jab values badalna band ho jaayein, to greedy policy nikaalo — wo optimal hai.

1-step lookahead aur backup diagrams

Har update ek 1-step lookahead hai — current state se ek action, phir agle state ki value. Backup diagrams dikhate hain ki DP saare states ko visit karta hai, MC sirf visited states ko, aur TD beech me hai.

DP ki 4 kamzoriyan

  • Model chahiye: transition probabilities pata honi chahiye — real life me aksar nahi pata hoti.
  • Discounting chahiye: bina discount ke values diverge kar sakti hain.
  • Saare states visit karta hai: bade state space me impossible hai.
  • Sample inefficient: har update ke liye poora model chahiye, experience se nahi seekhta.

Ye kamzoriyan hi woh reasons hain jinki wajah se MC aur TD aaye — aur baad me deep learning (Chapter 4).

Temporal-Difference Learning

Temporal-difference (TD) learning MC aur DP ka best combination hai: MC ki tarah model-free (experience se seekhta hai) aur DP ki tarah bootstrapping karta hai (episode khatam hone ka wait nahi karta).

TD update (Eq 3-3 / 3-4)

V(s) ← V(s) + α·[r + γ·V(s′) − V(s)] — bracket ke andar hai TD error: jo mila (r + γ·V(s′)) minus jo expect kiya tha (V(s)). Yehi Rescorla–Wagner ka δ hai, bas ab next state ki value bhi include hai.

Bootstrapping + sampling

  • Bootstrapping: apne hi estimate se seekhna — V(s′) ka use karke V(s) update karna. DP bhi yahi karta hai.
  • Sampling: real experience se seekhna — model ke bajaye environment se sample lena. MC bhi yahi karta hai.
  • TD = bootstrapping + sampling. Isiliye TD har step pe seekh sakta hai, episode khatam hone se pehle hi.

TD vs MC vs DP — kab kaunsa?

DP: model chahiye, saare states sweep karta hai. MC: episode end ka wait, high variance. TD: har step seekhta hai, low variance, thoda bias (kyunki V(s′) khud ek guess hai). Practice me TD sabse zyada use hota hai — Q-learning, SARSA, DQN, sab TD par bane hain.

Yaad rakho: TD error = "surprise". Jab reward expectation se alag hota hai, tabhi seekhne ko kuch milta hai. Zero surprise = zero learning — bilkul dopamine ki tarah.

Q-Learning — Off-Policy TD

Q-learning RL ka sabse famous algorithm hai. Iska idea simple hai: har state-action pair ki value (Q-value) seekho, aur hamesha best Q-value wala action lo. Ye off-policy hai — kisi bhi behavior se data aaye, Q-learning optimal policy seekh sakta hai.

Q-learning update (Eq 3-5, Alg 3-1)

Q(s, a) ← Q(s, a) + α·[r + γ·max_a′ Q(s′, a′) − Q(s, a)] — next state me best possible action ki value lo, chahe agent ne wo action liya ho ya nahi. Isi liye ye off-policy hai: target policy (greedy) behavior policy (ε-greedy) se alag hai.

Tabular Q-learning

Chhote problems me Q-values ko ek table me store karte hain — har state-action pair ke liye ek cell. Table bharne ke baad optimal policy = har state me max Q wala action. Simple, guaranteed converge karta hai (agar har state-action baar-baar visit ho).

Q-learning ki taakat

  • Off-policy: purana data, random data, kisi aur agent ka data — sab se seekh sakta hai.
  • Model-free: transition probabilities nahi chahiye.
  • Simple: ek update rule, ek table — bas.

Isi simplicity ki wajah se Q-learning Chapter 4 me DQN ki neev bana — wahan table ki jagah neural network aa jaata hai.

class SARSAAgent(QLearningAgent):
    def __init__(self, actions, goal_reward, name="SARSA",
                 alpha=0.1, gamma=0.99, epsilon=0.1, explore="uniform", anneal=False):
        self.goal_reward = goal_reward
        QLearningAgent.__init__(
            self,
            actions=list(actions),
            name=name,
            alpha=alpha,
            gamma=gamma,
            epsilon=epsilon,
            explore=explore,
            anneal=anneal)
    def policy(self, state):
        return self.get_max_q_action(state)
    def act(self, state, reward, learning=True):
        '''
        This is mostly the same as the base QLearningAgent class. Except that
        the update procedure now generates the action.
        '''
        if learning:
            action = self.update(
                self.prev_state, self.prev_action, reward, state)
        else:
            if self.explore == "softmax":
                # Softmax exploration
                action = self.soft_max_policy(state)
            else:
                # Uniform exploration
                action = self.epsilon_greedy_q_policy(state)
        self.prev_state = state
        self.prev_action = action
        self.step_number += 1
        # Anneal params.
        if learning and self.anneal:
            self._anneal()
        return action
    def update(self, state, action, reward, next_state):
        '''
        Args:
            state (State)
            action (str)
            reward (float)
            next_state (State)
        Summary:
            Updates the internal Q Function according to the Bellman Equation
            using a SARSA update
        '''
        if self.explore == "softmax":
            # Softmax exploration
            next_action = self.soft_max_policy(next_state)
        else:
            # Uniform exploration
            next_action = self.epsilon_greedy_q_policy(next_state)
        # Update the Q Function.
        prev_q_val = self.get_q_value(state, action)
        next_q_val = self.get_q_value(next_state, next_action)
        self.q_func[state][action] = prev_q_val + self.alpha * \
            (reward + self.gamma * next_q_val - prev_q_val)
        return next_action

import numpy as np
def state_mapping(obs):
    """
    Since this is tabular, we can't use real numbers. There would be an infinite
    number of states. Instead I round and convert to an integer. This is a
    simple form of _tile coding_.
    """
    return tuple(np.round(100 * obs[0:1]))
def run_episode(env, agent, learning=True):
    episode_reward = 0
    observation = env.reset()
    episode_over = False
    reward = 0
    action_buffer = []
    while not episode_over:
        if hasattr(agent, "q_func"):
            action = agent.act(
                state_mapping(observation),
                reward,
                learning=learning)
        else:
            action = agent.act(
                state_mapping(observation),
                reward)
        action_buffer.append(observation[0])
        observation, reward, episode_over, _ = env.step(action)
        episode_reward += reward
    agent.end_of_episode()
    return episode_reward, action_buffer
def train_agent(env, agent_func, n_repeats):
    train_rewards_buffer = np.zeros((n_episodes, n_repeats))
    train_bid_buffer = np.zeros((n_episodes, n_repeats, env.batch_size))
    for instance in range(n_repeats):
        agent = agent_func(range(env.action_space.n))
        for episode in range(n_episodes):
            episode_reward, action_buffer = run_episode(env, agent)
            train_rewards_buffer[episode, instance] = episode_reward
            train_bid_buffer[episode, instance, :] = np.pad(
                action_buffer, (0, env.batch_size - len(action_buffer)),
                'constant', constant_values=0)
    if hasattr(agent, "q_func"):
        print_arbitrary_policy(agent.q_func)
    # Test
    agent.epsilon = 0
    episode_reward, test_bid_buffer = run_episode(env, agent, learning=False)
    print(
        "{}: {}".format(
            "TEST",
            episode_reward))
    print(train_rewards_buffer.transpose())
    print(test_bid_buffer)
    return train_rewards_buffer, train_bid_buffer.mean(axis=2)

Extensions — Double Q, n-Step, Eligibility Traces

Basic Q-learning achha hai, lekin usme 3 problems hain: overestimation, slow learning, aur single-step updates. Extensions in teeno ko fix karte hain.

Double Q-learning — overestimation ka ilaaj

Q-learning max operator ki wajah se values ko overestimate karta hai — kyunki max hamesha optimistic hota hai. Double Q-learning do Q-tables rakhta hai: ek se action chuno, doosre se uski value lo. Overestimation bias khatam.

Delayed Q-learning aur Opposition learning

Delayed Q-learning: rewards ko buffer karke baad me update karo — PAC (probably approximately correct) guarantees deta hai. Opposition learning: ulta bhi seekho — jo action bura tha, usse bhi seekh lo.

n-step algorithms (Eq 3-7/3-8/3-9, Alg 3-3)

1-step TD sirf ek step aage dekhta hai; MC poora episode. n-step SARSA beech ka rasta hai — n steps ka actual reward lo, phir bootstrap karo. n badhane se MC jaisa (zyada variance, kam bias), n ghatane se TD jaisa. n ek hyperparameter hai jo bias-variance tradeoff control karta hai.

Eligibility traces — SARSA(λ) (Alg 3-4)

Har state-action ko ek trace do — jo abhi visit hua, uska trace 1; purane visits ka trace λ se decay hota hai. Reward aane par saare traces update hote hain — recent states ko zyada credit, purane ko kam. λ = 0 matlab 1-step TD, λ = 1 matlab MC. Practice me λ < 1 use hota hai. Watkins's Q(λ) me non-greedy action par trace reset hota hai. Accumulating vs replacing traces — replacing zyada stable hota hai.

Interview point: "n-step aur eligibility traces ka kya fayda?" — ye TD aur MC ke beech ka bridge hain. n-step se learning tez hoti hai kyunki credit assignment behtar hota hai — reward ka asar peeche tak pahunchta hai.

# Cell 3 - n-Step SARSA agent (simple_rl has no n-step implementation)
import numpy as np
import sys
from collections import defaultdict
from simple_rl.agents import Agent, QLearningAgent
class nStepSARSAAgent(QLearningAgent):
    def __init__(self, actions, n, name="n-Step SARSA",
                 alpha=0.1, gamma=0.99, epsilon=0.1, explore="uniform", anneal=False):
        self.T = sys.maxsize
        self.stored_rewards = []
        self.stored_states = []
        self.stored_actions = []
        self.n = n
        QLearningAgent.__init__(
            self,
            actions=list(actions),
            name=name,
            alpha=alpha,
            gamma=gamma,
            epsilon=epsilon,
            explore=explore,
            anneal=anneal)
    def policy(self, state):
        return self.get_max_q_action(state)
    def act(self, state, reward, learning=True):
        action = self.update(reward, state)
        return action
    def update(self, rt1, st1):
        if len(self.stored_states) == 0:
            action = self.epsilon_greedy_q_policy(st1)
            self.stored_actions.append(action)
            self.stored_states.append(st1)
            self.stored_rewards.append(rt1)
            return action
        if self.step_number < self.T:
            # Observe and store the next reward R_{t+1} and next state
            # S_{t+1}
            self.stored_rewards.append(rt1)
            self.stored_states.append(st1)
            # if s_{t+1} terminal
            if st1.is_terminal():
                self.T = self.step_number + 1
            else:
                action = self.epsilon_greedy_q_policy(st1)
                self.stored_actions.append(action)
        self.tau = self.step_number - self.n + 1
        if self.tau >= 0:
            # G = sum(\gamma * R)
            start_index = self.tau + 1
            end_index = min(self.tau + self.n, self.T)
            G = 0
            for i in range(
                    start_index, end_index + 1):  # Because range is exclusive
                G += self.gamma**(i - self.tau - 1) * self.stored_rewards[i]
            if self.tau + self.n < self.T:
                G += self.gamma**self.n * \
                    self.q_func[self.stored_states[(
                        self.tau + self.n)]][self.stored_actions[(self.tau + self.n)]]
            s_tau = self.stored_states[self.tau]
            a_tau = self.stored_actions[self.tau]
            self.q_func[s_tau][a_tau] += self.alpha * \
                (G - self.q_func[s_tau][a_tau])
        # If at end repeat until tau == T - 1
        self.step_number += 1
        if self.step_number >= self.T and self.tau < self.T - 1:
            self.update(None, None)
        return self.stored_actions[-1]
    def end_of_episode(self):
        self.prev_state = None
        self.prev_action = None
        self.episode_number += 1
        self.T = sys.maxsize
        self.step_number = 0
        self.stored_rewards = []
        self.stored_states = []
        self.stored_actions = []
    def reset(self):
        self.prev_state = None
        self.prev_action = None
        self.step_number = 0

# Cell 3 - SARSA(lambda) agent with eligibility traces (accumulate or replace)
import numpy as np
import sys
from collections import defaultdict
from simple_rl.agents import Agent, QLearningAgent
class lambdaSARSAAgent(QLearningAgent):
    def __init__(self, actions, name="lambda SARSA",
                 alpha=0.5, lam=0.5, gamma=1.0, epsilon=0.1, explore="uniform",
                 anneal=False, replacement_method="accumulate"):
        self.lam = lam
        self.eligibility = defaultdict(lambda: defaultdict(lambda: 0))
        self.replacement_method = replacement_method
        QLearningAgent.__init__(
            self,
            actions=list(actions),
            name=name,
            alpha=alpha,
            gamma=gamma,
            epsilon=epsilon,
            explore=explore,
            anneal=anneal)
    def act(self, new_state, reward, learning=True):
        next_action = self.epsilon_greedy_q_policy(new_state)
        if self.prev_state is None:
            self.prev_state = new_state
            self.prev_action = next_action
            return next_action
        self.update(
            self.prev_state,
            self.prev_action,
            reward,
            new_state,
            next_action)
        self.prev_state = new_state
        self.prev_action = next_action
        return next_action
    def update(self, state, action, reward, next_state, next_action):
        td_error = reward + self.gamma * \
            self.q_func[next_state][next_action] - self.q_func[state][action]
        if self.replacement_method == "accumulate":
            self.eligibility[state][action] += 1
        elif self.replacement_method == "replace":
            self.eligibility[state][action] = 1
        for s in self.eligibility.keys():
            for a in self.eligibility[s].keys():
                self.q_func[s][a] += self.alpha * \
                    td_error * self.eligibility[s][a]
                self.eligibility[s][a] *= self.gamma * self.lam
    def end_of_episode(self):
        self.eligibility = defaultdict(lambda: defaultdict(lambda: 0))
        QLearningAgent.end_of_episode(self)
class lambdaWatkinsSARSAAgent(lambdaSARSAAgent):
    def epsilon_greedy_q_policy(self, state):
        # Policy: Epsilon of the time explore, otherwise, greedyQ.
        if np.random.random() > self.epsilon:
            # Exploit.
            action = self.get_max_q_action(state)
        else:
            # Explore
            action = np.random.choice(self.actions)
            # Reset eligibility trace
            self.eligibility = defaultdict(lambda: defaultdict(lambda: 0))
        return action

Deep Q-Networks — Tabular se Deep tak

Tabular Q-learning tab fail hota hai jab states bahut zyada ho jaayein — jaise Atari game ke pixels (millions of states). Table me jagah nahi hai. Solution: Q-values ko function se approximate karo — aur wo function ek neural network ho, to use kehte hain DQN.

Function approximation kya hai?

Q(s, a) ko table me store karne ke bajaye, ek function Q(s, a; θ) seekho jo state se Q-value predict kare. Ye function regression ki tarah train hota hai — target hai r + γ·max Q(s′, a′). Neural network is function ko represent karta hai.

Neural network architectures

  • MLP (multilayer perceptron): simple feed-forward — tabular data ke liye.
  • CNN: images/pixels ke liye — Atari games me yehi use hota hai.
  • RNN / LSTM / GRU: sequence ya time-dependent states ke liye.
  • Autoencoders: state ko compress karke informative representation banana.
  • DBN/RBM, ESN: purane architectures — aaj kal kam use hote hain.

Frameworks

TensorFlow, PyTorch, MXNet, Keras, Ignite/Skorch, Gluon, ONNX — sab me DQN implementations milte hain. Book PyTorch ko intuitive API ke liye prefer karta hai. Framework chunna ek lock-in decision hai — soch-samajh kar chuno.

Yaad rakho: DQN = Q-learning + neural network. Algorithm wahi hai (Chapter 3), bas Q-table ki jagah network aa gaya. Lekin ye chhota change bade stability problems laata hai — agla section dekho.

DQN ke Tricks — Replay aur Target Network

Neural network + Q-learning seedha jod do to training diverge kar jaata hai. DQN ke 2 famous tricks ne ise stable banaya — experience replay aur target network.

Experience replay

Har transition (s, a, r, s′) ko ek replay buffer (FIFO) me store karo. Training ke liye buffer se random minibatch sample karo. Fayde:

  • Correlation tootta hai: consecutive states bahut similar hote hain — random sampling se data decorrelate hota hai.
  • Data reuse: ek hi experience se kayi baar seekh sakte ho — sample efficiency badhti hai.
  • Catastrophic forgetting kam: purana experience buffer me rehta hai, network use bhoolta nahi.

Target network (Q-network clones)

Target r + γ·max Q(s′, a′) calculate karne ke liye same network use karo to target har update pe hilta rehta hai — moving target ko chase karna mushkil hai. Solution: network ki ek copy (target network) rakho, use slowly update karo (ya har N steps pe copy karo). Training stable ho jaata hai.

Loss function — MSE vs Huber (Eq 4-2)

Loss = (target − prediction)² — MSE. Lekin outliers se MSE explode ho jaata hai, isliye Huber loss prefer karte hain — chhote errors pe MSE jaisa, bade errors pe linear. Multi-head outputs se ek network kayi actions ki Q-values de sakta hai.

class QLearningAgentParameters(AgentParameters):
    def __init__(self, default_q=0, alpha=0.1):
        super().__init__(algorithm=QLearningAlgorithmParameters(),
                         exploration=EGreedyParameters(),
                         memory=ExperienceReplayParameters(),
                         networks={})
        self.default_q = default_q
        self.alpha = alpha
    @property
    def path(self):
        return 'q_learning_agent:QLearningAgent'
class QLearningAgent(Agent):
    def __init__(self, agent_parameters,
                 parent: Union['LevelManager', 'CompositeAgent'] = None):
        super().__init__(agent_parameters, parent)
        self.default_q = self.ap.default_q
        self.q_func = defaultdict(lambda: defaultdict(lambda: self.default_q))
    def train(self) -> float:
        loss = 0
        if self._should_train():
            # Required: State, action, reward
            transition = self.current_episode_buffer.get_last_transition()
            if transition is None:
                return loss
            state = tuple(transition.state["observation"])
            action = transition.action
            reward = transition.reward
            actions_q_values = self.get_all_q_values_for_states(
                transition.next_state)
            max_q_next_state = np.max(actions_q_values)
            delta = (reward + self.ap.algorithm.discount *
                     max_q_next_state - self.q_func[state][action])
            self.q_func[state][action] += self.ap.alpha * delta
            # Coach want's me to return the total training loss, but we're not
            # really training. Instead, I will return the TD error.
            loss = np.abs(delta)
        return loss
    def get_all_q_values_for_states(self, state):
        # This is almost a replica of the ValueIterationAgent. Probably could
        # be refactored to use that.
        state = tuple(state["observation"])
        l = np.array([self.q_func[state][a]
                      for a in self.spaces.action.actions])
        # Add a little random noise to all q_values to prevent ties
        # See https://github.com/NervanaSystems/coach/issues/414
        l = l + np.random.normal(loc=0, scale=0.000000001, size=l.shape)
        return l
    def choose_action(self, curr_state):
        actions_q_values = self.get_all_q_values_for_states(curr_state)
        action, action_probabilities = self.exploration_policy.get_action(
            actions_q_values)
        action_info = ActionInfo(action=action,
                                 action_value=actions_q_values[action],
                                 max_action_value=np.max(actions_q_values),
                                 all_action_probabilities=action_probabilities)
        return action_info

Rainbow DQN aur Offline RL

DQN ke baad researchers ne 6 alag improvements combine karke Rainbow DQN (2017) banaya — aur har improvement ka ablation study kiya ki kaunsa kitna kaam aata hai.

Rainbow ke 6 improvements

  • Double Q-learning: overestimation kam karo (Chapter 3).
  • n-step returns: tez credit assignment.
  • Distributional RL (C51): sirf expected value nahi, poora distribution seekho — 51 bins me reward distribution. Multimodal rewards handle hote hain.
  • Prioritized experience replay: jin transitions se zyada seekhne ko hai (bada TD error), unhe zyada sample karo.
  • Noisy nets: exploration ke liye network weights me noise daalo — ε-greedy se behtar.
  • Dueling networks: value V(s) aur advantage A(s,a) alag se seekho — Q = V + A. State kitna achha hai, ye action se alag pata chalta hai.

Atari results aur cost

Pong aur Ms Pac-Man par Rainbow DQN se behtar hai — khaas taur par Ms Pac-Man me (power pellets seekh leta hai). Cost: DQN ~4.8 hours / $5.80 per 1M frames, Rainbow ~7.6 hours / $8.50. Behtar performance ki keemat hai — lekin production me ye cost negligible hai agar problem valuable ho.

Offline RL aur BCQ

Batch-constrained deep Q-learning (BCQ): jab sirf logged data ho (koi live environment nahi), to normal DQN diverge kar jaata hai — kyunki wo aise actions ki value overestimate karta hai jo data me kabhi dekhe hi nahi. BCQ conditional VAE se sirf wohi actions leta hai jo data me dikhe hain. CartPole buffer experiment me DQN diverge hua, BCQ stable raha. Offline RL Chapter 9 me detail me hai.

Policy Gradient Theorem

Value-based methods pehle Q-value seekhte hain, phir policy nikaalte hain. Policy gradient methods seedha policy seekhte hain — state se action probabilities ka mapping. Ye continuous actions aur stochastic policies ke liye behtar hain.

Policy gradient theorem (Eq 5-3)

∇J(θ) = E[G · ∇ ln π(a|s)] — policy parameters θ ko us direction me badhao jahan return G zyada hai. Gradient ka matlab: jin actions se achha return mila, unki probability badhao; jin se bura mila, unki ghatao. Log-probability trick se ye expectation sample karke estimate ho jaata hai — model nahi chahiye.

Stochastic policies kyun?

  • Exploration built-in: policy khud probabilities deti hai — random sampling se exploration hota hai, ε-greedy ki zaroorat nahi.
  • Smooth changes: policy dheere-dheere badalti hai — value-based methods me policy ek dum flip ho sakti hai.
  • Continuous actions: Gaussian policy se mean aur standard deviation predict karo — koi bhi real-valued action sample kar sakte ho.

Logistic aur softmax policies

Logistic policy (Eq 5-6/5-7): 2 actions ke liye — sigmoid se probability. Softmax policy (Eq 5-8/5-9): kayi actions ke liye — har action ka score exponentiate karke normalize karo. Dono ke gradients Appendix A/B me derive kiye gaye hain — lekin practice me autodiff (JAX/PyTorch/TensorFlow) ye calculation kar deta hai.

Yaad rakho: Policy gradient = "achhe actions ki probability badhao". Bas itna hi hai — baaki sab (baseline, actor-critic, trust region) is idea ko stable aur efficient banane ke tareeke hain.

from simple_rl.agents import PolicyGradientAgent
class LogisticPolicyAgent(PolicyGradientAgent):
    def __init__(self, actions, num_feats):
        self.α = 0.01
        self.γ = 0.99
        self.num_feats = num_feats
        PolicyGradientAgent.__init__(
            self, name="logistic_policy_gradient", actions=actions
        )
        self.reset()
    @staticmethod
    def logistic(x):
        return 1 / (1 + np.exp(-x))
    @staticmethod
    def π(θ, s):
        π = LogisticPolicyAgent.logistic(np.dot(θ.T, s))
        return np.array([π, 1 - π])
    @staticmethod
    def Δ(θ, s):
        π = LogisticPolicyAgent.logistic(np.dot(θ.T, s))
        return np.array([s - s * π, -s * π])
    def act(self, state, reward):
        if self.previous_pair is not None:
            self.episode_history.append(Step(self.previous_pair, reward))
        π = LogisticPolicyAgent.π(self.θ, state)
        action = np.random.choice((0, 1), p=π)
        self.previous_pair = Pair(state.data, action)
        return action
    def reset(self):
        self.θ = np.zeros(self.num_feats)
        self.end_of_episode()
        PolicyGradientAgent.reset(self)
    def end_of_episode(self):
        T = len(getattr(self, "episode_history", []))
        G = 0
        grad_buf = []
        for t in reversed(range(T)):
            G = G * self.γ + self.episode_history[t].reward
            grad = LogisticPolicyAgent.Δ(self.θ, self.episode_history[t].pair.state)[
                self.episode_history[t].pair.action
            ]
            self.θ += self.α * np.power(self.γ, t) * grad * G
            grad_buf.append(np.power(self.γ, t) * grad * G)
        reinforce_gradient_buffer.append(
            [np.mean(np.abs(grad_buf)), np.std(grad_buf)])
        self.episode_history = []
        self.previous_pair = None
        PolicyGradientAgent.end_of_episode(self)

REINFORCE aur Baseline

REINFORCE sabse pehla policy gradient algorithm hai — simple, lekin high variance ki wajah se slow. Baseline uska fix hai.

REINFORCE (Alg 5-1)

Episode khelo, har step ka return G calculate karo, phir policy ko G · ∇ ln π direction me update karo. Problems:

  • High variance: Monte Carlo returns noisy hote hain — gradient har episode me alag direction dikhata hai.
  • Last action ko zyada credit: episode ke end wale actions ka return turant dikhta hai, shuru wale actions ka asar dilute ho jaata hai.

REINFORCE with baseline (Alg 5-2)

Return G ki jagah advantage δ = G − V(s, w) use karo — V(s) ek baseline hai jo batata hai ki is state se normally kitna return milta hai. Agar action ne baseline se behtar kiya (δ positive), to uski probability badhao; warna ghatao. Variance dramatically kam ho jaati hai, kyunki ab sirf "expected se behtar/bura" matter karta hai, absolute return nahi.

Gradient variance reduction (Fig 5-4)

Baseline se gradient estimates stable hote hain — learning tez aur smooth. Baseline khud ek value function hai jo saath-saath seekhta hai. Yehi idea actor-critic ka foundation hai — agla section.

class LogisticPolicyAgentWithBaseline(PolicyGradientAgent):

        self.α_θ = α_θ
        self.α_w = 0.1
        self.γ = 0.99
        self.num_feats = num_feats
        PolicyGradientAgent.__init__(
            self, name=prefix + "logistic_policy_gradient_with_baseline", actions=actions
        )
        self.reset()
    def act(self, state, reward):
        if self.previous_pair is not None:
            self.episode_history.append(Step(self.previous_pair, reward))
        π = LogisticPolicyAgent.π(self.θ, state)
        action = np.random.choice((0, 1), p=π)
        self.previous_pair = Pair(state.data, action)
        self.t += 1
        return action
    def reset(self):
        self.θ = np.zeros(self.num_feats)
        self.w = 0
        self.end_of_episode()
        PolicyGradientAgent.reset(self)
    def end_of_episode(self):
        self.t = 0
        T = len(getattr(self, "episode_history", []))
        G = 0
        grad_buf = []
        for t in reversed(range(T)):
            G = G * self.γ + self.episode_history[t].reward

Actor-Critic aur A2C

Actor-critic me do networks hote hain: actor policy seekhta hai, critic value function seekhta hai. Critic actor ko batata hai ki uska action kitna achha tha — isse REINFORCE ka Monte Carlo wait khatam ho jaata hai.

n-step actor-critic / A2C (Alg 5-3)

Har step pe critic se advantage lo — poora episode wait nahi karna. CartPole example me tile coding use hua (605 features) — continuous state ko tiles me baant kar linear function approximation. Important: critic ka learning rate actor se bada ya barabar rakho (α_w ≥ α_θ) — critic ko pehle sahi hona chahiye, tabhi actor ko sahi feedback milega.

Eligibility traces actor-critic (Alg 5-4)

Traces se credit assignment behtar hota hai — reward ka asar peeche ke states tak pahunchta hai. Comparison (Eq 5-11) dikhata hai ki REINFORCE, baseline, aur actor-critic me sirf G estimate karne ka tareeka alag hai — baaki sab same hai.

Actor-critic kyun best of both worlds hai?

  • Value methods ki tarah bootstrapping — har step seekho, episode ka wait nahi.
  • Policy methods ki tarah continuous actions aur stochastic policies.
  • Variance baseline se control hoti hai, bias bootstrapping se thoda aata hai — acceptable tradeoff.

A2C hi woh base hai jisse A3C, PPO, SAC sab bane hain — isiliye ise achhe se samjho.

# Cell 3 - tile coding (taken from https://github.com/MeepMoop/tilecoding)
class TileCoder:
    def __init__(
        self,
        tiles_per_dim,
        value_limits,
        tilings,
        offset=lambda n: 2 * np.arange(n) + 1,
    ):
        tiling_dims = np.array(np.ceil(tiles_per_dim), dtype=np.int) + 1
        self._offsets = (
            offset(len(tiles_per_dim))
            * np.repeat([np.arange(tilings)], len(tiles_per_dim), 0).T
            / float(tilings)
            % 1
        )
        self._limits = np.array(value_limits)
        self._norm_dims = np.array(tiles_per_dim) / (
            self._limits[:, 1] - self._limits[:, 0]
        )
        self._tile_base_ind = np.prod(tiling_dims) * np.arange(tilings)
        self._hash_vec = np.array(
            [np.prod(tiling_dims[0:i]) for i in range(len(tiles_per_dim))]
        )
        self._n_tiles = tilings * np.prod(tiling_dims)
    def __getitem__(self, x):
        off_coords = (
            (x - self._limits[:, 0]) * self._norm_dims + self._offsets
        ).astype(int)
        return self._tile_base_ind + np.dot(off_coords, self._hash_vec)
    @property
    def n_tiles(self):
        return self._n_tiles

        self.α_w = α_w
        self.γ = 0.99
        self.num_feats = num_feats
        # tile coder tiling dimensions, value limits, number of tilings

        lims = [(-0.72, 0.72), (-5, 5)]
        tilings = 5
        self.T = TileCoder(tiles_per_dim, lims, tilings)
        print(self.T.n_tiles)
        PolicyGradientAgent.__init__(
            self, name=prefix + "one_step_actor_critic", actions=actions
        )
        self.reset()
    def update(self, state, action, reward, next_state, terminal: bool):
        raw_state = state.copy()
        state = self.augment(state)
        next_state = self.augment(next_state)
        v = self.v(self.w, state)
        if terminal:
            δ = reward + self.γ * 0 - v
        else:
            δ = reward + self.γ * self.v(self.w, next_state) - v
        self.w += self.α_w * δ * self.Δv(self.w, state)
        self.θ += (
            self.α_θ * self.I * δ *
            LogisticPolicyAgent.Δ(self.θ, raw_state)[action]

        self.previous_pair = Pair(state.data, action)
        self.t += 1

        self.end_of_episode()

Off-Policy Actor-Critics

On-policy methods sirf current policy ke data se seekhte hain. Off-policy methods kisi bhi policy ke data se seekh sakte hain — purana data, random data, human demonstrations. Isse sample efficiency badhti hai, lekin correction chahiye: importance sampling.

Importance sampling (Eq 6-1) — dice example

Fair die ka mean 3.5 hai. Biased die ko bina roll kiye uska mean estimate karna ho to? Fair die ke rolls ko importance ratio (target prob / behavior prob) se weight karo. Result: 4.3 — biased die ka mean, bina use roll kiye. Yahi trick off-policy RL me use hoti hai — behavior policy β ke data ko target policy π ke hisaab se reweight karo.

GTD aur Greedy-GQ

  • GTD (Alg 6-1): gradient temporal-difference — linear function approximation ke saath stable rehta hai, jahan normal TD diverge kar sakta hai.
  • Greedy-GQ (Alg 6-2): target policy ke liye doosra model rakhta hai — greedy action ki value alag se estimate karo.

Off-PAC (Eq 6-3)

Off-policy actor-critic — actor ko importance ratio π/β se correct karo. Actor ka update rate critic se chhota rakho — critic pehle stable ho, phir actor badle. Off-PAC jaldi anneal karta hai (converge karta hai) kyunki off-policy data zyada hota hai.

Yaad rakho: Off-policy = data reuse. Replay buffer, logged data, human demos — sab off-policy ki wajah se possible hai. Lekin importance sampling se variance badhta hai — isiliye ratio ko clip karte hain (PPO me yehi idea hai).

DPG, DDPG aur TD3

Stochastic policies ko har action sample karna padta hai — slow hai. Deterministic policy har state ke liye ek hi specific action deti hai — sampling nahi, seedha action. Isse learning tez hoti hai, khaas taur par continuous actions me.

Deterministic policy gradients (DPG)

Policy μ(s) deterministic hai — ek state, ek action. Gradient (Eq 6-4): ∇J = E[∇μ(s) · ∇aQ(s,a)] — chain rule se policy ko Q-value badhane ki direction me move karo. Importance sampling ki zaroorat nahi (koi action distribution hi nahi hai) aur saare actions par maximize karne ki zaroorat nahi.

DDPG (Alg 6-3) — deep deterministic policy gradients

DPG + deep learning. Actor aur critic dono neural networks. DQN se liye gaye tricks: replay buffer (decorrelate), target networks (slow update, τ se), batch normalization, aur exploration ke liye action me noise (Gaussian noise, ya parameter noise — jo behtar hai). DDPG high-dimensional states aur continuous actions dono handle karta hai — isiliye industry me bahut popular hai.

TD3 (Alg 6-4) — twin delayed DDPG

DDPG ka improved version — 3 fixes:

  • Delayed policy updates (DPU): critic ko pehle stable hone do, phir actor update karo. Kam training = behtar performance (chicken-and-egg loop tootta hai).
  • Clipped double Q-learning (CDQ): do critics rakho, minimum value lo — overestimation ko underestimate se kaato. Ant environment me bahut fayda hua (coupled actions ki wajah se overestimation zyada tha).
  • Target policy smoothing (TPS): target action me noise daalo — Q-value ke sharp peaks smooth ho jaate hain.
Interview point: "DDPG vs TD3" — TD3 = DDPG + delayed updates + twin critics (min lo) + target smoothing. Book recommend karta hai: DDPG ke bajaye hamesha TD3 prefer karo.

Trust Region — KL Divergence, NPG, TRPO

Policy gradient me sabse mushkil sawaal: step size kitna ho? Zyada bada step policy ko kharab kar dega; zyada chhota step learning ko slow kar dega. Trust region methods iska principled jawab dete hain.

Advantage loss (Eq 6-5)

L(θ) = E[ln π(a|s) · A(s,a)] — advantage A = Q − V. Positive advantage wale actions ki probability badhao, negative wale ki ghatao. Advantage ek natural damping deta hai — agar policy galat direction me jaaye, negative advantage use wapas kheenchta hai.

Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence (Eq 6-6)

Do distributions kitni alag hain, ye naapne ka tareeka. Same distributions → KL = 0. Bahut alag → KL bada. Example: fair coin vs biased coin — KL nonzero. Fair die vs double-value die — KL near zero (overlap wali jagah same distribution hai). KL spread nahi naapta — sirf difference naapta hai.

Natural policy gradients (NPG) aur TRPO (Eq 6-7/6-8/6-9)

NPG: step size ko parameter space me nahi, policy distributions ke distance me naapo. TRPO: KL divergence ko constraint banao — nayi policy purani se δ se zyada alag nahi honi chahiye. Problem: constrained optimization ke liye conjugate gradient descent chahiye — complex aur slow. Aur multi-head networks (policy + value ek saath) TRPO me mushkil hain, kyunki KL value prediction ke baare me kuch nahi batata. Isiliye TRPO popular nahi hua — lekin uska simpler version PPO bahut popular hai (agla section).

Hiking analogy: Gradient ek compass reading hai — ek reading se seedha destination tak jaoge to bhatak jaoge. Har kilometer pe nayi reading lo (chhote steps). Trust region yehi karta hai — har step pe check karta hai ki policy zyada to nahi badal gayi.

# Cell 2 - KL divergence implementation (log base 2 = extra bits; guards against 0/inf)
def kld(p: np.ndarray, q: np.ndarray) -> float:
    """Kullback-Leibler divergence D(P || Q) for discrete distributions
    Parameters
    ----------
    p, q : array-like, dtype=float, shape=n
    Discrete probability distributions.

    relative_entropy = p * np.log2(p / q, where=p != 0)
    return np.sum(relative_entropy, where=np.isfinite(relative_entropy))

PPO — Clipped Objective

PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) aaj ka sabse zyada use hone wala RL algorithm hai. Iska idea TRPO se aaya, lekin implementation bahut simple hai: importance ratio ko clip kar do.

Clipped objective (Eq 6-11)

L = E[min(r·A, clip(r, 1−ε, 1+ε)·A)] — r = π_new/π_old (importance ratio). ε aam taur par 0.1–0.3. Agar advantage positive hai aur ratio 1+ε se zyada badhne lage, to loss clip ho jaata hai — policy zyada tezi se improve nahi kar sakti. Agar advantage negative hai, to min function loss ko bada rakhta hai — policy kharab actions se door bhaagti hai. Ye pessimistic (lower) bound banata hai — worst-case performance guarantee karta hai.

Value function aur entropy (Eq 6-12)

Final objective = clipped loss + C1·value loss + C2·entropy bonus. Value loss (squared error) se critic seekhta hai; entropy bonus exploration ko encourage karta hai (deterministic policy banne se rokta hai). C1/C2 hyperparameters hain — hyperparameter search se set karo.

PPO algorithm (Alg 6-5)

Multiple agents T steps tak policy chalate hain, advantages compute karte hain, phir K epochs tak minibatches par optimize karte hain. Koi replay buffer nahi — on-policy hai. Simple, stable, aur har framework me available hai.

Interview point: "PPO kyun popular hai?" — 3 reasons: TRPO jaisi stability bina conjugate gradient ke, simple implementation (sirf clipping), aur har problem par decent performance. Default choice hai jab pata nahi kaunsa algorithm use karein.

Reacher Case Study aur Doosre PG Algorithms

Simulation se real life tak — ek servo motor ko PPO se control karna. Ye experiment dikhata hai ki real hardware par RL kaise debug karte hain, aur hyperparameters kitne matter karte hain.

Servo reacher — setup

Ek servo motor ($20 se kam) + goal position. State = current position + goal. Action = servo position. Reward = goal se negative distance. Episode tab khatam jab servo goal ke 5% andar aa jaaye. States/actions −1 se 1 tak normalize.

Debugging story — learning rate

Pehla attempt: fixed goal −1, PPO default learning rate 2.5e−4. Policy atak gayi — seekh hi nahi rahi thi. Debug karke pata chala: learning rate bahut chhota tha itne simple problem ke liye. Rate ko 0.05 tak badhaya to kuch hi updates me solve ho gaya. Seekh: simple problem = bada learning rate.

Steps aur minibatch

  • Minibatch 2–4 (bahut chhota) — baar-baar retrain karo, jaldi solve karo.
  • Steps per update 32–64 — minibatch se bada rakho taaki samples decorrelate ho jaayein aur ek update me multiple episodes aa jaayein.
  • Network chhota rakho (autoencoder-like fan-in) — overfitting roko.

Random goals aur simulation

Har episode me random goal (−1 se 1) — agent ko generalize karna pada. Real servo par tuning mushkil hai (5 din me ek servo kharab ho gaya — wear and tear!), isliye simulation me hyperparameter tuning karo, phir real par deploy karo. Final policy ~1,000 steps me stable ho gayi.

Doosre PG algorithms — quick tour

  • Retrace(λ): importance ratio ko 1 par clip karo — stable lekin biased.
  • ACER: Retrace + replay + TRPO + dueling — complex lekin continuous actions par DQN jaisi performance.
  • ACKTR: K-FAC approximation se natural gradient fast calculate karo — TRPO jaisi performance, kam compute.
  • Emphatic methods (Geoff-PAC): counterfactual objectives — promising lekin abhi niche hai.

Kaunsa algorithm chunein?

Book ki recommendations: TD3 prefer karo DDPG se; unstable dynamics wale environments me trust region methods (PPO/TRPO); stable dynamics me deterministic methods; aur agar performance/robustness ki chinta hai to simple algorithms + chhote networks (PPO ya TD3).

Deterministic policy sirf ek hi tareeka seekhti hai task karne ka. Lekin agar environment badal jaaye ya agent raaste se bhatak jaaye, to wo fail ho jaati hai. Maximum entropy RL agent ko saare tareeke seekhne par majboor karta hai — robust policy banti hai.

Shannon entropy (Eq 7-1)

H(X) = −Σ p(x)·log p(x) — random variable me kitni information hai, bits me. Coin ke 2 states → 1 bit. Die ke 6 states → 2.58 bits. Zyada choices = zyada entropy = zyada information.

Maximum entropy objective (Eq 7-2)

π* = argmax Σ [r + α·H(π(a|s))] — reward ke saath entropy bonus bhi maximize karo. α (temperature) control karta hai kitna exploration chahiye: α ≈ 0 matlab deterministic policy; α bada matlab zyada random, zyada exploration.

Candy store analogy

Dukaan ke beech me bachche ke paas sau directions hain — har direction me nayi candy. Darwaze ke paas sirf 2–3 choices. Entropy kehta hai: beech ki taraf jao, jahan choices zyada hain. Agent bhi wahi karta hai — aise states ki taraf jata hai jahan action choices zyada hain, taaki sab kuch explore ho.

Kab max-entropy, kab deterministic?

Discrete problem jisme sirf ek optimal trajectory hai aur agent kabhi bhatakta nahi — deterministic theek hai. Continuous problems jisme agent optimal path se hat sakta hai — wahan max-entropy robust policy deta hai.

Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)

SAC maximum entropy objective ka off-policy policy gradient implementation hai. "Soft" isliye kyunki policy stochastic hai — hard (deterministic) Q-learning/DDPG ke opposite.

Soft value functions (Eq 7-3/7-4/7-5)

V(s) = E[Q(s,a) − α·log π(a|s)] — state ki value = action value + entropy bonus. Q-function bhi isi soft value se update hoti hai. Critic ka objective hai soft Bellman error minimize karna.

Policy objective (Eq 7-6)

Policy KL divergence minimize karke seekhti hai — Stein variational gradient descent se derive hua hai. Implementation me autodiff use hota hai, manual derivation ki zaroorat nahi.

Practical details

  • Temperature sensitivity: performance α par bahut sensitive hai — har problem ke liye scan karo. Bade rewards chhote α ko overwhelm kar dete hain — isliye rewards rescale karo.
  • Gaussian actions: reparameterization trick se continuous actions handle hote hain. Discrete actions out-of-the-box nahi chalte — unke liye alag SAC variant hai (jo zero tuning par DQN se compete karta hai).
  • Auto temperature (Eq 7-7): temperature ko constraint banao — policy ki entropy ek minimum se zyada honi chahiye. Temperature khud seekhta hai — hyperparameter tuning khatam. Ye SAC ko hyperparameter-free (lagbhag) banata hai.
Interview point: "SAC kyun achha hai?" — off-policy (sample efficient) + max-entropy (robust) + auto temperature (no tuning). Continuous control ke liye default choice hai.

MDP ko Dobara Sochna — POMDP aur CMDP

MDP assume karta hai ki agent state ko perfectly observe kar sakta hai. Real life me aisa nahi hota — sensors limited hain, kuch cheezein hidden hain. Jab Markov assumption toot jaati hai, to policy converge hi nahi karti — chahe kitna bhi hyperparameter tune kar lo.

Partially Observable MDP (POMDP)

POMDP me state ke saath observations (Ω) aur observation function (O) bhi hoti hai — jo state+action ko observation probabilities me map karti hai. Agent ko belief state (b) banana padta hai — "mujhe lagta hai environment is state me hai". Belief state ko track karne ke liye Kalman filter ya neural Bayes filter use hota hai; simplest tareeka hai state representation ke end me RNN laga do — past observations se belief ban jaata hai.

Case study — autonomous vehicles

Intersection par left/right/straight kab jaana hai — camera/LIDAR line-of-sight hai, aas-paas ki speeding car dikhai nahi deti. LSTM-enabled policy se agent ne seekha kab nikalna safe hai aur kab collision hoga. Reward engineering se unsafe situations avoid karna seekhaya gaya. Phir hierarchical RL add karke high-level actions (go/stop/follow) bhi seekhe.

Contextual MDP (CMDP)

Diabetes patient ko drug dena — MDP same hai (sugar levels → drug), lekin patient ka gender/age observation ko affect karta hai. Context fixed parameters hain (har measurement par nahi badalte) — isliye POMDP se alag hai. Context ko model karne se search space chhota hota hai aur learning tez hoti hai. Recommendation systems me CMDP bahut use hota hai (chhote episodes, bahut saare independent users).

Changing action sets aur regularized MDPs

  • Stochastic action sets: kabhi-kabhi actions available nahi hote (road band, ad time-dependent) — sirf observed actions se policy seekho.
  • Lifelong learning: naye actions add hote hain (naya robot skill, naye ads) — policy ko do hisson me baanto: state → meta-action, meta-action → action. Sirf meta-action model retrain karo.
  • Regularized MDPs: SAC jaise algorithms reward me regularizer add karte hain — prove hua hai ki sahi rules follow karo to convergence guarantee rehti hai.

Hierarchical RL aur Skills

Complex problems ko chhote parts me baanto — ye engineering ka pehla rule hai. Hierarchical RL (HRL) yahi karta hai: low-level policies chhoti "skills" seekhti hain (cheez uthana), high-level policy planning karti hai (kab kaunsi skill use karni hai).

Options framework

Ek option = (initiation set, policy, termination condition). Jaise "coffee banao" option ke andar "cup uthao", "button dabao" skills hain. High-level policy options chunti hai, low-level policy environment se interact karti hai. Isse search space chhota hota hai — walking seekhte time hands par chalne wale actions try karne ki zaroorat nahi.

HIRO — data-efficient HRL

Problem: high-level replay buffer me purani low-level policies ka data hota hai — representative nahi hai. Fix: dynamic relabeling — buffer ke goals ko us hisaab se badal do jo actually hua. Result: legged robot ne chalna bhi seekha aur block pushing, hidden goal finding, resource gathering jaise high-level tasks bhi.

DIAYN — unsupervised skill discovery

Reward ke bina skills seekho! Diversity Is All You Need — skills distinguishable honi chahiye (alag states visit karein), aur diverse honi chahiye (entropy maximize karo). Discriminator entropy ko reward banata hai. Result: HalfCheetah ne running, crawling, sitting — bina kisi reward ke — seekh liya. Ye skills baad me kisi bhi task me reuse ho sakti hain.

HAAR — skills ko task se jodo

Pretrained skills hain, ab unhe specific problem me kaise use karein? Advantage-based rewards — high-level policy ka advantage low-level skills ko reward karta hai. Pixel distance nahi (kyunki pixels me distance physical distance nahi hota), advantage use karo. Skill repeat karne ka time anneal karo — long-range exploration badhta hai.

Interview point: "HRL kab use karein?" — jab agent complex ho (robot arm, text agent), environment nahi. Agar environment complex hai lekin agent simple hai, to HRL fayda nahi dega. Aur skills hamesha human-designed nahi hoti — agent khud discover kar sakta hai (DIAYN).

Multi-Agent RL (MARL)

Ab tak ek agent tha. Real life me kayi agents hote hain — cooperative, competitive, ya dono. MARL in sabko model karta hai.

3 settings

  • Cooperative: sab agents ek common goal ke liye — jaise taxation policy optimize karna (AI Economist).
  • Competitive: ek ka fayda doosre ka nuksaan — zero-sum, jaise trading aur advertising bidding.
  • Mixed: ad marketplace — global revenue maximize karo (cooperative) lekin advertisers kam pay karna chahte hain (competitive).

Frameworks — Markov game vs extensive-form game

Markov game (MG): MDP ka extension — kayi agents parallel actions lete hain, alag rewards milte hain, state global hai. Industry me zyada use hota hai (simple mental model). Extensive-form game (EFG): tree form — chance, state, actions sequential dikhte hain. Rock-Paper-Scissors EFG me: agent 2 ko nahi pata agent 1 ne kya kiya — ye information set hai (simultaneous game ka representation).

Centralized vs decentralized

Centralized learning, distributed execution: sabka experience ek learner ke paas, policies distribute karo — exploration tez hoti hai. Decentralized (DEC-MDP): har agent locally seekhta hai — scalable hai, alag capabilities handle karta hai, aur single-agent algorithms reuse kar sakte ho. Problem: agents badhte hain to communication bottleneck banta hai.

Challenges — kyun MARL mushkil hai?

  • Nonstationarity: doosre agents environment badal rahe hain — single-agent algorithms (Q-learning, PG) converge nahi karte.
  • Scalability: agents zyada ho to sync aur communication mushkil.
  • Optimality define karna mushkil: nontrivial environments me convergence guarantees nahi hain.
  • Diversity: har agent ka goal aur capabilities alag ho sakte hain.

Ek classic failure pattern: galat problem definition — centralized model banaya, production me pata chala agents communicate nahi kar sakte. Wapas start karna pada. Isiliye problem define karne me time lagao (Chapter 9).

Expert Guidance — Imitation, Inverse RL, Curriculum

Agent ko scratch se seekhne do ya expert ki madad lo? Bachche bhi pehle explore karte hain, phir curriculum se seekhte hain. RL me bhi expert guidance learning ko bahut tez kar sakti hai — agar sahi tareeke se use karein.

Behavior cloning — simple lekin overfit karta hai

Expert ke actions ko supervised learning se copy karo. Problem: jo state expert ne kabhi dikhayi nahi, wahan agent ko kuch nahi pata — aur complex problems me aisi states bahut hain.

Imitation RL — DQfD, GAIL, SQIL

  • DQfD: Bellman update me expert se difference ka supervised loss add karo — lekin overfitting rokne ke liye regularization.
  • GAIL: adversarially action distributions ko expert ki taraf perturb karo.
  • SQIL: sabse simple — replay buffer ko control karo aur reward signal reconstruct karo. Koi extrinsic reward nahi ho to bhi seekhta hai. Expert advice ka amount weight se control karo — shuru me zyada, baad me pure RL.

Inverse RL (IRL) — reward hi seekh lo

Expert optimal nahi bhi ho sakta — to uski trajectory copy karne ke bajaye reward function seekho jo expert behavior ko explain kare. 5 steps: expert demos lo → reward model banao → MDP solve karo → reward update karo (expert trajectory se match karne ke liye) → repeat. Problem: baar-baar RL retrain karna padta hai (slow), aur failed demos bhi useful hote hain — unke rewards demote karo.

Curriculum learning, meta-learning, transfer learning

  • Curriculum: aasan subgoals se shuru karo, dheere-dheere mushkil — jaise snowboarding ya science seekhte hain. Self-play (teacher-student) se curriculum auto-generate hoti hai.
  • Residual RL: do policies add karo — ek accurate model wali, ek mushkil part (grasping) wali.
  • Meta-learning (learning to learn): kam data se tez seekhna — curiosity, evolutionary, adversarial approaches ka mash-up.
  • Transfer learning: 3 types — same task same domain (sim-to-real), same domain different task (curriculum), different domain (Pong policy se Breakout). Sabse practical: pehle se solved similar problem ka experience reuse karo.

Problem Definition — RL Problem kaise Pehchanein?

Har problem RL problem nahi hai. Pehchanne ke liye 2 high-level signs aur 5 low-level indicators hain.

RL problems sequential hote hain

ML single-step decisions optimize karta hai — aaj ka best action. RL trajectories optimize karta hai — aaj ka action kal ko affect karta hai. CEO bhi aisa hi sochta hai: aaj customer se har paisa nichodo mat, relationship banao taaki long-term profit ho. Ad placement ya recommendations me ML click-bait de sakta hai (clicks zyada, satisfaction kam) — RL long-term engagement dekhta hai.

RL problems strategic hote hain

Software processes automate karta hai, ML decisions automate karta hai, RL strategies automate karta hai. Strategies rare hoti hain, lekin unka impact sabse zyada hota hai — company ki direction, healthcare policy, taxation (MARL se taxation optimize ho raha hai).

5 low-level indicators

  • Entity: koi concrete cheez/person jo environment se interact kare.
  • Environment: bounded context — interface ke peeche simulation ho ya real life, data same hona chahiye.
  • State: kya observe kar sakte ho? Informative, clean, uncorrelated features chuno — kam features behtar hain.
  • Action: action se environment ki state badalni chahiye — aur actions kam rakho.
  • Success quantify karo: profit? clicks? lives saved? Stakeholder ki bhasha me — accuracy nahi, business metric me.

Kab seekhta hai? — Online, offline, concurrent

  • Online: live interact karke seekho — standard, lekin sample inefficient.
  • Offline/batch: logged data se seekho — bina environment touch kiye. Sample efficient, lekin explore nahi kar sakte; distribution shift problem hoti hai (value methods aur PG dono struggle karte hain).
  • Concurrent: kayi agents ek saath explore karein — coordinated exploration se state-action space jaldi cover hota hai (jaise thousands of customers parallel me).

State aur Policy Engineering

Algorithm chunne se pehle state aur policy design karo. Book ka claim: ek achha feature (ya state representation) algorithm badalne se zyada fayda deta hai.

State engineering (state representation learning)

  • Forward models: lab me ground truth se state predict karna seekho, phir field me deploy karo.
  • Constraints: priors use karo — jaise features strictly positive honi chahiye, to policy ko wo states try hi mat karne do.
  • Dimensionality reduction: PCA se state space chhota karo; autoencoders se informative representation seekho (DonkeyCar me VAE — Chapter 7).
  • World models: offline data se environment ka model banao, phir usme agent ko online ki tarah train karo.

Example: robotics me gripper ki height ek simple measurement add karne se unseen objects uthane me 12% improvement mila — pixels se seekhne se zyada fayda ek achhe sensor se hua.

Policy ke 3 phases

(1) State representation ko internal format me convert karo. (2) Internal model — decision engine. (3) Hidden representation ko environment ke layak action me convert karo. Har phase ko alag se engineer kar sakte ho.

Discrete vs continuous states

Discrete: simple, debug karna aasan, table use kar sakte ho, Q-learning chalta hai. Lekin discretization se information loss hota hai (NYC grid me 2-mile cells — vehicle customer se 2 mile door ho sakta hai). Continuous: infinite states, approximation chahiye. Linear approximation provably convergent hai; nonlinear (neural nets) ka koi convergence proof nahi hai (2020 tak) — lekin practice me kaam karta hai. Complexity badhane ke liye polynomial, Fourier, ya radial basis transforms use karo — linear ki guarantees rehti hain.

Discretization techniques

Binning (fixed ranges), tile coding (overlapping tiles — zyada accurate), hashing (unbounded features jaise IP addresses), supervised (labeled data se), unsupervised (k-means se bin positions). Mixed spaces ho to unify karo, continuous me convert karo, ya split karke subproblems banao.

Action spaces

Binary (simplest), continuous (Gaussian mean+std predict karo), hybrid (discrete+continuous — split karo ya parameterized DQN/action branching), massive (YouTube ke 10^8 items — embeddings se reduce karo ya top-k ranking karo), aur kab action lena hai (options framework, ya repeat-action — motor wear kam karne ke liye).

Exploration aur Reward Engineering

RL ki zyada tar problems exploration problems hain — agent ko pata hi nahi goal kahan hai. Aur reward design karna sabse important kaam hai — galat reward = galat solution.

Exploration — bachche kaise explore karte hain?

Bachche depth-first search jaisa karte hain — dead end tak jaate hain, mental map banate hain, phir goal milne par us map se tez pahunchte hain. RL agents goal accidentally dhoondhte hain. Is gap ko bharne ke liye:

  • Visitation counts / UCB / Thompson sampling: kam visit kiye states ko bonus do.
  • Information gain (VIME): surprising trajectories ki taraf jao — KL divergence se naapo.
  • Curiosity (prediction error): next state predict karo, galat prediction par reward do. Problem: noisy TV — agent TV dekh kar atak jaata hai kyunki wo predict nahi kar sakta (Fig 9-7).
  • Random network distillation (RND): random network ke embeddings predict karo — error = novelty. Noisy TV problem solve hoti hai kyunki static image ko model kar sakte ho.
  • Episodic curiosity: states ke beech distance naapo — naye states ko reward do, paas wale ko punish. Depth-first exploration milti hai.

Reward engineering guidelines

Reward hona chahiye: fast (millions of calls), true reward se correlate (proxy sahi ho), de-noised (variance kam), nonfunctional requirements encode kare (safety, efficiency), time consider kare (discounting), plateaus na ho (states distinguishable ho), smooth (jolting actions na aayein), simple (complex reward = unexpected policy).

Reward shaping — normalize, clip, transform, model

  • Normalize: scale sahi rakho — gradients stable rehte hain.
  • Clip: DQN ±1 clip karta hai, lekin Pac-Man me dots aur fruit same ho jaate hain — galat proxy. Normalize karne se agent ghosts hunt karne lagta hai.
  • Transform: goal se door ho to reward tezi se badhe — lekin slippery slope hai.
  • Model: domain knowledge daalo — jaise robot ko pehle khada hona hai, phir bhaagna hai.

Common rewards aur reward hacking

Sparse (sirf goal par), distance-to-goal (sabse strong proxy), punish steps (kam steps encourage), punish damage (lekin cliff se 1 mile door bhaagne lag sakta hai), image-based (goal images se reward). Aur reward hacking se bacho — Unity example (Fig 9-9): box se wall jump karna tha, agent ne side se sneak karna seekh liya. Reward sahi tha, lekin agent ne exploit kar liya.

Yaad rakho: "There is no optimal policy without a reward, and your reward changes the optimal policy." Reward badlo to optimal policy badal jaati hai — isiliye reward design sabse zyada soch-samajh kar karo.

Frameworks aur Scaling RL

Proof-of-concept se production tak jaane ke liye frameworks chahiye — aur phir scaling. Framework chunna aur scale karna dono alag skills hain.

RL framework me kya dekhein?

  • Abstractions: components swap karna aasan ho (policy, exploration, replay buffer).
  • Simplicity: codebase navigate karna aasan ho — install karne me weeks na lagein.
  • Observability: logging/monitoring built-in ho ya add karna aasan ho.
  • Scalability, minimal dependencies, docs aur examples, included algorithms, support, commercial backing.

Research frameworks (OpenAI baselines) popular hain lekin production ke liye nahi bane. Stable Baselines kam visible hai lekin use karna aasan aur robust hai. Deep learning ke liye PyTorch vs TensorFlow — PyTorch ki API simple hai; lock-in se savdhaan raho. Ancillary tools: Reverb (replay queue), Apache Bookkeeper (distributed log).

Scaling — kyun mushkil hai?

Sequential (actions ordered hone chahiye), combining experience (sync + bandwidth), independence (koi agent atak jaaye to blocking), environment complexity (3D simulators ko GPU chahiye), cost (GPT-3 train karna ~$10M). Zyada horsepower dene se training tez nahi hoti — dedicated architectures chahiye.

Scaling architectures

  • Gorila: parameter server + actors + learners — pehla distributed DQN, 20x faster.
  • A3C: single machine, 16 CPU cores, shared memory (Hogwild — bina lock ke), Gorila jaisi performance 1/8 time me.
  • PAAC: single learner + GPU + kayi actors — A3C jaisi performance phir 1/8 time me (12 hours me Atari).
  • Ape-X: distributed replay — 3x A3C performance, lekin 16x CPUs.
  • DD-PPO: decentralized distributed — 256 GPUs tak near-linear scaling.
  • IMPALA: queue se actor-learner decouple — off-policy correction (Retrace). 1B frames: DeepMind Lab $236, Football $899.
  • SEED: central inference — environments distributed, model central. IMPALA se ~2x tez, 1B frames: Lab $54, Football $369.
Yaad rakho: Fastest hamesha best nahi hota. DD-PPO self-healing (cluster) ke liye behtar ho sakta hai. Aur diminishing returns hain — IMPALA se SEED sirf 2x behtar hai, C++ likhne ki keemat par.

Evaluation — Performance kaise Naapein?

RL me "kab khatam hua?" batana mushkil hai — profit hamesha aur badha sakte ho, lives hamesha aur bacha sakte ho. Isiliye evaluation ke sahi measures chahiye.

Policy performance vs learning performance

  • Policy performance: policy kitni achhi hai — finite horizon me sum of rewards; infinite me discounted. Stakeholders ko discounted profit nahi, actual profit chahiye — final measure me discounting hata do.
  • Learning performance: kitni jaldi optimal policy seekhi — time to near-optimality. Time ko environment steps, wall-clock, ya training steps me naapo.
  • Regret (Fig 10-7): optimal reward aur actual reward ka difference over time — solar plant example: theoretical max power vs actual power ka gap.
  • Average reward: sparse rewards me average bekaar hai — median ya percentile use karo.

Statistical comparisons — savdhaan raho

Tests: Student's t-test (normal + equal variance), Welch's t-test (unequal variance OK), Wilcoxon Mann-Whitney (nonparametric, medians), bootstrap/permutation (bade samples chahiye, ~1000+). Problems: RL results normal nahi hote (outliers aate hain), rewards truncated hote hain, variances alag hote hain. Colas et al. ne dikhaya: TD3 vs SAC compare karne ke liye kam se kam 15 repeats chahiye. Aur seeds set karna mushkil hai — Python, NumPy, Gym, DL framework, RL framework, replay buffer — har jagah seed set karo, tabhi do runs same aayenge.

Behavior suite aur explainability

Behavior suite: 7 challenges (credit assignment, basic, scalability, noise, memory, generalization, exploration) — radar plot se algorithms compare karo (Fig 10-8). XAI: tree/linear models inherently explainable; neural nets ke liye black-box methods — global (U-trees) ya local (counterfactual, saliency maps). RL me explainability aur mushkil hai kyunki actions sequential hain aur effects compound hote hain.

Deployment — Production me Agent

Deployment ka matlab hai proof-of-concept ko reliable product banana. RL me ye ML se zyada mushkil hai — policy nonstationary hai, state preserve karni padti hai, aur retraining batch me nahi hoti.

3 phases — priorities badalti hain

  • Development: flexibility aur speed — rapid experimentation, feedback loop chhota rakho.
  • Hardening: robustness — offline training, provenance (kya chal raha hai, ye pata hona chahiye).
  • Production: reliability — monitoring aur feedback mechanisms, kyunki policy diverge kar sakti hai.

MLOps — reliability, scalability, flexibility

Reliability (testing, CI/CD, architecture), scalability (har RL product ko laptop se zyada compute chahiye), flexibility (future-proof — tools jo fixed way enforce karte hain, jaldi outdated ho jaate hain). Hierarchy of needs: required (environment + compute) → valuable (pipelines, provenance) → optimal (self-healing, continuous learning).

Architecture abstractions

Serving interface (MDP interface + exploration), log (disaster recovery + batch learning + queue), parameter server, policy servers (replicate kar sakte ho), replay buffers, actors/critics split, learning (train aur serve ka code same ho), data augmentation (state representation models).

Tooling — monitoring, logging, CI/CD, experiment tracking

  • Monitoring: RL metrics + ML stats + software metrics — hierarchy banao. Users ko odd result mila to trust toot jaata hai — kabhi-kabhi broken product dikhana bad prediction se behtar hai.
  • Logging/tracing: MDP state changes ka dedicated log rakho; correlation IDs se trajectories trace karo (microservices jaisa).
  • CI/CD + unit testing for data: incoming data ka schema test karo — galat data catastrophic failure la sakta hai.
  • Experiment tracking: TensorBoard simple hai lekin scale nahi karta; har run ke metrics database me rakho.
  • Hyperparameter tuning: Optuna — performance model banakar tez search karo, kharab runs ko jaldi roko.

Deploying multiple agents aur policies

Canary (kuch users ko naya agent — stickiness zaroori), shadowing (hidden agent real data par, lekin explore nahi kar sakta), A/B testing (bandits se agents chuno), ensembles (diverse agents ka combined decision robust hota hai). Policy updates: continuous se batch se never-update tak continuum (Fig 10-9) — jitna kam update karo, utna behtar (monitoring burden kam). Stateless aur immutable deployments prefer karo — policy update ko naya deployment treat karo.

Safe, Secure aur Ethical RL

RL agents robots aur cars control kar sakte hain — galat action se nuksaan ho sakta hai. Aur attackers ke liye RL ka attack surface ML se bada hai. Ethics bhi utni hi important hai — RL poori strategies automate karta hai, sirf decisions nahi.

Safe RL

  • Constrained optimization: TRPO/PPO me constraints daalo — jaise web service replicas available capacity se zyada deploy na karein.
  • Reward shaping: dangerous behaviors ko penalize karo — jaise robotic arm ko fully extend karne par penalty (metal fatigue).
  • Safe sets + filters: external filter unsafe actions ko roke — jaise vehicles ke beech minimum distance.
  • Reset controllers: agent ko safe state me wapas le jaane ka automated tareeka seekho.
  • Formally constrained RL: mathematical guarantees — lekin sirf toy examples me possible hai abhi.

Important: ye methods training ke dauraan safety guarantee nahi karte — sirf kaafi training ke baad approximately safe hote hain. Unsafe acts karke hi safe hona seekhte hain — jaise bachcha garam cup chhoo kar seekhta hai.

Secure RL

White-box attacks (source code access) aur black-box attacks (probing). ML attacks: training phase me poisoning (galat labels daalo), prediction phase me adversarial examples (transferable hote hain — doosra model train karke pehle par attack karo). RL me attack surface bada hai: environment, observation, reward, action, replay buffer, parameter server — sab target ho sakte hain. Mitigations: robust training (perturbations), attack detection (next-state prediction se divergence naapo), hierarchical analysis. Lekin pehle basics: Symantec report — 65% attacks phishing se, 23% unpatched software se — RL-specific exploits sirf ~3%. Basics pehle, fancy defenses baad me.

Ethical RL — checklist

Har project me ye sawaal poochho: Bias (kya kuch groups zyada affect hote hain?), personal impact (log kaisa feel karenge? human-in-loop possible hai?), climate (energy cost worth hai?), unintended use, nefarious use, legality, human rights (privacy?), justification/explainability, performance misinformation (kya results misinterpret ho sakte hain?). Tum power me ho — strategies automate kar rahe ho. Stakeholders khud impact assess nahi kar sakte; tumhari zimmedari hai simple bhasha me pros/cons batana. Aur yaad rakho: kabhi-kabhi decision itna important hota hai ki use human ko hi lena chahiye — algorithm sirf information de sakta hai, zimmedari nahi le sakta.

Tips & Tricks — Framing se Debugging tak

Book ke ant me practical tips ka khazana hai — jo cheezein chapters me fit nahi hui, lekin production me kaam aati hain.

Framing the problem

Random policy ko imagine karo — kya wo kabhi sahi karta hai? Agar random policy kabhi sahi nahi karti, to RL bhi nahi kar payega. Khud agent bano — kya tum sahi decisions le sakte ho in observations se? Baselines use karo: pehle random agent, phir cross-entropy method, phir simple Q-learning ya policy gradient. Har naye algorithm ko scientific experiment treat karo.

Your data

  • Rescale: observations ko mean 0, std 1 karo (rolling stats ya observed limits se). Data statistics badal sakte hain — nonstationarity se savdhaan.
  • Decorrelate: z-transform, PCA, ya diff se correlation hatao.
  • Monitor sab kuch: reward distributions (outliers?), inputs, value predictions, gradients (bahut bade?), action entropy (explore kar raha hai ya atak gaya?), KL divergence.
  • Discretization badhao: actions ke beech time badhao (frame skipping) — training stable aur tez hoti hai. Socho: tum kitni baar decision loge?

Training

Kayin environments par develop karo (simple se realistic, bade/chhote, observable/partially observable) — ek environment par overfit mat karo. Alag seeds use karo — ek seed par achha result chance ho sakta hai. Hyperparameter sensitivity test karo — agar sirf ek specific setting par kaam karta hai, to overfitting hai. Learning rate schedules try karo — fixed value rarely best hoti hai.

Evaluation aur deployment

Visualize karo pehle, statistics baad me. Agar performance curves me clear gap hai kayi seeds par, to improvement real hai. Stochastic policies ko evaluate karte time deterministic version use karo aur exploration band karo. Deployment me composability socho — off-the-shelf components jodo; frameworks help karte hain lekin Gym jaisa standard abhi ek hi hai.

Debugging — sabse zyada time yahin lagta hai

Deep RL "brittle, hard to reproduce, unreliable across runs" hai. Engstrom et al. ne dikhaya: code-level optimizations ka asar algorithm choice se zyada hota hai (PPO vs TRPO comparison me). RND research me ek bug mila — value function oscillate kar rahi thi kyunki ek array galti se zero ho raha tha har episode start pe. Fix karte hi performance dramatically behtar. Rules: kam code likho (har line ek potential bug hai), off-the-shelf libraries use karo, simple se shuru karo (linear approximator), fixed seeds + single threading pehle test karo, phir scale karo. Monitoring list: observations/actions/rewards log karo, value function visualize karo, gradients dekho, entropy dekho.

Jab algorithm solve nahi kar pata

MountainCar-v0 conceptually simple hai lekin RL ke liye mushkil — ye exploration aur credit assignment problem hai, algorithm ki nahi. Solution: exploration behtar karo ya reward badlo. Problem simplify karo — observation space chhota karo, phir complex version par jao.

F1End Matter · Interview FAQs
Interview FAQs
RL interviews (current era) ke liye — bandits se lekar deployment aur safety tak, 37 sawaal-jawaab. Har jawaab isi book ke concepts par aadharit hai.
Q01 RL kya hai — ML se kaise alag hai?
RL sequential decisions seekhta hai — ek ke baad ek faisle jinka long-term asar hota hai. ML single-step decisions karta hai (ye photo me kya hai?). RL ka goal total (discounted) reward maximize karna hai; ML ka goal prediction accuracy. RL = strategy, ML = decision, software = process.
Q02 MDP kya hai? Uske parts kya hain?
Markov Decision Process — states (S), actions (A), transition model p(s′,r|s,a), reward, aur policy π(a|s). Markov property: agla state sirf current state aur action par depend karta hai. Agar problem MDP me likh sakte ho, to RL apply kar sakte ho.
Q03 Bellman equation kya kehti hai?
V(s) = expected reward + γ × V(next state). Optimal version: V*(s) = max_a [r + γ·V*(s′)]. Matlab: kisi state ki value = wahan se milne wala reward + aage ki states ki discounted value. Saari value-based RL isi equation ko solve karti hai.
Q04 Exploration vs exploitation kya hai?
Exploitation = jo best lagta hai use karo. Exploration = naye options try karo. Agar sirf exploit karo to behtar option miss ho sakta hai; sirf explore karo to kabhi fayda nahi milega. ε-greedy, UCB, softmax, annealing — ye sab is tradeoff ko handle karne ke tareeke hain.
Q05 ε-greedy vs UCB — kaunsa behtar hai?
ε-greedy random explore karta hai — simple hai. UCB kam-try kiye actions ko bonus deta hai — uncertainty ko track karta hai, isliye smarter hai. Production me ε-greedy se shuru karte hain, phir UCB ya Thompson sampling par jaate hain.
Q06 Monte Carlo vs TD learning — farak kya hai?
MC poora episode khatam hone ke baad seekhta hai (high variance, zero bias, model-free). TD har step pe seekhta hai — bootstrapping karta hai (low variance, thoda bias). TD aam taur par tez hai; n-step methods dono ke beech ka bridge hain.
Q07 Q-learning kya hai? Off-policy kyun kehte hain?
Q(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α[r + γ·max Q(s′,a′) − Q(s,a)]. Off-policy isliye kyunki target me max action ki value li jaati hai — chahe agent ne wo action liya ho ya nahi. Isiliye kisi bhi data (replay buffer, purana data) se seekh sakta hai.
Q08 SARSA Q-learning se kaise alag hai?
SARSA on-policy hai — jo action actually liya, usi ki value se update hota hai: Q(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α[r + γ·Q(s′,a′) − Q(s,a)]. Cliff Gridworld me SARSA safe path leta hai (kyunki exploration me girne ka experience aata hai), Q-learning optimal lekin risky path leta hai.
Q09 Double Q-learning kyun chahiye?
Q-learning max operator ki wajah se values overestimate karta hai. Double Q-learning do tables rakhta hai — ek se action chuno, doosre se value lo. Overestimation bias khatam hota hai. TD3 me yehi idea twin critics ke saath use hota hai.
Q10 n-step methods aur eligibility traces kya hain?
n-step: n steps ka actual reward lo, phir bootstrap karo — TD aur MC ke beech. Eligibility traces: har state-action ko trace do — recent visits ko zyada credit. SARSA(λ) me λ = 0 matlab 1-step TD, λ = 1 matlab MC. Practice me λ < 1 use hota hai.
Q11 DQN kya hai? Tabular Q-learning se behtar kyun hai?
DQN = Q-learning + neural network. Table ki jagah network Q-values predict karta hai — continuous/high-dimensional states (jaise Atari pixels) handle kar sakta hai aur unseen states par generalize karta hai. Tabular methods ko quantization aur feature engineering chahiye.
Q12 DQN ke 2 main tricks kya hain?
Experience replay (transitions buffer me store karke random sample karo — correlation tootta hai, data reuse hota hai) aur target network (target calculate karne ke liye network ki slow-updated copy rakho — moving target problem khatam). Inke bina DQN diverge kar jaata hai.
Q13 Rainbow DQN ke 6 components kya hain?
Double Q-learning, n-step returns, distributional RL (C51 — 51 bins me reward distribution), prioritized experience replay, noisy nets (exploration), dueling networks (V + A alag se). Har component ka ablation study kiya gaya tha.
Q14 Policy gradient theorem kya hai?
∇J(θ) = E[G·∇ln π(a|s)] — jin actions se achha return mila, unki probability badhao. Policy seedha optimize hoti hai — value function ki zaroorat nahi (lekin baseline se variance kam hoti hai). Continuous actions aur stochastic policies ke liye best hai.
Q15 REINFORCE ki problem kya hai? Baseline kaise fix karta hai?
REINFORCE Monte Carlo returns use karta hai — high variance, slow learning. Baseline V(s) subtract karke advantage (G − V) use karo — gradient ka mean same rehta hai (unbiased) lekin variance kam ho jaati hai. Yehi actor-critic ka foundation hai.
Q16 Actor-critic kya hai? A2C vs A3C?
Actor policy seekhta hai, critic value function seekhta hai aur actor ko feedback deta hai. A2C synchronous version hai (n-step actor-critic). A3C asynchronous — kayi threads parallel me, shared memory (Hogwild). Dono ka core algorithm same hai.
Q17 DDPG kya hai? DQN se kaise alag hai?
DDPG deterministic policy seekhta hai continuous actions ke liye — actor seedha action predict karta hai (probability nahi). DQN discrete actions ke liye hai. DDPG me replay buffer + target networks (actor aur critic dono ke) + action noise for exploration use hota hai.
Q18 TD3 DDPG se behtar kyun hai?
3 fixes: delayed policy updates (critic pehle stable ho), clipped double Q-learning (do critics ka minimum lo — overestimation kam), target policy smoothing (target action me noise — Q-value peaks smooth). Book kehta hai: DDPG ke bajaye hamesha TD3 use karo.
Q19 TRPO kya hai? KL divergence kyun use hoti hai?
TRPO policy update ko constraint karta hai — nayi policy purani se KL divergence δ se zyada alag nahi honi chahiye. KL do distributions ka difference naapta hai. Problem: conjugate gradient descent chahiye — complex aur slow. Isiliye PPO popular hua.
Q20 PPO ka clipped objective kaise kaam karta hai?
L = min(r·A, clip(r, 1−ε, 1+ε)·A), jahan r = new/old policy ratio, ε ~0.1–0.3. Advantage positive ho aur ratio zyada badhe to loss clip — policy ek step me zyada improve nahi kar sakti. Ye pessimistic lower bound deta hai — worst-case performance guarantee.
Q21 SAC kya hai? Entropy kyun add karte hain?
Soft Actor-Critic — maximum entropy RL ka off-policy implementation. Objective me reward + α·entropy hota hai — agent saare tareeke seekhta hai, sirf ek nahi. Isse robust policies banti hain. Auto-temperature version me α khud seekhta hai — tuning nahi chahiye.
Q22 Temperature α ka kya asar hota hai?
α ≈ 0 matlab deterministic policy (Q-learning jaisa). α bada matlab zyada exploration. α bahut bada ho to entropy reward se badi ho jaati hai — agent goal bhool jaata hai. Annealing (0.5 → 0.005) ya auto-temperature use karo.
Q23 POMDP kya hai? MDP se kaise alag hai?
Partially Observable MDP — agent state ko poora observe nahi kar sakta. Belief state banana padta hai (past observations se). RNN/LSTM se belief track karte hain. Example: autonomous car intersection par — aas-paas ki car dikhai nahi deti.
Q24 Hierarchical RL kab use karein?
Jab agent complex ho (robot arm, text agent) aur problem skills me baanta ja sake. Low-level policies skills seekhti hain, high-level planning karti hai. DIAYN bina reward ke skills discover karta hai. Agar environment complex hai lekin agent simple hai, to HRL fayda nahi dega.
Q25 MARL kya hai? Centralized vs decentralized?
Multi-Agent RL — kayi agents ek environment me. Cooperative (common goal), competitive (zero-sum), mixed. Centralized learning me sabka experience ek learner ke paas; decentralized me har agent locally seekhta hai (scalable). Main challenge: nonstationarity — doosre agents environment badal rahe hain.
Q26 Imitation learning vs inverse RL — farak kya hai?
Imitation (behavior cloning, DQfD, GAIL, SQIL): expert ki trajectory copy karo. Inverse RL: expert ki trajectory se reward function seekho — kyunki expert optimal nahi bhi ho sakta. IRL slow hai (baar-baar RL retrain karna padta hai) lekin reward design ki problem solve karta hai.
Q27 Offline (batch) RL kya hai? Challenges kya hain?
Logged data se seekhna bina live environment ke. Sample efficient hai lekin explore nahi kar sakte — distribution shift problem: value methods unseen actions ki value galat estimate karte hain, policy gradients diverge kar sakte hain. BCQ jaise algorithms sirf data me dikhe actions tak limit karte hain.
Q28 Reward shaping kya hai? Reward hacking se kaise bachein?
Shaping = beech-beech me chhote rewards dekar agent ko guide karna (distance-to-goal, step penalty). Hacking = agent reward exploit kar le (Unity box example — wall jump karne ke bajaye side se sneak kiya). Bachne ke liye: reward ko true goal se correlate rakho, simple rakho, aur policy ko observe karo.
Q29 Curiosity-driven exploration kya hai? Noisy TV problem kya hai?
Next state predict karo — prediction error ko reward banao. Agent surprising states ki taraf jaata hai. Problem: noisy TV — agent TV dekh kar atak jaata hai kyunki wo unpredictable hai. RND (random network distillation) isse fix karta hai — static image ko model kar sakta hai.
Q30 Evaluation me regret kya hai?
Optimal reward aur actual reward ka difference over time. Solar plant example: theoretical max power vs actual power ka gap. Regret kam matlab policy optimal ke kareeb hai. Statistical comparison ke liye kam se kam 15 repeats chahiye (Colas et al.) — aur sahi test chuno (Welch's t-test aam taur par robust hai).
Q31 RL deploy karte time sabse badi galtiyan kya hain?
Policy ko baar-baar update karna (diverge ho sakti hai — jitna kam update karo utna behtar), monitoring na karna (trust toot jaata hai), train-serve code alag rakhna (divergence), data ka schema test na karna (galat data = catastrophic failure), aur safety ko end me sochna (shuru se socho).
Q32 Safe RL kaise karte hain?
Constrained optimization (TRPO/PPO me constraints), reward shaping (dangerous actions penalize), safe sets + action filters (unsafe action roko), reset controllers (safe state me wapas). Yaad rakho: ye methods training ke dauraan safety guarantee nahi karte — sirf kaafi training ke baad approximately safe hote hain.
Q33 RL kab use NAHI karna chahiye?
Jab problem single-step ho (ML behtar hai), jab rules se solve ho jaaye (tax calculation), jab data hi nahi hai aur simulation banana mushkil hai, jab galti ki keemat bahut zyada hai aur safety guarantee nahi de sakte, ya jab simple baseline hi kaafi achha hai. Hamesha pehle simple baseline try karo.
Q34 RL project shuru karne ke 2 golden rules kya hain?
Start Simple (sabse simple version se shuru karo — MDP samajh aayega, success milegi) aur Keep Development Cycles Small (pehle keystroke se working code tak ka time chhota rakho — 2 din wait karke galti pata chale to time barbaad hai).
Q35 PPO vs SAC — kaunsa chunein?
Continuous control me SAC aam taur par PPO se behtar hai (MuJoCo benchmarks — HalfCheetah, Humanoid, Ant). SAC off-policy hai (sample efficient) aur auto-temperature se tuning kam hai. PPO simple hai aur har jagah chalta hai. Apne problem par dono test karo — published numbers par blind trust mat karo.
Q36 Q-table explode kyun hoti hai? DQN isse kaise bachta hai?
States = bins^features — features badhte hi states exponential badhte hain (4 features × 10 bins = 10,000 states; 8 features × 20 bins = 25.6 billion). DQN table store nahi karta — neural network Q-values predict karta hai aur unseen states par generalize karta hai.
Q37 Discount factor γ ka kya asar hota hai?
γ = 0 matlab sirf immediate reward (myopic). γ = 1 matlab future reward bhi utna hi valuable. Aam taur par 0.9–0.99 use hota hai. γ zyada ho to agent long-term sochta hai (restock zyada karta hai); γ kam ho to short-term greedy hota hai.
