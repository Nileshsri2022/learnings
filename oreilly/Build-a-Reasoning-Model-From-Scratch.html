Preface — Reasoning model kyun, aur ye book kis liye hai

Reasoning model wo LLM hai jo jawab dene se pehle sochta hai — problem ko steps me todta hai, har step ko check karta hai, galti pakde to course correct karta hai, aur tab final jawab deta hai. Ye book aise hi ek model ko scratch se banakar samjhati hai: train karna, evaluate karna, refine karna — sab kuch haath se, code ke saath.

Book ka daava hai ki reasoning model LLM ki pehli leher ke baad ka sabse bada development hai. Ye general intelligence ki taraf sabse nazdeek ka kadam hai, aur scientific discovery me madad karne wale LLMs ki taraf bhi. Lekin book ka style "theory pehle" wala nahi hai — har chapter ek concrete coding sawaal se shuru hota hai, aur reader se expect kiya jata hai ki wo code chala kar book ke results khud reproduce kare.

Reasoning model matlab kya hai (book ki working definition)

Ek model jo final answer se pehle ek internal thinking trace generate karta hai. Trace aam generated text hi hota hai — alag module nahi. Farq ye hai ki training process un traces ko reward karta hai jo sahi jawab tak le jaayein. Isliye model seekh jata hai ki solution strategies ko search kare, test kare aur correct kare — aur mushkil problem par lambi generation se zyada mehnat kar sake.

Ek aam galatfahmi ko book saaf karti hai: reasoning koi ek naya trick nahi hai. Ye teen cheezon ka combined effect hai — test-time computation, training, aur data aur rewards ka combination.

  • Evaluation badal gaya: MATH aur AIME jaise benchmarks jo kabhi top models ko alag karte the, ab top models unhe routinely pass kar lete hain. Agla bottleneck wo problems hain jinke jawab me insaan ko dinon lag jayein.
  • Model ka use badal gaya: reasoning models zyada time lete hain aur zyada compute kharch karte hain. Har query par inhe use karna sahi nahi — inka behaviour samajhna hi ye decide karne me madad karta hai ki extra cost kab justified hai.
  • Kya banaya jaye, wo badal gaya: jahan candidate solutions ki badi jagah ho, wahan reasoning model ki search-jaisi behaviour wo solutions dhoondh sakti hai jo insaan miss kar de. Methods sirf language tak limited nahi — book ek robotic hand aur cube ka example deti hai, jahan sirf language text dekhne wala reasoning model bhi acchi planning kar paya.

Chapter 1 — Reasoning model kya hota hai

Conventional LLM seedha jawab deta hai. Reasoning model pehle sochta hai — ek lambi thinking trace generate karta hai, usme steps try karta hai, khud ko check karta hai, aur tab final jawab deta hai. Ye chapter usi farak ko kholta hai: reasoning kya hai, kaise banti hai, aur kab kaam aati hai.

1.1 Reasoning model kya hai

Working definition: ek LLM jo final answer se pehle ek extended internal thinking trace generate karta hai — intermediate text jisme wo problem ko step by step solve karta hai, alternatives try karta hai, apna kaam check karta hai aur revise karta hai. Trace koi alag module nahi hai — ye ordinary token-by-token text generation hai, aur final answer uske baad aata hai. Qwen3 me thinking part <think>…</think> tags me wrap hota hai.

Thinking trace / chain of thought us intermediate kaam ka visible record hai. Yehi trace model ko mushkil problem par zyada compute kharch karne deta hai (lambi traces) aur reasoning ko inspectable aur debuggable banata hai. Book zor dekar kehti hai: trace sahi jawab tak pahunchne ka means hai, insaani sense me "reasoning" nahi — model "samajhta" nahi hai, usne ek behaviour seekha hai jo reliably sahi jawab deta hai.

Key concepts — ek saath samjho

  • Verifiable reward: RL training me wo reward jo answer se objectively compute ho sake — jaise extracted final answer known correct answer se match karta hai — insaan ke subjective judgement se nahi. Isi se reasoning models ki RL training scalable banti hai: reasoning ki human labelling nahi chahiye.
  • RLHF vs RLVR: RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback) human preferences ko reward banata hai — model ko helpful aur safe align karne ke liye. RLVR (reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards) objectively checkable rewards use karta hai — math answers, code ke tests pass hona — aur yehi reasoning ki training hai. Chapter 6 ki GRPO training RLVR hai.
  • SFT (supervised fine-tuning): input–output examples par training — jaise teacher model ki reasoning traces ko imitate karna (Chapter 8 distillation me use hota hai). SFT reasoning ka format aur style sikhata hai; RL model ko wo reasoning discover karna sikhata hai jo asal me kaam kare.
  • Inference-time scaling / test-time compute: bada model train karne ke bajaye, generate karte waqt zyada compute kharch karke behtar jawab paana — zyada tokens, zyada samples, refinement. Chapters 4–5 ka subject, aur reasoning models ki defining practical property: zyada sochne ka time aam taur par behtar jawab deta hai.
  • Distillation: bade teacher model ki capability ko chhote student model me transfer karna, teacher ke outputs (reasoning traces) par student ko train karke — Chapter 8.
  • System 1 / System 2 analogy: ek mashhoor psychology framework se liya gaya comparison — conventional LLMs tez, intuitive System 1 jaise behave karte hain (turant jawab); reasoning models ek dheema, deliberate System 2 mode add karte hain (problem ko solve karke). Book saaf kehti hai ye sirf ek analogy hai, human cognition ke baare me daava nahi.

1.2–1.4 Reasoning model kaise banta hai, aur reasoning emerge kaise hoti hai

Teen ingredients: (1) ek pretrained base model, (2) reasoning data par SFT, (3) verifiable rewards ke saath reinforcement learning. RL loop aise chalta hai: model kai candidate traces sample karta hai → verifiable reward unhe score karta hai → policy un traces ki taraf update hoti hai jo kaam kar gayi. Ye cycle baar-baar chalti hai, aur har cycle me reasoning lambi aur zyada reliable hoti jati hai.

"Aha moment" behaviour: training ke dauraan model khud seekh jata hai ki wo double-check kare aur backtrack kare — jaise trace ke beech me error notice karke "ruko, dobara check karta hoon" kehna aur khud ko correct karna. Ye behaviour program nahi kiya gaya — RL objective se emerge hota hai.

1.5 Conventional LLM se farak — numbers ke saath

Book ka running diagnostic problem, Chapters 1–5 me use hota hai: "Ek student ke paas $100 hain. Wo $10 ki book, $5 ka snack aur $2 ka pen khareedta hai. Uske paas kitne paise bache?" Sahi reasoning: 100 − 10 − 5 − 2 = 83. Base Qwen3-0.6B model greedy decoding se jawab deta hai $20 (galat) — wo subtraction me confuse ho jata hai. Reasoning model steps me subtract karke jawab deta hai $83 (sahi). Ye ek contrast (20 vs 83) poori book me reasoning ke value ka sabse simple demonstration hai.

1.6 Kaun fayda uthata hai

Use cases ka map: math, coding, science, planning, agents — jahan galat jawab mehenga pade aur jahan jawab ko check karna use dhoondhne se aasan ho, wahan reasoning model ki value sabse zyada hai. Jahan jawab seedha aur sasta chahiye, wahan conventional model hi kaafi hai.

Tokenizer aur special tokens

Text model tak pahunchne se pehle token IDs me tootta hai. Model sirf IDs ke saath kaam karta hai, tokenizer wapas text me convert karta hai. Special tokens: <|endoftext|> (ID 151643 — end-of-text / stop token), <|im_start|> aur <|im_end|> (chat delimiters), aur <think> / </think> (reasoning delimiters).

2.3–2.5 Autoregressive generation kaise kaam karti hai

Causal / autoregressive generation: model pichhle saare tokens se agla token predict karta hai, ek baar me ek token. Generation ek loop hai: forward pass → logits → token chuno → append karo → repeat. Har step pichhle token par depend karta hai, isliye generation inherently sequential hai — parallel nahi ho sakti.

Logits aur softmax: model ka raw output har position par logits ka vector hota hai (vocabulary ke har token ke liye ek — 151,936 values); softmax logits ko probabilities me badalta hai. Greedy decoding sabse zyada probability wala token chunta hai (argmax); sampling distribution se draw karta hai.

Model loading: weights Hugging Face (Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B) se book ki Qwen3Model class se load hote hain (torch_dtype=torch.bfloat16), aur generation ke liye model evaluation mode me rakha jata hai. Source code Appendix C me hai.

Worked examples: prompt "The capital of France is" — greedy generation (argmax har step par) Paris naam wala continuation deta hai; wahi prompt reasoning model se thinking trace ke saath jawab deta hai. $100 problem generate_text_simple se base model ke saath galat $20 deta hai, reasoning model ke saath sahi $83.

2.6 KV cache — sabse bada generation speedup

Generation ke dauraan, pehle ke tokens ke liye compute kiye gaye keys aur values har attention layer me cache karke reuse kiye ja sakte hain — har naya token sirf cached keys/values ke saath apna attention compute karta hai. Bina cache ke har step poore sequence par attention dobara compute karta hai; cache ke saath generation bahut tez hoti hai.

Python · generate_text_simple — core idea (notes se reconstruct, teaching excerpt)
def generate_text_simple(model, tokenizer, prompt, max_new_tokens=50):
    ids = tokenizer.encode(prompt)
    for _ in range(max_new_tokens):
        logits = model(ids)              # last position ke logits lo
        next_id = torch.argmax(logits[-1])
        ids.append(next_id)
        if next_id == 151643:            # <|endoftext|> — stop token
            break
    return tokenizer.decode(ids)

Chapter 3 — Reasoning models ko evaluate karna

Ek accuracy number bina uski conditions ke kuch nahi kehta — prompt kya tha, decoding kaisi thi, sample size kitna tha, hardware kya tha, answer extract sahi hua ya nahi. Ye chapter ek reproducible evaluation pipeline banata hai aur MATH-500 par base aur reasoning model ke beech ka gap naapta hai — wo gap jiske against baad ke har chapter ka method measure hoga.

3.1 Evaluation mushkil kyun hai

Evaluation ek fixed set of problems hoti hai jinke correct answers pata hote hain, fixed conditions me model se chalayi jati hai, aur ek accuracy number deti hai. Book zor deti hai: accuracy number meaningless hai bina un conditions ke jinhone use produce kiya — prompt, decoding, sample size, hardware, answer extraction sahi hua ya nahi.

3.2–3.3 Mini-eval aur model chunna

Verifiable answer / answer extraction: math problems ke liye model se kaha jata hai ki final answer \boxed{…} expression me daale; evaluator regex se \boxed{} ke contents extract karke (normalize karke, numerically) reference answer se compare karta hai. Ye wahi "verifiable" idea hai jo RLVR me hai, measurement par apply kiya gaya.

Mini-eval / tiny custom benchmark: haath se banaya gaya chhota test set (book ka 10 arithmetic/word problems ka hai) jo bade benchmark par time kharch karne se pehle evaluation pipeline ko debug karne ke liye use hota hai. Ye extraction bugs aur prompt problems ko saste me pakad leta hai.

MATH-500: 500 competition-style mathematics problems ka standard benchmark (MATH dataset ka subset), har ek ka reference answer ke saath. Book isko Chapters 3–8 ke liye main yardstick banati hai — isliye book ke saare accuracy numbers comparable MATH-500 accuracies hain, jab tak kuch aur na kaha gaya ho.

Greedy vs sampled evaluation: greedy decoding (temperature 0) har problem ka ek deterministic answer deti hai; sampled evaluation (temperature > 0, jaise 0.9) ek distribution deti hai, aur accuracy phir average / pass@k-style results ke roop me report hoti hai. Book headline numbers ke liye greedy evaluation use karti hai, siwaay jahan sampling hi test kiya ja raha method ho.

Worked examples — numbers

  • Mini-eval: 10 hand-written problems. Worked example: ek problem jiska sahi answer 1/3 hai — model 2/3 predict karta hai, to extraction-and-compare step use False mark karta hai. Mini-eval base aur reasoning model dono par CPU par chalaya jata hai, MATH-500 se pehle pipeline validate karne ke liye.
  • $100 problem as evaluation case: base model \boxed{20} (galat), reasoning model \boxed{83} (sahi) predict karta hai — ab wahi jawab automatic verifier se score hote hain.

Evaluation cost generation se dominate hoti hai (lambi reasoning traces slow hoti hain). 0.6B model memory me aasani se fit hota hai, lekin CPU par 500 lambi traces generate karne me hours lagte hain, GPU par minutes se kuch hours, trace length par depend karke.

Chapter 4 — Inference-time scaling se reasoning improve karna

Weights badle bina, generate karte waqt zyada compute kharch karke behtar jawab paana — yehi inference-time scaling hai. Chapter ka central claim, empirically dikhaya gaya: base model ke liye inference-time scaling reasoning model ke advantage ka bada hissa recover kar sakti hai. Sabse bada single gain CoT prompting se aata hai.

4.1 Inference-time scaling ka intuition

Generation time par zyada compute kharch karo — lambi generation, kai candidate answers, ya unme voting — to behtar answer mile. Koi weight change nahi hota. Accuracy vs compute curve badhti hai, lekin cost bhi badhti hai — yehi tradeoff poora chapter naapta hai.

4.2 Temperature — distribution ko sharp ya flat karna

Temperature softmax se pehle logits par apply hone wala divisor hai. Temperature < 1 distribution ko sharp karta hai (zyada confident, zyada deterministic); temperature > 1 use flat karta hai (zyada random, zyada diverse). Temperature 0 greedy decoding ki limit hai. Book ke experiments me moderate temperature (lagbhag 0.9) useful diversity deta hai bina incoherence ke, jabki bahut high temperature (5) near-gibberish produce karta hai.

Worked example — "Berlin" token: ek real prompt par "Berlin" token (token ID 19846) ki probability temperature 5 par 0.0003 hai, temperature 0.5 par 33.98%, aur temperature 0.35 par lagbhag 42% (1,000 samples me 435 baar). Temperature 5, seed 123 ke saath multinomial sample token 65094 ("mistress") return karta hai — bahut high temperature ki incoherence ka example.

4.3 Top-p (nucleus) sampling

Tokens ko probability ke hisaab se sort karo, sabse chhota set rakho jinki cumulative probability p (jaise 0.9) tak pahunche, baaki ko discard karo, aur rakhe hue probabilities ko renormalise karo. Ye candidate set ko model ke confidence ke hisaab se adapt karta hai: jab model confident hota hai to sirf ek-do tokens bachte hain; jab uncertain hota hai to kai bachte hain. Top-k (k sabse likely tokens rakhna) iska simpler fixed-size alternative hai.

Worked example: p = 0.8 par toy distribution me kept set simple case me 2 tokens hota hai (variant me 3), renormalised probabilities jaise 0.5589 (token index 4), 0.1021 (index 8) aur 0.3390 (index 9) — numbers renormalisation ke baad 1 tak sum hote hain, jaise hone chahiye.

4.4 Temperature + top-p combine karna

Dono ek saath use hote hain: pehle temperature se distribution shape karo, phir top-p se tail cut karo, phir sample karo. Book ka generation loop (Chapter 2 ka) isi tarah extend hota hai — argmax step ko sampling functions se replace karke.

4.5 Majority voting (self-consistency)

n independent answers sample karo (temperature/top-p ke saath), har ek ka boxed answer extract karo, aur sabse frequent answer ko final answer banao. Intuition: sahi reasoning path tak kai independent samples ke pahunchne ki probability kisi ek galat path se zyada hoti hai, isliye majority ek single sample se zyada reliable hoti hai. Cost: ek sample ke generation time ka n×.

Chapter 5 — Self-refinement se inference-time scaling

Greedy decoding ki sabse badi kami: wo ek baar jo token chun le, use wapas nahi dekhta. Ek locally plausible token poore jawab ko galat raaste par le ja sakta hai, aur model recover nahi kar sakta. Ye chapter ek loop banata hai — generate karo, critique karo, revise karo, score karo, aur sirf tabhi revision rakho jab score behtar ho.

5.1 Greedy generation ki kami

Har step par greedy decoding sabse likely token chunti hai aur peeche mud kar nahi dekhti. Ek locally plausible token poore answer ko bhatka sakta hai, aur model ke paas pehle ki choice par dobara vichaar karne ka koi mechanism nahi hota — isliye ek single greedy trace confidently galat ho sakta hai.

5.2 Heuristic score — response ki quality naapna bina sahi jawab jaane

  • Token log-probability: har generated token ke liye, us token ki log probability jo model ne preceding text ke basis par assign ki thi. Confident token ki log-probability 0 ke paas hoti hai; surprising token ki badi negative hoti hai.
  • Joint probability / sequence log-probability: poore response ke per-token probabilities ka product (log-probabilities ka sum). Ye "model ko ye poora answer kitna likely laga" ka natural score hai, lekin ye lambi responses ko penalise karta hai (zyada tokens multiply hote hain), isliye isko normalize karna padta hai.
  • Average log-probability (length-normalised score): log-probabilities ka sum, tokens ki sankhya se divide karke. Ye book ka chuna hua heuristic hai: ye model ki per-token average confidence measure karta hai aur chhote answers ko systematically favour nahi karta. Zyada (kam negative) average log-probability matlab zyada confident, aur empirically behtar, response.

Chapter 6 — Reinforcement learning se reasoning model train karna (GRPO)

SFT reasoning ka format sikhata hai; RL reasoning ko discover karna sikhata hai. Ye chapter Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) se base model ko reasoning model me badalta hai — sirf 50 steps me, ek simple verifiable reward ke saath: MATH-500 accuracy 15.2% se 47.4% tak, aur model khud lambi thinking traces generate karna seekh jata hai.

6.1 RL for LLMs — basic terms

  • Policy: LLM khud (next tokens par ek probability distribution).
  • Trajectory: ek complete generated response (thinking trace + answer).
  • Reward: completed output ke liye ek scalar number — yahan, boxed answer sahi hai ya nahi, usse compute hota hai.
  • SFT ke opposite: imitate karne ke liye koi target outputs nahi hote — model ko sampling se acche outputs discover karne hote hain.

6.2 GRPO — Group Relative Policy Optimization

Book me use hua RL algorithm (DeepSeek-R1 wale kaam se). Har prompt ke liye current policy G completions ka group sample karti hai; har completion ko reward milta hai; ek completion ka advantage uske reward ka group ke mean aur standard deviation ke relative value hota hai — reward minus group mean, group standard deviation se divide karke. Group average se behtar completions ko positive advantage milta hai (unke tokens reinforce hote hain); kharab ko negative advantage (unke tokens discourage hote hain).

GRPO ka main simplification PPO se: alag value/critic network nahi chahiye — group khud baseline provide karta hai.

Worked example — advantage: 4 completions ka group, rewards [1, 1, 0, 0] — mean 0.5, standard deviation 0.5, to do sahi completions ka advantage +1.0 aur do galat ka −1.0. Agar group ke saare rewards identical hain (sab sahi ya sab galat), to standard deviation 0 hoti hai aur advantage 0 — group kuch nahi sikhata, isliye group diversity matter karti hai.

Advantage formula: advantage = (reward − group ke rewards ka mean) / (group rewards ka standard deviation + chhota epsilon).

KL divergence aur clipping — training ko stable rakhna

  • KL penalty: ek term jo policy ko reference model (original base model) se zyada door drift karne par penalise karta hai. Coefficient β (beta) se control hota hai. Bada β model ko base ke paas rakhta hai (stable lekin slow learning); chhota β zyada freely change karne deta hai (tez learning, degenerate output ka risk). Book ke main GRPO run me β = 0.04 hai (Chapter 7 me β = 0 bhi test kiya gaya hai).
  • Clipping (PPO-style): nayi policy ki token probability aur purani policy ki token probability ka ratio [1−ε, 1+ε] range me clip kiya jata hai (ε = 0.2), taaki ek update policy ko zyada na badal sake. Ye PPO se inherited stabilising mechanism hai.

Verifiable reward aur training data

Book ka reward: extracted boxed answer reference se match kare to +1, warna 0, plus well-formed boxed answer ke liye chhota format reward. Reward deliberately simple aur objective hai; book zor deti hai ki model reasoning isliye seekhta hai kyunki sirf wahi traces reward paati hain jo sahi boxed answer par end hoti hain.

Training data: MATH training set se sample kiye gaye 2,000 math problems (MATH-500 se alag), GRPO prompts ke roop me. Har training step prompts ka batch sample karta hai, har prompt ke liye completions ka group generate karta hai, rewards compute karta hai aur policy update karta hai.

Chapter 7 — GRPO ko improve karna

GRPO kaam karta hai — lekin kaise aur behtar kiya jaye? Ye chapter chaar orthogonal levers test karta hai: reward design, group size, KL aur learning rate jaise hyperparameters, aur LoRA se efficient training — plus Dr. GRPO variant. Best standard-GRPO run 48.6% (G=16) tak pahunchta hai, reference reasoning model ke 48.2% ko essentially match karte hue.

7.1 Reward design aur reward hacking

Reward function kya reward karta hai, yehi decide karta hai ki model kya seekhega. Book teen schemes compare karti hai: (a) pure outcome reward (sirf sahi final answer ke liye +1), (b) outcome + format reward (well-formed boxed answer aur thinking trace ke liye chhota bonus), (c) length-aware reward (unnecessarily lambi traces ko penalise karna).

Lesson: format bonus early training me madad karta hai (model ko expected output shape sikhata hai, taaki outcome reward earn hi ho sake), lekin badly designed reward ko "hack" kiya ja sakta hai — jaise sirf length reward karne se model rambling seekh jata hai. Reward hacking matlab: model literal reward ko uske intent ko defeat karte hue optimise karta hai — jaise format bonus collect karne ke liye correctly formatted lekin galat answer dena, ya length reward ke liye trace pad karna. Remedy: outcome reward ko dominant rakho aur auxiliary rewards ko chhota rakho.

7.2 Group size (G)

Group size matlab GRPO me har prompt ke liye kitne completions sample kiye jayein. Bada group zyada reliable group mean/standard deviation deta hai (behtar baseline) aur zyada diverse traces, lekin har step me zyada generation time lagta hai. Book G = 4, 8 aur 16 test karti hai: G=4 → 45.6%, G=8 → 47.4%, G=16 → 48.6% (best, average response length lagbhag 612 tokens). Bade groups in runs me monotonically madad karte hain, zyada generation per step ki keemat par.

7.3 GRPO hyperparameters — KL aur learning rate

  • KL coefficient β: β = 0 penalty ko poori tarah hata deta hai (model freely drift kar sakta hai); β = 0.04 Chapter 6 ka default hai; bada β model ko zyada constrain karta hai. Results: β = 0 → 48.0% (β = 0.04 wale run ke 47.4% se thoda upar); β = 0.1 → accuracy kam, model over-constrained. Book β = 0 ko short runs ke liye viable maanti hai, lekin lambi training ke liye chhota KL term rakhne ki recommend karti hai — bina KL anchor ke model lambi runs me degenerate output ki taraf drift kar sakta hai.
  • Learning rate aur clipping ε: optimizer ka step size (Chapter 6 me 1e-6; chapter 5e-7 aur 2e-6 test karta hai) aur PPO clip range (0.2). Bada learning rate tez seekhta hai lekin kam stable hota hai; tests dikhate hain 1e-6 0.6B model ke liye accha middle ground hai — 5e-7 slow seekhta hai aur 50 steps me kam final accuracy deta hai, 2e-6 early gains tez deta hai lekin noisier curve aur thoda kam final score.

7.4 RL training with LoRA

LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation): saare 0.6B weights update karne ke bajaye, base model ko freeze karo aur sirf chhote low-rank matrices train karo jo attention (aur feed-forward) weights me add hote hain. Rank r = 16 aur scaling α = 32 ke saath trainable parameters model ka chhota fraction reh jate hain, training tez hoti hai aur bahut kam memory use hoti hai, aur adapted model ko baad me base weights me merge kiya ja sakta hai. Book LoRA ko bade models par ya chhote hardware par GRPO chalane ka practical tareeka batati hai.

LoRA result: rank 16, α 32, 50 steps train karke MATH-500 accuracy mid-40s% (full fine-tuning ke 47.4% ke paas), bahut kam trainable parameters aur kam memory use ke saath.

7.5 Comparing GRPO variants — Dr. GRPO

Dr. GRPO: GRPO ka ek variant (literature se) jo advantage me standard-deviation normalisation hata deta hai (advantage = reward − group mean, std se divide kiye bina) aur loss me length normalisation hata deta hai — is ground par ki wo normalisations biases introduce karti hain (wo chhote completions ko favour karti hain aur noise ko amplify kar sakti hain). Book ise implement karke standard GRPO se compare karti hai: is scale par Dr. GRPO standard GRPO ke comparable score karta hai (high-40s% ke aas-paas), kuch lambi completions ke saath — book ka conclusion hai ki ye ek reasonable alternative hai, clear win nahi.

Chapter 8 — Reasoning models ko distil karna

RL reasoning create karta hai; distillation use failata hai — chhote aur saste models me. 8.1 Distillation kya hai

Knowledge distillation: ek chhote "student" model ko bade "teacher" model ke behaviour reproduce karne ke liye train karna, teacher ke outputs par train karke, human-written data par nahi. Student teacher ki reasoning style aur, kuch had tak, uski capability seekh leta hai — teacher ke size aur cost ke fraction par.

Teacher aur student yahan: teacher reasoning model hai (GRPO-trained Qwen3-0.6B reasoning model / reference Qwen3-0.6B reasoning model) aur student base Qwen3-0.6B model hai. Book khud acknowledge karti hai ki main experiment me size same hai (0.6B → 0.6B): experiment ka point ye dikhana hai ki reasoning traces par SFT se transfer hoti hai, aur wahi method genuinely chhote student par apply hota hai (jaise 32B teacher ko 0.6B student me distil karna, text aur Appendix D me discuss kiya gaya hai).

8.2 Teacher traces aur rejection sampling

Teacher traces: teacher ke problems ke liye poore outputs — thinking trace plus final answer. Distillation dataset me sirf wahi traces rakhi jati hain jo sahi answer par end hoti hain (rejection sampling / verifiable reward se filtering). Ye key quality control hai: student ko sirf wo reasoning imitate karni chahiye jo kaam kar gayi.

Rejection sampling: har problem ke liye kai teacher traces generate karna aur sirf sahi wali rakhna. Ye wahi idea hai jo self-consistency (Chapter 4) me tha, data-collection method me badla hua.

8.3 SFT on traces

Supervised fine-tuning jisme training example (problem prompt → teacher ki poori trace + answer) hota hai, aur loss trace aur answer tokens par usual next-token cross-entropy hota hai (prompt tokens loss se mask out hote hain, −100 se). Student teacher ke style me traces produce karna seekh leta hai.

Distillation vs RL: distillation RL se sasta aur zyada stable hai (acche examples par ordinary SFT hai), lekin ye sirf wo reasoning copy kar sakta hai jo teacher pehle se produce karta hai — RL ki tarah nayi reasoning discover nahi kar sakta. Book dono ko complementary batati hai: RL reasoning create karta hai, distillation use chhote/saste models me failata hai.

F1End Matter · Interview FAQs

Interview FAQs

AI agent interviews (current era) ke liye — foundations se lekar production tak, 36 sawaal-jawaab. Har jawaab isi book ke concepts par aadharit hai.

Q01Reasoning model kya hota hai — conventional LLM se ye kaise alag hai?
Reasoning model wo LLM hai jo final answer se pehle ek extended thinking trace generate karta hai — problem ko steps me solve karta hai, alternatives try karta hai, khud ko check karta hai aur revise karta hai. Conventional LLM seedha jawab deta hai. Trace ordinary token-by-token generation hi hoti hai, lekin training (SFT + verifiable rewards wali RL) use shape karti hai. Qwen3 me thinking part <think>…</think> tags me wrap hota hai. Interview me $100 problem ka example do: base model $20 (galat), reasoning model $83 (sahi).
Q02Thinking trace kya hoti hai aur ye kyun matter karti hai?
Thinking trace reasoning model ke intermediate kaam ka visible record hai — steps, checks, backtracking, sab text me. Ye teen kaam karta hai: model ko mushkil problem par zyada compute kharch karne deta hai (lambi traces), reasoning ko inspectable aur debuggable banata hai, aur galti pakdi ja sake to correction possible karta hai. Book zor deti hai: trace sahi jawab tak pahunchne ka means hai, insaani sense me samajhna nahi — model ne ek behaviour seekha hai jo reliably sahi jawab deta hai.
Q03Verifiable reward kya hai aur ye RL training ko scalable kyun banata hai?
Verifiable reward wo reward hai jo answer se objectively compute ho sake — jaise extracted final answer known correct answer se match karta hai — insaan ke subjective judgement se nahi. Isse RL training scalable banti hai kyunki reasoning ki human labelling nahi chahiye: reward function khud check kar leta hai ki jawab sahi hai ya nahi. Book ka GRPO reward +1 deta hai sahi boxed answer par, 0 galat par, plus chhota format bonus.
Q04RLHF vs RLVR me kya farak hai?
RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback) human preferences ko reward banata hai — model ko helpful aur safe align karne ke liye. RLVR (reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards) objectively checkable rewards use karta hai — math answers, code ke tests pass hona — aur yehi reasoning ki training hai. Book ki Chapter 6 GRPO training RLVR hai. Interview point: RLHF alignment ke liye, RLVR capability (reasoning) ke liye.
Q05SFT vs RL — reasoning seekhne me dono ka kya role hai?
SFT (supervised fine-tuning) input-output examples par training hai — jaise teacher ki reasoning traces imitate karna. Ye reasoning ka format aur style sikhata hai. RL (jaise GRPO) me koi target outputs nahi hote — model sampling se acche outputs discover karta hai, reward ke basis par. Book ka message: SFT format sikhata hai, RL wo reasoning discover karna sikhata hai jo asal me kaam kare. Dono saath use hote hain — pehle SFT, phir RL, phir distillation.
Q06Inference-time scaling kya hai aur ye kyun matter karta hai?
Inference-time scaling matlab: bada model train karne ke bajaye, generate karte waqt zyada compute kharch karke behtar jawab paana — lambi generation, kai samples, ya voting. Ye reasoning models ki defining practical property hai: zyada sochne ka time aam taur par behtar jawab deta hai. Book dikhati hai ki base model inference-time scaling se 15.2% se 52.0% tak pahunch jata hai MATH-500 par — lekin keemat badi hai: best combination me 862.6 minutes GPU time lagte hain.
Q07System 1 / System 2 analogy ka matlab kya hai?
Ye ek mashhoor psychology framework se liya gaya comparison hai: conventional LLMs tez, intuitive System 1 jaise behave karte hain (turant jawab), reasoning models ek dheema, deliberate System 2 mode add karte hain (problem ko solve karke). Book saaf kehti hai ye sirf ek analogy hai, human cognition ke baare me daava nahi. Interview me ye bolna zaroori hai — analogy ko literal claim mat banao.
Q08Qwen3-0.6B ka architecture kya hai — key numbers batao.
Qwen3-0.6B ke numbers: lagbhag 0.6 billion parameters, hidden size 1,024, 28 transformer layers, grouped-query attention (16 query heads, 2 key-value heads), RMSNorm, rotary position embeddings (RoPE), SwiGLU activation feed-forward me. Tokenizer byte-pair encoding hai, vocabulary 151,936 tokens. Special tokens: <|endoftext|> ID 151643 (stop token), <|im_start|> / <|im_end|> (chat delimiters), <think> / </think> (reasoning delimiters).
Q09Tokenizer kya karta hai aur special tokens kyun matter karte hain?
Tokenizer text ko token IDs me todta hai — model sirf IDs ke saath kaam karta hai, tokenizer wapas text me convert karta hai. Special tokens control signals hote hain: <|endoftext|> (ID 151643) generation rokne ka stop token hai, <|im_start|> / <|im_end|> chat messages ko delimit karte hain, <think> / </think> reasoning trace ko wrap karte hain. Generation loop me stop check 151643 ke against hota hai — yehi wajah hai ki ye ID yaad rakhna interview me useful hai.
Q10KV cache kya hai aur ye kitna speedup deta hai?
KV cache generation ke dauraan pehle ke tokens ke keys aur values ko cache karke reuse karta hai — har naya token sirf cached keys/values ke saath apna attention compute karta hai. Bina cache ke har step poore sequence ka attention dobara compute karta hai. Table 2.1: CPU par 1.15 se 28 tokens/sec (lagbhag 25×), GPU par 8.36 se 30.28 tokens/sec (lagbhag 3.6×). Book kahta hai KV cache sabse bada generation speedup hai, aur CPU par iska asar sabse zyada hai.
Q11Chat template kyun zaroori hai — raw prompt se kya farak padta hai?
Chat template messages ki list (system/user/assistant) ko exact text format me badalta hai jis par model train hua tha — <|im_start|> / <|im_end|> markers aur reasoning models ke liye <think> block insert karke. Model ko wahi format expect hota hai. Chapter 3 dikhata hai ki template hatane se base model +50% (relative) boost pata hai lekin reasoning model −40% gir jata hai — kyunki reasoning model ko template expect karne ki training mili thi. Isliye evaluation me prompt format fixed rakhna chahiye.
Q12Greedy decoding vs sampling — kab kaun use karein?
Greedy decoding har step par sabse zyada probability wala token chunta hai (argmax) — deterministic, ek hi answer. Sampling distribution se draw karta hai — temperature aur top-p se control hota hai, diverse answers milte hain. Greedy headline evaluation ke liye use hota hai (reproducible numbers); sampling self-consistency aur exploration ke liye. Temperature 0 greedy ki limit hai; temperature 0.9 book ke experiments me useful diversity deta hai; temperature 5 near-gibberish deta hai.
Q13MATH-500 kya hai aur ye book ka main benchmark kyun hai?
MATH-500 500 competition-style mathematics problems ka standard benchmark hai (MATH dataset ka subset), har ek ka reference answer ke saath. Book isko Chapters 3–8 ke liye main yardstick banati hai — isliye book ke saare accuracy numbers comparable MATH-500 accuracies hain. Base model 15.3%, reasoning model 50.8% (greedy, full 500, CUDA). Har method — CoT, self-consistency, GRPO, distillation — isi par naapa jata hai.
Q14Boxed answer extraction kaise kaam karti hai?
Math problems ke liye model se kaha jata hai ki final answer \boxed{…} expression me daale. Evaluator regex se \boxed{} ke contents extract karke (nested braces handle karke), normalize karke (whitespace hatana, fractions/decimals handle karna) reference answer se compare karta hai aur Boolean return karta hai. Yehi verifiable evaluation hai — Chapter 3 ka extract_boxed_answer aur is_correct functions yehi karte hain.
Q15Mini-eval kyun banate hain — seedha MATH-500 kyun nahi chalate?
Mini-eval haath se banaya gaya chhota test set hai (book ka 10 arithmetic/word problems ka) jo bade benchmark par time kharch karne se pehle evaluation pipeline ko debug karne ke liye use hota hai. Ye extraction bugs aur prompt problems ko saste me pakad leta hai. Book ka worked example: sahi answer 1/3, model 2/3 predict karta hai — extraction-and-compare step use False mark karta hai. Pehle mini-eval CPU par chalao, phir MATH-500.
Q16Base 15.3% vs reasoning 50.8% — is gap ka matlab kya hai?
Ye gap MATH-500 par greedy evaluation ka hai (full 500 problems, CUDA): base Qwen3-0.6B 15.3%, reasoning model 50.8% — lagbhag 3×. Ye gap baad ke har chapter ka target hai: CoT + sampling + voting base ko 52.0% tak le jata hai, GRPO 47.4%–48.6% tak, distillation mid-40s% tak. Gap ka matlab ye nahi ki base model bekaar hai — ye dikhata hai ki reasoning (trace + training) kitna value add karta hai.
Q17Prompt format se accuracy itni kyun badalti hai?
Chapter 3 Section 3.8 quantify karta hai: chat template/system prompt hatane se base model lagbhag +50% (relative) boost pata hai lekin reasoning model lagbhag −40% gir jata hai; "Problem:" prefix se base +20%, reasoning −30%. Reason: reasoning model ko apne chat template ki expect karne ki training mili thi, isliye template hatane se use nuksaan hota hai; base model, jo template par kabhi train hi nahi hua, template se hinder hota hai. Lesson: methods compare karte waqt prompt format fixed rakho.
Q18Temperature kya karta hai — Berlin token example se samjhao.
Temperature softmax se pehle logits par apply hone wala divisor hai: < 1 distribution ko sharp karta hai (zyada deterministic), > 1 flat karta hai (zyada random). Book ka example: "Berlin" token ki probability temperature 5 par 0.0003, temperature 0.5 par 33.98%, aur temperature 0.35 par lagbhag 42% (1,000 samples me 435 baar). Temperature 5, seed 123 par multinomial sample token 65094 ("mistress") deta hai — bahut high temperature ki incoherence. Moderate temperature (lagbhag 0.9) useful diversity deta hai.
Q19Top-p (nucleus) sampling kaise kaam karta hai?
Top-p me tokens ko probability ke hisaab se sort karo, sabse chhota set rakho jinki cumulative probability p (jaise 0.9) tak pahunche, baaki ko discard karo, aur rakhe hue probabilities ko renormalise karo. Ye candidate set ko model ke confidence ke hisaab se adapt karta hai: confident hone par sirf ek-do tokens bachte hain, uncertain hone par kai. Book ka example: p = 0.8 par kept set 2 tokens (variant me 3), renormalised probabilities jaise 0.5589, 0.1021, 0.3390 — sum 1 hota hai. Top-k iska simpler fixed-size alternative hai.
Q20Self-consistency / majority voting kya hai aur ye kab kaam karta hai?
Self-consistency me n independent answers sample karo (temperature/top-p ke saath), har ek ka boxed answer extract karo, aur sabse frequent answer ko final banao. Intuition: sahi reasoning path tak kai independent samples ke pahunchne ki probability kisi ek galat path se zyada hoti hai. $100 problem par 5 samples (83, 22, 54, 83, 61) ka majority 83 (sahi) hota hai. Cost ek sample ka n× hoti hai. Ye tab kaam karta hai jab model ka modal answer aksar sahi ho.
Q21CoT prompting sabse bada single gain kyun deta hai?
Chain-of-thought prompting me prompt me instruction add hoti hai ki model step-by-step reason kare aur phir boxed final answer de. Base model ke liye ye sabse bada single gain hai: MATH-500 par greedy 15.2% se CoT 40.6% (+25.4 points). Reason: CoT ek aise model se thinking trace elicit karta hai jo kabhi reasoning produce karne ki training hi nahi paya — trace ke bina model seedha (aur aksar galat) jawab deta hai. Sampling alone bina CoT ke sirf 17.8% deta hai — isliye combination synergistic hai.
Q22Pass@k vs majority vote me kya farak hai?
Pass@k matlab "k samples me se kam se kam ek sahi ho" — ye voting kya achieve kar sakta hai uska upper bound hai. Majority-vote accuracy wo hai jo asal me milti hai jab sabse frequent answer chuna jaye. Book report kiye gaye numbers majority-vote accuracies hain, pass@k nahi. Interview me ye distinction dikhana important hai — pass@k hamesha majority vote se zyada ya barabar hota hai, lekin wo achievable accuracy nahi batata.
Q23Greedy decoding ki sabse badi kami kya hai?
Greedy decoding har step par sabse likely token chunti hai aur peeche mud kar nahi dekhti. Ek locally plausible token poore answer ko galat raaste par le ja sakta hai, aur model ke paas pehle ki choice par dobara vichaar karne ka koi mechanism nahi hota — isliye ek single greedy trace confidently galat ho sakta hai. Chapter 5 ka solution self-refinement loop hai: generate karo, critique karo, revise karo, score karo, aur sirf behtar revision rakho.
Q24Average log-probability heuristic kya hai — Berlin vs Bridge example?
Har token ki log-probability model ki us token par confidence batati hai. Joint probability poore response ki probabilities ka product hai — ye lambi responses ko penalise karta hai. Average log-probability (sum / token count) length-normalised score hai: Berlin completion −0.2041, Bridge completion −3.8906 — wide margin se alag. Joint probabilities: Berlin 5.9372e-08 vs Bridge 1.0481e-13 (Berlin lagbhag 500,000× zyada probable). Yehi heuristic self-refinement loop me keep/discard rule ke liye use hota hai.
Q25Self-refinement loop kaise kaam karta hai aur keep-if-better rule kyun zaroori hai?
Loop: (1) initial response generate karo, (2) model se uski critique karwao, (3) critique ke hisaab se revised response banao, (4) average log-probability se score karo, (5) revision sirf tab rakho jab score behtar ho, (6) fixed iterations tak repeat karo. Keep-if-better rule essential hai kyunki refinement answer ko kharab bhi kar sakta hai — $100 problem par doosri iteration jawab ko bigaad deti hai, aur loop sahi tareeke se pehla behtar version retain karta hai.
Q26Self-refinement self-consistency se kamzor kyun hai (42.0% vs 52.0%)?
MATH-500 par self-refinement 3 iterations me base ko 40.6% se 42.0% tak le jata hai, jabki self-consistency n=10 + top-p + CoT 52.0% tak pahunchta hai. Reason: refinement ek single answer ko in place improve karta hai, jabki voting kai independent samples ki diversity ko use karta hai. Refinement ka value tab hai jab aap kai answers sample nahi kar sakte — aur heuristic score ek principled keep/discard rule deta hai. Dono complementary hain, competing nahi.
Q27GRPO kya hai aur ye PPO se kaise alag hai?
GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) DeepSeek-R1 wala RL algorithm hai. Har prompt ke liye policy G completions ka group sample karti hai, har ek ko reward milta hai, aur advantage group ke mean aur standard deviation ke relative compute hota hai. PPO se main farak: GRPO me alag value/critic network nahi chahiye — group khud baseline provide karta hai, jo iska main simplification hai. Clipping (ε = 0.2) aur KL penalty PPO se inherited stabilising mechanisms hain.
Q28Group-relative advantage kaise compute hota hai — [1,1,0,0] example?
Advantage = (reward − group mean) / (group std + chhota epsilon). Example: 4 completions, rewards [1, 1, 0, 0] — mean 0.5, standard deviation 0.5, to do sahi completions ka advantage +1.0 aur do galat ka −1.0. Positive advantage wale tokens reinforce hote hain, negative wale discourage. Agar group ke saare rewards identical hon (sab sahi ya sab galat), to std 0 aur advantage 0 — group kuch nahi sikhata, isliye group diversity matter karti hai.
Q29KL penalty aur clipping training me kyun zaroori hain?
KL penalty policy ko reference model (original base model) se zyada door drift karne par penalise karta hai, coefficient β se control hota hai (book ka main run β = 0.04). Bada β stable lekin slow learning deta hai; chhota β tez lekin degenerate output ka risk. Clipping policy ratio ko [1−ε, 1+ε] range me rakhta hai (ε = 0.2), taaki ek update policy ko zyada na badal sake. Dono mil kar RL training ko stable rakhte hain — bina inke policy ek hi update me bigad sakti hai.
Q30GRPO training run ke key numbers kya hain?
Setup: Qwen3-0.6B base, 50 GRPO steps, group size G = 8, 8 prompts per step, max completion length 512 tokens, learning rate 1e-6, KL β = 0.04, clipping ε = 0.2, sampling temperature 1.0. Result: MATH-500 accuracy 15.2% se 47.4% (G=8, 512 tokens), reference reasoning model 48.2% ke paas. Average response length 78.85 tokens se badh kar 586.11 tokens ho gayi. Training data: MATH training set se 2,000 problems, DGX Spark par run hua.
Q31Training ke dauraan completion length kyun badhti hai?
GRPO training me average completion length initial chhote answers se kai sau tokens tak badhti hai, aur ye growth accuracy ke saath correlate karti hai. Ye DeepSeek-R1 ko mirror karne wala key observation hai: model "lamba sochna" seekh jata hai — reasoning RL objective se emerge hoti hai, program karne se nahi. Sirf wahi traces reward paati hain jo sahi boxed answer par end hoti hain, aur lambi traces me check aur backtrack karne ki jagah hoti hai.
Q32Reward hacking kya hai aur ise kaise rokte hain?
Reward hacking matlab model literal reward ko uske intent ko defeat karte hue optimise kare — jaise format bonus collect karne ke liye correctly formatted lekin galat answer dena, ya length reward ke liye trace pad karna. Book ke remedies: outcome reward ko dominant rakho, auxiliary rewards (format bonus jaise +0.1) ko chhota rakho, aur length-aware reward me excess tokens par penalty lagao. Reward design hi decide karta hai ki model kya seekhega — isliye ye Chapter 7 ka pehla section hai.
Q33Group size ka GRPO par kya asar padta hai?
Bada group zyada reliable group mean/standard deviation deta hai (behtar baseline) aur zyada diverse traces, lekin har step me zyada generation time lagta hai. Results: G=4 → 45.6%, G=8 → 47.4%, G=16 → 48.6% (best, average length lagbhag 612 tokens). In runs me bade groups monotonically madad karte hain. Interview point: group size accuracy aur per-step cost ka tradeoff hai, aur larger group ka fayda better advantage estimate se aata hai.
Q34LoRA kya hai aur GRPO ke saath kaise use hota hai?
LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) me base model ko freeze karke sirf chhote low-rank matrices train kiye jate hain jo attention (aur feed-forward) weights me add hote hain. Rank r = 16 aur scaling α = 32 ke saath trainable parameters model ka chhota fraction reh jate hain, training tez hoti hai aur bahut kam memory use hoti hai, aur adapted model ko baad me base weights me merge kiya ja sakta hai. GRPO + LoRA 50 steps me mid-40s% MATH-500 accuracy deta hai — full fine-tuning ke 47.4% ke paas.
Q35Dr. GRPO kya hai aur ye standard GRPO se kaise alag hai?
Dr. GRPO GRPO ka variant hai jo advantage me standard-deviation normalisation hata deta hai (advantage = reward − group mean, std se divide kiye bina) aur loss me length normalisation hata deta hai — is ground par ki wo normalisations biases introduce karti hain (wo chhote completions ko favour karti hain aur noise amplify kar sakti hain). Book ke runs me ye standard GRPO ke comparable score karta hai (high-40s%), kuch lambi completions ke saath — reasonable alternative hai, clear win nahi.
Q36Distillation kya hai — teacher, student aur rejection sampling samjhao.
Distillation me bade teacher model ke outputs par chhote student model ko train kiya jata hai. Teacher (reasoning model) 2,000 problems ke liye traces generate karta hai; sirf wahi traces rakhi jati hain jo sahi boxed answer par end hoti hain (rejection sampling — quality control). Phir student ko un (prompt, trace) pairs par SFT kiya jata hai (3 epochs, lr 1e-5, batch 8, max seq 1,024 tokens), prompt tokens loss se mask karke. Student teacher ki accuracy ka bada hissa (mid-40s%) bahut kam tokens me recover kar leta hai — isliye ye book ka sabse efficient reasoning model hai accuracy per token ke hisaab se. Distillation RL se sasta aur stable hai, lekin sirf wo reasoning copy kar sakta hai jo teacher pehle se produce karta hai.
